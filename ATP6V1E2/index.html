ATP6V1E2 encodes the E2 subunit of the vacuolar H⁺-ATPase (V-ATPase), a proton pump that acidifies endosomes, lysosomes, secretory vesicles, and related intracellular compartments. Although not established as a validated Alzheimer's disease gene, ATP6V1E2 is mechanistically relevant because it intersects with endosomal–lysosomal acidification, synaptic vesicle cycling, autophagy and protein clearance, oxidative stress, inflammation, and cognitive decline.

ATP6V1E2 encodes the E2 subunit of the vacuolar H⁺-ATPase, which acidifies endosomes, lysosomes, and secretory vesicles. Functional evidence directly showed that decreased Atp6v1e2 expression reduces lysosomal acidification, confirming a direct role in compartmental pH regulation essential for protein clearance and autophagy.

Homocysteine-Induced Downregulation

In rat and N2a neuronal models, homocysteine — a recognized modifiable AD risk factor — reduced ATP6V1E2 expression alongside synaptic-vesicle-cycle genes snap25, cplx1, and slc32a1. This was accompanied by neuronal apoptosis, hippocampal and cortical pathology, synaptic structural impairment, depressive-like behavior, and LDH leakage.

GWAS Locus & Cognitive Trajectory

A genome-wide association study of longitudinal cognitive change in cognitively normal older adults identified a variant near the CRIPT/ATP6V1E2 region associated with attention-domain decline over time. This does not prove causality but places the locus near a genetic signal for late-life cognitive trajectory — a phenotype directly relevant to preclinical Alzheimer's disease.

Studies of V-ATPase subunits broadly show that complex disruption can impair Golgi structure, glycosylation, intracellular trafficking, lipid metabolism, mitochondrial respiration, and energy metabolism — all pathways implicated in neurodegeneration. ATP6V1E2 may also participate in membrane raft/redox signaling, linking it to ROS production, ERK activation, and inflammatory mediators including TNF-α.

Neuroinflammation Relevance

Status: Pathway Candidate, Not Validated Target

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — ATP6V1E2 in Alzheimer's Disease

ATP6V1E2 emerges as a mechanistically plausible but unvalidated Alzheimer's disease candidate. Its role as the V-ATPase E2 subunit places it at the intersection of lysosomal acidification, synaptic vesicle cycling, autophagy, and protein clearance — all pathways dysregulated in AD. The most compelling evidence is indirect: homocysteine-driven downregulation in neuronal models co-occurs with synaptic gene suppression and neuronal injury, and a GWAS signal near the ATP6V1E2 locus associates with longitudinal attention decline in older adults. Neither observation confirms direct causality in Alzheimer's disease. The gene's broad cellular footprint — spanning mitochondrial metabolism, redox signaling, inflammation, and multiple essential trafficking pathways — argues against systemic therapeutic modulation without substantially more targeted mechanistic evidence. The priority agenda is validation: expression studies in AD brain tissue, functional rescue experiments in homocysteine and lysosomal-dysfunction models, and rigorous follow-up of the GWAS signal. Until that evidence exists, ATP6V1E2 is best positioned as a hypothesis-generating pathway node and candidate biomarker, not a therapeutic target.
