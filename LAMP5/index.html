Overview

⚠ Safety Caveat: Existing AD-model evidence suggests that reducing LAMP5 worsens network dysfunction and functional deficits. LAMP5 also has roles in immune/endosomal biology and cancer contexts that raise additional concerns for systemic targeting.

LAMP5 is most relevant to Alzheimer's disease (AD) as a marker and functional component of specialized GABAergic inhibitory interneurons, especially cortical and entorhinal interneuron populations involved in circuit regulation. The strongest AD-specific evidence indicates that loss of LAMP5-positive interneurons contributes to neuronal network dysfunction, hyperexcitation, and hypersynchronicity in amyloid-β– and tau-driven disease models.

LAMP5 is not best supported as a conventional inhibitory target in AD. Instead, the evidence favors a protective or homeostatic role in inhibitory interneuron survival and synaptic function. Therapeutic relevance would most plausibly involve preserving LAMP5-positive interneurons, restoring inhibitory circuit stability, or modulating LAMP5-associated pathways such as synaptic transmission, lysosomal/autophagy biology, metabolic signaling, and neuroimmune crosstalk.

LAMP5 also has broader disease biology outside AD, including roles in lysosomal/autophagy pathways, immune/endosomal signaling, and cancer progression. These findings suggest biological activity and potential druggability in some contexts, but they do not directly validate LAMP5 as an AD therapeutic target.

Key Insights

Interneuron Vulnerability in AD Models

In AD-relevant mouse models, synaptic LAMP5 is lost, LAMP5 interneurons degenerate, and genetic reduction of LAMP5 worsens functional deficits and increases network hypersynchronicity in both amyloid-β– and tau-driven models, establishing LAMP5 interneurons as a direct vulnerability node.

Inhibitory Circuit Homeostasis

LAMP5 is expressed in GABAergic interneuron subclasses and inhibitory synaptic terminals, where it influences short-term synaptic plasticity and evoked GABAergic transmission — directly aligned with AD mechanisms involving excitation–inhibition imbalance, subclinical epileptiform activity, and cognitive impairment.

Entorhinal & Specialized Cortical Subtypes

LAMP5-positive populations include neurogliaform cells, rosehip cells, VIP/LAMP5, LAMP5/LHX6, and LAMP5+RELN+ interneurons. Entorhinal LAMP5+RELN+ interneurons are especially significant because the entorhinal cortex is affected early in AD and is critical for episodic memory and hippocampal input.

GLP1R–Insulin & Metabolic Signaling

LAMP5-positive neurogliaform and rosehip interneurons co-express GLP1R and insulin transcripts, suggesting an intracortical GLP1R–insulin signaling loop. This is indirectly relevant to AD given the established roles of impaired glucose metabolism, insulin signaling, and GLP1R-linked neuroprotection.

Systemic Inhibition Carries Safety Risk

Oncology findings show LAMP5 can be therapeutically tractable via knockdown or antibody–drug conjugate strategies, but these approaches are not transferable to AD because LAMP5-positive neurons are normal, potentially protective brain cells. LAMP5 roles in immune/endosomal biology further complicate systemic targeting.

AD Genetic-Risk Mapping & Downstream Role

AD genetic-risk mapping in primate cortex points most strongly to microglial cis-regulatory elements, not LAMP5-expressing neurons, suggesting LAMP5 does not emerge as a primary genetic-risk hub. LAMP5 interneurons may nonetheless be affected downstream of inflammatory or degenerative processes, and non-AD studies document selective LAMP5 neuron–microglia crosstalk.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — LAMP5 in Alzheimer's Disease

LAMP5 is a biologically meaningful marker of vulnerable inhibitory interneurons in AD, not a conventional drug target for inhibition. The weight of evidence positions LAMP5-positive GABAergic interneurons as protective components of inhibitory circuit homeostasis whose loss — in both amyloid-β– and tau-driven disease models — drives network hyperexcitability and worsens cognitive outcomes. Therapeutic strategies aimed at preserving or restoring LAMP5-positive interneuron function align with this evidence and represent the most scientifically defensible direction. By contrast, direct LAMP5 suppression or systemic inhibition carries real risk of worsening AD circuit dysfunction and of off-target consequences in immune and oncological contexts. The strongest near-term priority is rigorous characterization of LAMP5 interneuron loss across human AD brain regions and disease stages, followed by mechanistic dissection of LAMP5-associated pathways — including GLP1R–insulin signaling, lysosomal/autophagy biology, and microglial crosstalk — as potential druggable nodes upstream or downstream of interneuron vulnerability.
