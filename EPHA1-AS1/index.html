Overview

EPHA1-AS1 is implicated in Alzheimer's disease (AD) primarily as a genetically regulated, immune-cell-associated long noncoding RNA rather than through a clearly defined neuronal mechanism. The strongest evidence links inherited variants that affect EPHA1-AS1 expression to AD susceptibility.

Across the cited studies, higher EPHA1-AS1 expression was associated with reduced AD risk, suggesting a potentially protective role. This evidence comes from population genetics, Mendelian randomization, colocalization, single-cell eQTL analyses, and immune-cell expression studies. Current data support EPHA1-AS1 as a candidate immune-regulatory factor in AD risk, but not yet as a proven driver of amyloid, tau, synaptic degeneration, neuroinflammation, or cognitive decline.

Overall Assessment

Verdict — EPHA1-AS1 in Alzheimer's Disease

EPHA1-AS1 is a genetically credentialed, immune-cell-associated lncRNA with a consistent protective association signal in Alzheimer's disease genetics. The evidence — drawn from Mendelian randomization, single-cell eQTL analyses, colocalization, and large-scale GWAS — converges on the conclusion that inherited variants regulating EPHA1-AS1 expression in peripheral immune cells may causally reduce AD susceptibility. However, the field has not yet characterized the specific pathways, target genes, or immune cell states through which this protection operates, and no direct neuronal or amyloid/tau-related mechanism has been demonstrated.
