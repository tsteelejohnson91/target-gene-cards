Key Insights

Core NMJ Scaffold Function

RAPSN's strongest established role is clustering and anchoring nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the postsynaptic muscle endplate via the Agrin–LRP4–MuSK signalling axis, supporting synaptic differentiation and stable neuromuscular transmission.

Congenital Myasthenic Syndrome

Pathogenic RAPSN variants — most notably the recurrent N88K/p.Asn88Lys substitution — cause congenital myasthenic syndrome with fatigable weakness, ptosis, bulbar symptoms, hypotonia, respiratory crises, apnea, and feeding problems from early life.

Proteostasis & Aggrephagy Link

In a myasthenia gravis model, autoantibody-triggered nAChR internalization caused RAPSN aggregation that impaired receptor trafficking. HSPA/HSP70-BAG3-mediated aggrephagy cleared RAPSN aggregates via lysosomes — a conceptual overlap with Alzheimer's proteostasis dysfunction, but without CNS or AD brain evidence.

Lysosome Positioning & Dynamics

Rapsyn deficiency caused lysosome scattering, increased lysosomal dynamics and exocytosis, and greater membrane damage after challenge — raising a hypothetical relevance to neurodegeneration, though this was not tested in any Alzheimer's model.

Established Therapeutic Actionability (CMS Only)

RAPSN-associated CMS responds to pyridostigmine, salbutamol, 3,4-diaminopyridine, ephedrine, and calcitriol. These neuromuscular transmission-enhancing strategies have no demonstrated Alzheimer's disease application.

Indirect & Weak AD Associations

Genetic and omics links of RAPSN to Alzheimer's-adjacent contexts — obesity, metabolic syndrome, BMI, Parkinson's disease, lacunar stroke, and cancer methylation — are weak or indirect and do not establish RAPSN as causal in Alzheimer's disease.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — RAPSN in Alzheimer's Disease

RAPSN is not an established Alzheimer's disease target. Its biology is deeply rooted in neuromuscular junction physiology, where it functions as a postsynaptic scaffold for nicotinic acetylcholine receptor clustering under the control of the Agrin–LRP4–MuSK pathway. Pathogenic variants cause congenital myasthenic syndrome, with well-characterized clinical presentations and genuine therapeutic options. The conceptual bridges to Alzheimer's disease — cholinergic signalling overlap, lysosomal positioning, and HSPA/HSP70-BAG3-mediated aggrephagy — are biologically plausible but entirely unvalidated in the Alzheimer's brain. Weak or indirect genomic associations with metabolic and neurodegenerative contexts do not elevate RAPSN to causal status in Alzheimer's pathology. Substantial AD-specific experimentation would be required before RAPSN could responsibly enter any Alzheimer's target development pipeline. Safety considerations around neuromuscular junction integrity further constrain the therapeutic opportunity.
