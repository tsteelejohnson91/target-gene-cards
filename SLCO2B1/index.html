SLCO2B1 encodes OATP2B1, a broadly expressed organic anion uptake transporter involved in cellular and tissue exposure to endogenous steroid conjugates, prostaglandin-related lipid mediators, heme-related compounds, bile-acid-related molecules, flavonoid conjugates, environmental toxicants, and many drugs.

SLCO2B1 is not established as a direct Alzheimer's disease target: most cited studies do not examine amyloid-β, tau, synaptic loss, cognition, or Alzheimer's patient outcomes. The strongest Alzheimer's-relevant evidence is mechanistic and indirect — involving CNS-adjacent barrier transport, CSF clearance of inflammatory mediators, microglial heme/iron handling, neurosteroid transport, inflammatory-cell pharmacology, and gut–liver–brain pharmacokinetics.

The overall evidence positions SLCO2B1 as a candidate modifier of Alzheimer's-relevant exposure and inflammatory/metabolic pathways, rather than a validated disease-modifying therapeutic target.

Mechanistic Overview

Key Insights

No Direct Alzheimer's Evidence

The supplied literature does not demonstrate that SLCO2B1 directly regulates amyloid-β clearance or production, tau pathology, synaptic degeneration, neuronal death, cognition, or clinical Alzheimer's progression. Its relevance remains mechanistically plausible but indirect.

CSF Lipid Mediator Clearance

A rat arachnoid mater study found Slco2b1/Oatp2b1 expression in leptomeningeal/arachnoid cells contributing to the blood–CSF interface. Prostaglandin D2 clearance from CSF exceeded bulk-flow marker clearance and was inhibited by transporter inhibitors, implicating a network including Oat1, Oat3, Oatp2a1, and Oatp2b1 — relevant to neuroinflammation and sleep–wake biology.

Microglial Heme / Iron Handling

SLCO2B1 was identified as a membrane carrier capable of importing heme analogs, increasing intracellular iron, and supporting proliferation during iron restriction, with predominant brain expression in microglia. Slco2b1 knockout microglia showed impaired heme analog import — linking this transporter to microglial iron loading, oxidative stress, and chronic inflammatory activation relevant to neurodegeneration.

Steroid Sulfate & Neurosteroid Transport

Repeated substrates include estrone-3-sulfate, DHEA-S, pregnenolone sulfate, and androgen sulfates. These neurosteroids can influence synaptic function, mitochondrial activity, neuroinflammation, sex differences, and cognitive resilience — suggesting SLCO2B1 could modify Alzheimer's-relevant endocrine tone, although direct brain entry of neurosteroids via OATP2B1 was not confirmed in an in vitro BBB model.

Major Pharmacokinetic Modifier

OATP2B1 governs uptake/disposition of statins, fexofenadine, aliskiren, montelukast, erlotinib, abiraterone, and many other drugs. Grapefruit, apple, and orange juices, black tea, ginkgo, soy isoflavones, food dyes, antiviral agents, and TKIs all inhibit OATP2B1 — with some interactions producing large exposure changes highly relevant to polypharmacy in older Alzheimer's patients.

Systemic Modulation Risk

OATP2B1 is broadly expressed in intestine, liver, kidney, placenta, skeletal muscle, heart, platelets, and immune cells. Direct broad inhibition or activation could produce unintended effects on drug absorption, steroid and bile-acid handling, statin exposure, immune-cell activation, skeletal muscle toxicity, and hepatic clearance — requiring highly selective targeting strategies.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — SLCO2B1 / OATP2B1 in Alzheimer's Disease

The current evidence positions SLCO2B1 as a biologically plausible but unvalidated candidate modifier of Alzheimer's disease biology, rather than a disease-modifying therapeutic target. Its most defensible near-term roles are as a pharmacokinetic and pharmacogenomic determinant of drug exposure at CNS barriers, a potential regulator of CSF inflammatory lipid mediator clearance through the arachnoid/leptomeningeal transporter network, and a microglial heme and iron import carrier that could plausibly influence oxidative stress and neuroinflammatory tone. Steroid-sulfate and neurosteroid transport are also mechanistically relevant, though direct brain-entry roles remain contested. Critically, no Alzheimer's-specific functional data yet confirm that SLCO2B1 modulation changes amyloid pathology, tau accumulation, synaptic integrity, or cognitive outcomes. Given its wide peripheral expression footprint, systemic modulation carries substantial off-target risk. The most productive near-term strategy is to use SLCO2B1 as a precision-pharmacology and delivery node — optimizing drug exposure, predicting interactions, stratifying patients by genotype, and guiding transporter-aware compound design — while systematically generating the Alzheimer's-specific expression and functional data needed to determine whether direct therapeutic targeting is warranted and in which direction.
