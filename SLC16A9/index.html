Key Insights

No Direct AD Evidence

No cited study shows that SLC16A9 affects AD incidence, progression, cognition, amyloid or tau pathology, microglial activation, synaptic loss, or neurodegeneration. AD relevance is entirely indirect and hypothesis-generating.

Carnitine Biology & Fatty Acid Oxidation

SLC16A9 is repeatedly linked to carnitine biology and FAO. In cancer models, increased SLC16A9 raises carnitine uptake, promotes FAO dependence, and reduces ROS and DNA damage — pathways overlapping with AD-relevant mitochondrial dysfunction, though no brain or AD model data exist.

Creatine Transport & Energy Buffering

MCT9 can mediate creatine transport or efflux in experimental systems. Creatine/phosphocreatine metabolism is critical for ATP buffering in energy-demanding cells, suggesting a plausible but untested link to AD energy failure and oligodendrocyte–neuron metabolic support.

Urate Transport & NLRP3 Inflammasome

Multiple GWAS and functional studies associate SLC16A9 with serum uric acid and gout. In cardiomyopathy models, SLC16A9 silencing reduced intracellular urate, NLRP3 inflammasome activation, hypertrophy, and fibrosis — mechanistically interesting for AD neuroinflammation, but evidence is from non-brain systems only.

Vascular & Endothelial Links

SLC16A9 is expressed among urate transporters in endothelial cells; urate exposure can reduce nitric oxide signaling in HUVECs, and TNFα stimulation can increase SLC16A9 expression with NF-κB changes. These may conceptually relate to vascular contributions to cognitive impairment, but are peripheral endothelial findings.

Therapeutic Direction Unclear

Inhibiting SLC16A9 might reduce uric-acid-driven NLRP3 signaling in some contexts, but could also disrupt carnitine- or creatine-related energy metabolism. Context-dependent effects vary by cell type, substrate availability, pH, sodium dependence, species, and genetic variant — making therapeutic targeting premature.
