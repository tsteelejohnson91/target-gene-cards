GUCY1B1 encodes the β subunit of soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC), the nitric oxide (NO)-responsive enzyme that generates cyclic GMP (cGMP) and activates downstream cGMP/PKG signaling. In the Alzheimer's disease literature surveyed, GUCY1B1 is most directly implicated by a 2026 multi-cohort post-mortem brain RNA-seq regulatory-network study (ROSMAP, MAYO, and MSBB cohorts), which identified GUCY1B1 as a conserved deregulated regulatory factor across the parahippocampal gyrus, inferior frontal gyrus, and posterior cingulate cortex — regions selectively vulnerable in AD.

The broader evidence base connects GUCY1B1/GUCY1B3-containing sGC signaling to vascular tone, hypertension, endothelial function, neurovascular coupling, oxidative stress, ischemic injury, inflammation, apoptosis, synaptic regulation, mitochondrial function, and neural tissue protection — mechanisms that overlap substantially with vascular and stress-related contributors to AD pathophysiology.

GUCY1B1 is therefore best viewed as a plausible AD-relevant neurovascular and stress-response candidate, with therapeutic potential principally through pathway-level restoration of NO–sGC–cGMP–PKG signaling rather than direct targeting of the gene itself.

Key Insights

AD Network Dysregulation

A 2026 multi-cohort post-mortem RNA-seq study (ROSMAP, MAYO, MSBB) identified GUCY1B1 as a conserved deregulated regulatory factor in the parahippocampal gyrus, inferior frontal gyrus, and posterior cingulate cortex — regions selectively vulnerable in Alzheimer's disease — suggesting involvement in coordinated transcriptional dysregulation across vulnerable cortical and limbic networks.

NO–sGC–cGMP Core Mechanism

The strongest mechanistic link to AD runs through the NO–sGC–cGMP–PKG axis, which governs vascular relaxation, cerebral perfusion, endothelial function, neurovascular coupling, and cellular resilience. Reduced GUCY1A1/GUCY1B1 or GUCY1B3 expression is associated with impaired vasorelaxation, fibrosis, vascular stiffness, and elevated hypertension risk — all conditions that increase risk for cognitive decline and vascular contributions to dementia.

Pharmacologic Restoration Feasible

In neural retina models, oxidative stress impaired sGC function, while the sGC activator runcaciguat restored sGC activation and improved neuroretinal function, visual function, and tissue morphology. sGC activators/stimulators, PDE5/cGMP modulators, and NO-pathway agents can modulate cGMP signaling or sGC subunit expression, supporting pathway-level therapeutic feasibility.

Neural Injury & Cytoprotection

Gucy1b1 is linked to radiation-mitigation-associated brain-enriched proteins and AD-relevant processes including hippocampal integrity, dendritic spine preservation, mitochondrial membrane potential, calcium homeostasis, neuroinflammation, and synaptic plasticity. Reduced Gucy1b1 may worsen cell death under inflammatory injury, supporting a cytoprotective role for preserved sGC signaling.

NOTCH Transcriptional Connection

NOTCH-dependent regulation of GUCY1B3 expression connects sGC biology to transcriptional pathways relevant to vascular biology and amyloid-related γ-secretase/NOTCH signaling. However, no direct AD causal mechanism is demonstrated, and this link remains contextual rather than mechanistically validated.

Critical Evidence Gaps

No supplied study demonstrates that GUCY1B1 modulation changes amyloid-β accumulation, tau phosphorylation or spread, microglial activation in AD, synaptic loss in AD models, or cognitive outcomes in AD animals or patients. The evidence base is predominantly computational, vascular, or derived from non-AD disease models, leaving a substantial translational gap.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — GUCY1B1 in Alzheimer's Disease

GUCY1B1 occupies a biologically plausible but mechanistically unproven position in the AD landscape. Its identification as a conserved deregulated regulatory factor across multiple AD-vulnerable brain regions in a rigorous multi-cohort RNA-seq network study provides a meaningful computational anchor. The extensive literature on sGC signaling in vascular biology — covering endothelial function, neurovascular coupling, oxidative stress, mitochondrial integrity, synaptic plasticity, and cytoprotection — furnishes a rich mechanistic framework for how reduced GUCY1B1/sGC activity could amplify AD-related neurodegeneration through the neurovascular and stress-response axis. The demonstrated feasibility of pharmacologic restoration via sGC activators such as runcaciguat adds translational tractability. However, the absence of direct experimental evidence linking GUCY1B1 to amyloid, tau, or cognitive outcomes in AD-specific models means the gene cannot yet be classified as a validated AD driver or therapeutic target. Investment in cell-type-resolved expression studies, pathway engagement biomarkers, and sGC-modulator trials in vascular-AD model systems represents the most scientifically justified next step toward establishing or refuting this candidate's disease relevance.
