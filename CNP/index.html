Key Insights

Oligodendrocyte / Myelin CNP — Strongest Signal

CNP (2′,3′-CNPase) is linked to myelinic nanochannel maintenance, oligodendrocyte maturation, and axonal metabolic support. In an ALS mouse model, CNP depletion caused excessive myelin compaction, channel collapse, accelerated disease, and reduced survival — conceptually relevant to AD white-matter injury, though untested in AD directly.

Co-variation with Myelin Gene Network

CNP co-varies with MBP, PLP1, MOBP, and related myelin markers. Reduced CNP with these markers was interpreted as impaired oligodendrocyte maturation in a prenatal-stress model; increased expression appeared with ensheathment or remodeling in inflammatory or drug-exposure contexts. CNP may serve as a marker of myelin integrity rather than a causal AD driver.

C-type Natriuretic Peptide — Neurovascular Hypothesis

CNP signals through NPR-B/NPR2/GC-B (raising cGMP) and NPR-C-mediated protective pathways. In non-AD systems, this is associated with vascular regulation, endothelial integrity, anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects, anti-apoptotic signaling, oxidative-stress reduction, and calcium/cytoskeletal modulation — all overlapping with AD-relevant biology.

Neuronal cGMP–cGKI Signaling

CNP–NPR2–cGMP–cGKI signaling can regulate growth-cone stiffness, actin organization, axonal branching, and calcium transients in embryonic dorsal-root ganglion neurons. These mechanisms are broadly relevant to synaptic and neuritic vulnerability in neurodegeneration, though evidence comes from non-AD, non-cortical models.

FTLD CSF Proteomics — Indirect Biomarker Context

One FTLD CSF proteomics study included CNP in replicated diagnostic protein panels for FTLD and FTLD-TDP. This does not establish CNP as an AD biomarker, but it raises the possibility that CNP-related biology intersects with broader neurodegenerative proteomic signatures.
