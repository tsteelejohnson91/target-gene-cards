ATP6V0D1 & Alzheimer's Disease

Target Gene Card — AI-Assisted Evidence Summary

Overview

Therapeutic Caution: ATP6V0D1 participates in essential V-ATPase functions across tissues. Broad activation or inhibition risks off-target effects; cell-type-specific or normalization-based strategies are strongly preferred over global modulation.

ATP6V0D1 is a V0-sector subunit of the vacuolar H⁺-ATPase (V-ATPase) linked to Alzheimer's disease through two direct lines of evidence: a 2025 Aging Cell study in 5xFAD mice implicating a microglial Tmem9–ATP6V0D1 pathway in complement activation, synapse engulfment, synaptic loss, and cognitive/emotional impairment; and a 2020 Brain human AD study identifying ATP6V0D1 as a protein that directly interacts with phosphorylated tau within neurofibrillary tangles.

Across broader evidence, ATP6V0D1 is repeatedly associated with lysosomal and endosomal acidification, autophagy and phagosome maturation, mitochondrial–lysosomal crosstalk, immune-cell activation, complement biology, stress responses, senescence, and cell-death/survival pathways — all of which overlap strongly with central Alzheimer's pathogenic themes including tau aggregation, impaired proteostasis, defective autophagic-lysosomal clearance, microglial complement-mediated synapse loss, and aging-related cellular decline.

ATP6V0D1 therefore emerges as a mechanistically plausible Alzheimer's disease target, especially for strategies aimed at restoring lysosomal function, modulating microglial complement activity, preserving synapses, and improving clearance of pathological proteins. Alzheimer's-specific therapeutic validation, however, remains limited.

Key Insights

Microglial Tmem9–ATP6V0D1 Axis in 5xFAD Mice

In 5xFAD mice, a microglial Tmem9–ATP6V0D1 pathway drives complement activation and synaptic engulfment. Physical exercise suppressed this axis, reducing synapse loss and improving cognitive and emotional outcomes — providing the most direct AD-model evidence for ATP6V0D1 as a functional target.

Human AD Brain: Phospho-tau Interactome

Human AD brain proteomics (2020, Brain) identified ATP6V0D1 as a direct binding partner of phosphorylated tau within neurofibrillary tangles. The interactome was enriched for ubiquitination and phagosome maturation pathways, directly linking ATP6V0D1 to tau pathology and disrupted proteostatic networks.

Lysosomal Acidification & V-ATPase Core Function

ATP6V0D1's primary role as a V-ATPase subunit makes it central to lysosomal/endosomal pH homeostasis. Schisandrol A allosterically targeted ATP6V0D1 at cysteine 335, enhanced lysosomal acidification, promoted BIM degradation, preserved mitochondrial homeostasis, and protected neurons — validating the subunit as pharmacologically tractable.

Parkinson's & Neuronal Resilience Models

In Parkinson's-related stress models, reduced ATP6V0D1-associated lysosomal machinery was linked to mitochondrial dysfunction, defective autophagic flux, and increased neuronal vulnerability — reinforcing the hypothesis that intact ATP6V0D1 function supports neuronal resilience across neurodegenerative contexts.

Aging, Senescence & TFEB/TFE3 Programs

ATP6V0D1 is upregulated in small extracellular vesicles from senescent, progeria, and aged cells, and sits within TFEB/TFE3-coordinated lysosomal stress-response gene networks governing antioxidant defense and ER-stress resistance — suggesting potential relevance as an aging-linked biomarker, though not yet established in AD specifically.

Therapeutic Direction: Enhancement, Not Inhibition

In AD, the plausible therapeutic strategy is normalization or enhancement of lysosomal acidification and autophagic flux rather than broad V-ATPase inhibition, because AD involves defective clearance of aggregated proteins and damaged organelles. Its essential roles across tissues demand cell-type-specific or pathway-specific approaches to avoid systemic toxicity.

  5. Use functional, not just expression, readouts: Measure lysosomal pH, cathepsin activity, autophagic flux, tau clearance, amyloid-related processing, mitochondrial integrity, synaptic density, complement deposition, and microglial phagocytosis as primary endpoints.
  6. Avoid nonspecific global V-ATPase modulation: Because ATP6V0D1 participates in essential functions across all tissues, therapeutic development must aim for cell-type-specific, pathway-specific, or normalization-based approaches rather than broad activation or inhibition.
  7. Frame ATP6V0D1 as credible but unvalidated: Its strongest rationale connects tau pathology, lysosomal/autophagic dysfunction, microglial complement-mediated synapse loss, and neuronal stress resilience — but Alzheimer's-specific therapeutic validation has not yet been established.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — ATP6V0D1 is a mechanistically compelling but not yet therapeutically validated Alzheimer's disease target. Its dual direct evidence — a microglial Tmem9–ATP6V0D1 complement/synapse axis in 5xFAD mice and direct binding to phosphorylated tau in human AD neurofibrillary tangles — anchors it firmly within AD-relevant biology. Its broader roles in lysosomal acidification, autophagy, phagosome maturation, mitochondrial homeostasis, and TFEB/TFE3-regulated stress responses align tightly with the core pathogenic processes of AD. Pharmacological tractability is supported by allosteric modulation with schisandrol A in neuronal injury models. The most credible therapeutic hypothesis is selective enhancement of lysosomal acidification and autophagic flux in neurons and microglia, rather than broad V-ATPase suppression. The immediate priority is systematic functional validation across human AD tissue and disease-relevant cell types, with the Tmem9–ATP6V0D1 microglial axis and phospho-tau lysosomal interaction representing the most actionable entry points.
