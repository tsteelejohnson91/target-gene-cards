DENND1B encodes a DENN-domain protein and Rab35 guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF)-related regulator. Through Rab35- and AP-2-associated mechanisms it is implicated in endocytosis, endosomal recycling, receptor internalization, phagocytosis, autophagy, ciliary biology, cytoskeletal organization, and receptor trafficking — all processes broadly relevant to Alzheimer's disease biology, though no supplied record demonstrates a direct DENND1B–AD connection.

The gene appears prominently in immune-mediated disease genetics (childhood asthma, allergy, Crohn's disease, IBD, primary biliary cirrhosis, narcolepsy type 1, chronic pulmonary aspergillosis) and macrophage inflammatory-signaling studies. Akt/insulin-dependent phosphorylation of DENND1/connecdenn proteins links the pathway to metabolic signaling. One dementia-adjacent study associates DENND1B with cortical structural abnormalities in vascular cognitive impairment — the closest supplied evidence to any cognitive-decline endpoint.

In summary, DENND1B is a biologically interesting hypothesis-generating candidate sitting at the intersection of endosomal trafficking, immune/inflammatory regulation, metabolic signaling, and ciliary biology — domains that collectively matter in AD — but it requires dedicated AD-focused experimental validation before being considered a mechanistic or therapeutic priority.
