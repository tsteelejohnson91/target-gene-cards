SEPTIN10 belongs to the septin family of cytoskeletal GTPases, proteins broadly involved in cytoskeletal remodeling, vesicle trafficking, apoptosis, infection, neurodegeneration, and cancer. Its known functions—microtubule regulation, actin–microtubule crosstalk, membrane and lipid interactions, cell fate regulation, and inflammatory-response signatures—overlap with biological processes disrupted in neurodegeneration, but the available evidence does not demonstrate that SEPTIN10 modifies Alzheimer's disease processes in any direct way.

The strongest established roles of SEPTIN10 lie in cancer cell cytoskeletal coordination (regulation of actin stress fibers via CAPZA2, inhibition of microtubule polymerization via MAP4), paclitaxel sensitivity in drug-response models, phosphoinositide binding in a parasite ortholog, and control of retinal progenitor cell fate via miR-124-3p signaling. Key Insights

No Direct AD Evidence

Cytoskeletal Coordination

SEPTIN10 regulates actin stress fiber formation (via CAPZA2), intracellular tension, and microtubule polymerization (via MAP4 inhibition) in cancer cell contexts. Neuronal health depends on intact actin and microtubule systems, providing a plausible but unproven rationale for AD research.

Neural Cell Fate Biology

miR-124-3p downregulates SEPTIN10, reducing retinal progenitor proliferation and promoting neuronal/ganglion-cell differentiation. SEPTIN10 overexpression rescues proliferation. This indicates a role in neural cell fate, though not in mature neuronal degeneration typical of AD.

Drug-Response & Microtubule Stability

SEPTIN10 overexpression increased paclitaxel sensitivity; paclitaxel-resistant tumors showed reduced SEPTIN10 expression. This supports a functional role in microtubule stability relevant to cytoskeletal drug biology, but does not directly translate to AD therapy.

Membrane & Lipid Biology

A Schistosoma mansoni SEPTIN10 ortholog binds phosphatidylinositol phosphates through a conserved polybasic C-terminal region and key lysine residue. By analogy, this may relate to neuronal membrane organization and vesicle trafficking, but the data are parasite-derived and non-AD-specific.

Immune & Exosomal Signals

SEPTIN10 is upregulated during LPS-induced dendritic cell maturation, and PM2.5-induced exosomal circCLIP1 raises SEPTIN10 expression in airway cells. Septin10 knockdown did not significantly affect phagocytosis in an invertebrate model. These findings do not establish SEPTIN10 as a microglial or AD immune target.
