PADI2 / PAD2 & Alzheimer's Disease

Target Enabling Package — AI-Assisted Evidence Summary

Overview

Safety Note: Broad chronic PAD2 inhibition carries risks including impaired host defence, disrupted oligodendrocyte differentiation and myelination, altered antibody responses, vascular effects, and loss of potentially compensatory repair pathways. PAD2-selective, brain-penetrant inhibitors are preferred over pan-PAD blockade.

PADI2/PAD2 is a calcium-dependent peptidylarginine deiminase that converts protein arginine residues to citrulline, altering protein charge, structure, stability, interactions, antigenicity, inflammatory signalling, and chromatin regulation. In Alzheimer's disease (AD), the strongest evidence supports an astrocyte-centred mechanism in which PAD2 is upregulated in plaque-associated or hippocampal glial cells and drives pathological citrullination of intermediate filament proteins — especially vimentin and GFAP.

The most compelling AD-specific model proceeds as follows: an amyloid-β plaque environment or amyloid-related injury triggers PAD2 upregulation in astrocytes (and in some models microglia), leading to citrullination of astrocytic vimentin at residues 175/184 (Cit-Vim175/184) and/or GFAP. Released or accumulated citrullinated glial proteins then drive TLR4-dependent microglial inflammatory activation, which reduces microglial Aβ phagocytosis and clearance, thereby increasing amyloid pathology and cognitive dysfunction.

Human AD hippocampus and cortex show increased PAD2/PADI2 and abnormal accumulation of citrullinated proteins including citrullinated GFAP and vimentin. In a directly relevant AD study, PAD2 was selectively upregulated in plaque-associated astrocytes in human AD cortex and multiple APP-based AD mouse models. Both genetic astrocyte-specific Padi2 deletion and pharmacological PAD2 inhibition improved AD-like outcomes in mice, supporting PAD2 as a modifiable disease driver rather than merely a marker.

Key Insights

Plaque-Associated Astrocyte Pathology

PAD2 upregulation is localised to astrocytes surrounding amyloid-β plaques in human AD cortex and multiple APP-based mouse models — not a diffuse whole-brain increase — indicating a spatially restricted pathological niche.

Glial Inflammatory Signalling Axis

PAD2 citrullinates astrocytic vimentin at residues 175/184; the resulting Cit-Vim is released from astrocytes and activates microglia through TLR4-dependent signalling, creating a glial-to-glial inflammatory relay.

Worsened Amyloid Pathology

Released citrullinated vimentin promotes a pro-inflammatory microglial phenotype while impairing Aβ phagocytosis and clearance, directly contributing to higher amyloid burden and cognitive impairment in AD models.

Genetic Causality (5×FAD)

Astrocyte-specific deletion of Padi2 in 5×FAD mice rescued learning and memory, reduced Aβ burden, restrained pro-inflammatory microglial activation, and restored microglial phagocytosis — demonstrating causal involvement.

Pharmacological Druggability

PAD2 inhibition reproduced the protective effects of genetic deletion, including normalisation of glial signatures and cognitive improvement, supporting PAD2 as a pharmacologically tractable target in AD models.

Physiological Roles & Safety

PAD2 has beneficial physiological functions in CNS myelination, oligodendrocyte differentiation, transcriptional regulation, host defence, angiogenesis, and tissue repair. Broad chronic inhibition risks disrupting these functions; isoform-selective brain-penetrant inhibitors are required.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Prioritise PAD2 as an AD target in astrocyte–microglia inflammatory niches, with a focus on the peri-plaque microenvironment where PAD2 upregulation is most strongly evidenced.
  2. Validate the core pathway: PAD2 → Cit-Vim175/184 and/or citrullinated GFAP → TLR4-linked microglial activation → impaired Aβ clearance → cognitive decline should be the primary mechanistic hypothesis for translational experiments.
  3. Use citrullinated vimentin and citrullinated GFAP as pharmacodynamic biomarkers for PAD2 target engagement in AD mouse models and human tissue studies.
  4. Develop brain-penetrant, PAD2-selective inhibitors rather than pan-PAD agents to minimise off-target effects on PAD4 (NET/histone-associated) and to clarify PAD2-specific mechanisms in the CNS.
  5. Determine cell-type specificity across disease stages: assess whether pathogenic PAD2 activity is primarily astrocytic, microglial, oligodendroglial, neuronal, vascular, or peripheral immune-derived at early versus late AD.
  6. Broaden efficacy endpoints beyond amyloid burden to include cognition, microglial phagocytosis, inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α), TLR4 signalling, astrocyte reactivity, synaptic integrity, myelin/white-matter integrity, and citrullinated protein load.
  7. Prospectively monitor safety risks from systemic PAD2 inhibition, including impaired host defence, altered antibody responses, disrupted myelination, vascular effects, and loss of compensatory aging or repair pathways.
  8. Treat PADI2 as a promising but validation-dependent target: the strongest mouse data support causality and druggability, but broader context-dependent biology demands careful translational scrutiny before clinical advancement.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — PAD2/PADI2 in Alzheimer's Disease

PAD2/PADI2 occupies a compelling mechanistic position at the intersection of astrocyte pathology, glial citrullination, and neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease. The convergence of human AD tissue data, multiple APP-based mouse models, astrocyte-specific genetic deletion, and pharmacological rescue experiments collectively support PAD2 as a modifiable causal contributor rather than an incidental marker. The central pathway — plaque-driven PAD2 upregulation → Cit-Vim175/184 and Cit-GFAP → TLR4-dependent microglial activation → impaired Aβ clearance and cognitive decline — is mechanistically coherent and supported by independent experimental approaches. Druggability is demonstrated in mice. Key outstanding requirements include isoform-selective, brain-penetrant PAD2 inhibitor development; cell-type-resolved validation in human AD tissue; careful monitoring of physiological PAD2 functions in myelination and host defence; and biomarker qualification of citrullinated glial proteins for target engagement. PAD2 merits prioritisation as an astrocyte-centred, inflammation-linked AD therapeutic target, contingent on further translational validation.
