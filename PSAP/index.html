Overview

⚠ Disambiguation Caution

Two biologically distinct proteins share the acronym PSAP: prosaposin, a lysosomal/secreted glycoprotein (the primary focus here), and presenilin-associated protein, a mitochondrial pro-apoptotic protein linked to DR6/Bax-mediated cell death. These must not be conflated in experimental or therapeutic design.

PSAP in the Alzheimer's-relevant literature primarily refers to prosaposin, a lysosomal and secreted glycoprotein that is proteolytically cleaved into saposins A–D. These saposins regulate sphingolipid degradation, lysosomal competence, neurotrophic signaling, glial communication, and cellular stress responses. The strongest direct Alzheimer's-related evidence places PSAP in altered CSF/brain biomarker profiles, amyloid-β plaque association, tau/tangle-linked lysosomal changes, astrocyte regulation of extracellular Aβ and IL-6, entorhinal cortex cell–cell communication and A1-like astrogliosis, and preclinical therapeutic effects of the PSAP-derived peptide PS18 and PGRN–PSAP-modulating compounds such as AZP2006/ezeprogind.

Across broader neurodegeneration and injury studies, PSAP repeatedly converges on mechanisms central to AD: lysosomal/autophagic dysfunction, ceramide/sphingolipid imbalance, glucocerebrosidase/saposin C activity, progranulin trafficking, oxidative stress, mitochondrial apoptosis, neuroinflammation, microglial/astrocytic responses, efferocytosis, myelin preservation, and PSAP–GPR37/GPR37L1 receptor signaling.

Overall, PSAP is best viewed as a promising but context-dependent therapeutic node in AD — not yet a validated stand-alone target, but situated at the intersection of lipid metabolism, lysosomal clearance, glial inflammation, neuronal survival, amyloid/tau-associated pathology, and circuit maintenance.

Key Insights

Core Lysosomal Lipid Regulator

PSAP/prosaposin is the precursor of saposins A–D, enabling lysosomal sphingolipid degradation. This directly links PSAP to AD-relevant lysosomal failure, ceramide accumulation, autophagic defects, and impaired clearance of toxic proteins and cellular debris.

Neuroinflammation & Glial Dysfunction

In 5×FAD mice, elevated CSF xanthine potentiated microglial IL-6 release, with PSAP identified as a candidate xanthine-engaged target. Astrocyte PSAP knockdown altered extracellular Aβ and reduced IL-6; entorhinal cortex studies link PSAP signaling to A1-like reactive astrogliosis.

Amyloid & Tau Pathology Links

Human AD brain studies report prosaposin enrichment in amyloid-β plaques, correlation with Aβ burden, and strong colocalization with progranulin. Reduced neuronal PGRN/PSAP signals are connected to neurofibrillary tangle development; pathological colocalization with amyloid aggregates has also been described.

PGRN–PSAP Therapeutic Axis

PSAP and progranulin interact to regulate each other's lysosomal trafficking. AZP2006/ezeprogind stabilises this complex, improving lysosomal function, reducing tau hyperphosphorylation, supporting neuronal survival, reducing neuroinflammation, and promoting synaptogenesis in tauopathy/AD models.

PS18 Peptide Preclinical Efficacy

PS18 protected against Aβ1–42-induced memory impairment in mice: it activated PI3K/Akt, inhibited GSK-3α/β, increased ADAM10, reduced BACE1/PS1/PS2/AβPP, reduced mitochondrial damage, upregulated Bcl-2, downregulated BAX, and inhibited caspase-3, demonstrating direct anti-amyloid and anti-apoptotic activity.

Dual-Entity Disambiguation Risk

A separate protein — presenilin-associated protein — also abbreviated PSAP interacts with DR6 and Bax, mediates Bax translocation and cytochrome c release, and is required for DR6-induced apoptosis linked to Aβ neurotoxicity. Conflating this with prosaposin would fundamentally misdirect therapeutic strategy.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Treat PSAP as a multi-pathway candidate, not a single-direction target. Therapeutic benefit may require simultaneously increasing intracellular lysosomal PSAP/saposin function while limiting harmful extracellular PSAP-driven inflammatory signaling in specific glial states.
  2. Prioritize AD-specific causal validation. Experiments should test PSAP gain- and loss-of-function across microglia, astrocytes, neurons, and oligodendrocytes, measuring Aβ, tau phosphorylation/aggregation, synapse loss, IL-6, NLRP3, ceramide species, GCase activity, lysosomal flux, efferocytosis, myelin integrity, and cognition.
  3. Separate intracellular from extracellular mechanisms rigorously. Intracellular prosaposin/saposins support lysosomal lipid degradation and neuronal resilience, whereas secreted PSAP acts through GPR37/GPR37L1, LRP1, sortilin, or PGRN-related trafficking pathways with cell-type-specific and potentially opposing effects.
  4. Explore the PGRN–lysosome therapeutic axis. Stabilising PGRN–PSAP interactions, improving PSAP trafficking, restoring saposin C/GCase function, or deploying lysotropic modulators such as AZP2006-like compounds may be relevant for AD subtypes dominated by lysosomal dysfunction, tau pathology, or neuroinflammation.
  5. Evaluate PSAP-derived peptides as neuroprotective leads. PS18/prosaptide-like molecules warrant further testing in amyloid, tau, and mixed-pathology AD models given their demonstrated anti-apoptotic, anti-inflammatory, pro-survival, and APP-processing effects in preclinical systems.
  6. Exercise caution with global PSAP upregulation or suppression. PSAP deficiency causes severe lysosomal neurodegeneration and demyelination, but excessive or mislocalized PSAP signaling can be inflammatory or pro-death in certain contexts. Any strategy must be cell-type-, compartment-, receptor-, and disease-stage-specific.
  7. Investigate PSAP as an early biomarker. CSF PSAP has been identified as a potential preclinical AD marker; plaque-associated PSAP/PGRN changes may occur early. Future studies should define whether PSAP rises or falls by disease stage, sex, genotype, brain region, and pathology subtype.
  8. Keep presenilin-associated protein strictly distinct from prosaposin. If targeting the mitochondrial proapoptotic PSAP, the relevant strategy is inhibition of DR6–PSAP–Bax/cytochrome c signaling. If targeting prosaposin, relevant strategies involve lysosomal lipid metabolism, PGRN trafficking, glial signaling, and neurotrophic peptide pathways — entirely different biology.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment —

PSAP/prosaposin occupies a strategically important but mechanistically complex position in Alzheimer's disease biology. It is not yet a validated stand-alone AD therapeutic target, but the convergence of evidence across lysosomal lipid metabolism, Aβ/tau-associated pathology, glial neuroinflammation, PGRN trafficking, and neuroprotective peptide signaling makes it a compelling multi-modal candidate. The PGRN–PSAP axis and PS18-derived peptides represent the most clinically actionable leads, supported by preclinical data in relevant AD models. The key challenge — and the central caution — is that PSAP can be simultaneously neuroprotective and neuroinflammatory depending on cellular compartment, receptor engagement, and disease context. Effective therapeutic exploitation will therefore demand compartment-specific, cell-type-resolved, and disease-stage-aware strategies, alongside rigorous disambiguation from the distinct presenilin-associated protein that shares the same acronym.
