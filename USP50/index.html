⚠ Safety Caveat: USP50 has context-dependent roles in genome maintenance, host defense, cancer biology, ACE2 regulation, and immunometabolism. Broad systemic inhibition carries significant unintended-effect risk and must be evaluated carefully before advancing any therapeutic programme.

USP50 is a deubiquitinating enzyme that regulates inflammatory signaling, inflammasome activation, protein stability, DNA-damage checkpoint control, and related proteostasis pathways. It has not been directly studied in Alzheimer's disease (AD) models, AD patient tissue, amyloid-β or tau pathology, synaptic degeneration, or cognitive decline in the supplied evidence. Its relevance to AD is therefore indirect and mechanistic.

The strongest AD-relevant hypothesis is that USP50 may amplify neuroinflammatory pathways already implicated in AD — especially NLRP3/ASC inflammasome activation, caspase-1 cleavage, IL-1β/IL-18 release, ASC speck formation, pyroptosis, and NF-κB signaling. Across multiple non-AD inflammatory models, USP50 supports inflammasome activity by deubiquitinating ASC and stabilising NLRP3, suggesting that USP50 inhibition could theoretically reduce chronic inflammasome-driven neurotoxicity in AD.

USP50 also has additional indirect links to AD-relevant biology: immunometabolic regulation, autophagy-linked inflammasome clearance, ACE2 stability, lysosomal/endosomal dysfunction, genome maintenance, HSP90-client protein regulation, Wee1 checkpoint control, and DNA-damage response signaling. None of these connections are validated in AD in the provided evidence.

Key Insights

No Direct AD Evidence

USP50 expression or function has not been reported in AD brain tissue, amyloid-β models, tau models, synaptic degeneration, neuronal loss, or cognition in the supplied evidence. Its AD relevance is indirect and mechanistic only.

Positive Inflammasome Regulator

USP50 binds ASC/PYCARD and removes K63-linked ubiquitin chains from ASC, supporting ASC speck formation, caspase-1 activation, and IL-1β/IL-18 secretion. It also stabilises NLRP3 by removing K48-linked chains, preventing proteasomal degradation — a pathway widely considered pathogenic in AD neuroinflammation.

Pharmacologic Modulation (Pristimerin)

Pristimerin binds USP50 at Cys53, blocks USP50–ASC interaction, preserves K63-Ub on ASC, and promotes p62/SQSTM1-mediated autophagic degradation of inflammasome complexes. This confirms chemical tractability, though not yet in AD models.

NF-κB Feed-Forward Loop

NF-κB can regulate USP50 expression in macrophage inflammatory contexts, while USP50 in turn promotes NF-κB signaling and proinflammatory cytokine production — a potential self-amplifying neuroinflammatory circuit relevant to microglial activation in AD.

Immunometabolic & ACE2 Roles

USP50 helps stabilise CPT1a, promoting fatty acid oxidation in macrophages downstream of mitochondrial STAT3 — microglial metabolic state strongly shapes AD inflammatory behaviour, though untested in CNS. USP50 also stabilises ACE2 via deubiquitination, with indirect vascular and neuroinflammatory implications.

Significant Safety Liabilities

USP50 has context-dependent roles in DNA replication fork restart, telomere maintenance, Wee1/G₂–M checkpoint (via HSP90), cancer biology, host defense, and ACE2 regulation. Broad systemic inhibition could produce unintended effects across multiple organ systems; CNS-selective strategies are essential.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — USP50 as an Exploratory AD Neuroinflammation Target

USP50 is a biologically plausible but unvalidated candidate for Alzheimer's disease neuroinflammation. Its strongest mechanistic rationale rests on its established role as a positive regulator of NLRP3/ASC inflammasome activity — deubiquitinating ASC (K63-linked chains) and stabilising NLRP3 (K48-linked chains) — in non-AD inflammatory models. Because chronic NLRP3/ASC inflammasome signalling, caspase-1 activation, IL-1β release, and microglial pyroptosis are widely implicated in AD pathogenesis, USP50 inhibition presents a conceptually coherent therapeutic hypothesis. The identification of pristimerin as a chemical probe binding Cys53 and redirecting inflammasome complexes to autophagic clearance validates the target's pharmacological tractability. However, no evidence from AD tissue, amyloid-β or tau models, or cognitive endpoints currently exists. Moreover, USP50's roles in genome maintenance, host defence, ACE2 regulation, and immunometabolism create meaningful safety uncertainties that must be resolved with CNS-selective strategies and early safety profiling. The immediate scientific priority is establishing whether USP50 governs microglial NLRP3/ASC inflammasome activity in AD-relevant conditions — a finding that would materially elevate its target confidence.
