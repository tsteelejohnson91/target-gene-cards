Overview

⚠ Caveat — Strong Context Dependence: GPR37 activation can be neuroprotective in some settings (microglial phagocytosis, neuronal survival, myelin preservation) but pathogenic in others (oligodendrocyte IL-6 release, apoptotic/PANoptotic signaling, suppressed remyelination). Global agonism or antagonism without cell-type and pathway selectivity carries meaningful therapeutic risk.

GPR37 is a brain-enriched orphan G protein-coupled receptor with mechanistic relevance to Alzheimer's disease (AD), but it is not established in current literature as a validated AD gene, biomarker, or therapeutic target. Available evidence is largely indirect, derived from studies in Parkinson's disease, traumatic brain injury, ischemia, demyelination, retinal degeneration, pain, sepsis, and glial biology.

The most direct AD-related findings are limited and mixed. One study reported altered GPR37 processing in AD necropsy tissue — specifically increased 52 kDa GPR37 in the striatum — while another found no change in cortical GPR37 mRNA or CSF ecto-GPR37 in AD patients, suggesting GPR37 is not a robust general AD biomarker on current evidence.

Mechanistically, GPR37 intersects with multiple AD-relevant processes: neuroinflammation, microglial efferocytosis and phagocytosis, oligodendrocyte and myelin biology, prosaposin/PSAP signaling, neuroprotectin D1 (NPD1) lipid signaling, osteocalcin endocrine signaling, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, ER stress, ADAM10-mediated receptor shedding, Wnt/LRP6 signaling, synaptic plasticity, and neuronal survival.

Overall, GPR37 is best viewed as a plausible but unproven AD-relevant therapeutic node, appealing because it is a CNS-enriched GPCR positioned at immune-glial, myelin, proteostasis, and neuroprotective signaling interfaces. Its major limitation is pronounced context dependence with respect to ligand, cell type, receptor trafficking state, disease stage, and downstream pathway.

Key Insights

Sparse Direct AD Evidence

GPR37 has not been shown to directly regulate amyloid-β, tau pathology, or cognitive decline in AD models. AD relevance is inferred from mechanistic overlap with AD-relevant biology, not from disease-specific experimental validation.

Context-Dependent PSAP–GPR37 Axis

PSAP–GPR37 signaling can preserve myelin, improve connectivity, and promote anti-inflammatory macrophage polarization in some models, while driving IL-6 release from oligodendrocytes, PANoptosis, and maladaptive paracrine loops in others. Global PSAP–GPR37 stimulation carries therapeutic risk without cell-type and pathway control.

NPD1–GPR37 Protective Axis

In traumatic brain injury and pain/stress models, neuroprotectin D1 (NPD1) effects required GPR37 and were associated with reduced glial activation, neuroinflammation, demyelination, and cognitive impairment — mechanisms that overlap substantially with AD pathobiology and warrant direct investigation.

Oligodendrocyte & Myelin Biology

GPR37 is enriched in oligodendrocyte-lineage cells and is linked to myelin maintenance, oligodendrocyte differentiation, and remyelination. White-matter injury and network disconnection contribute to AD cognitive decline, making GPR37 a plausible myelin-preservation or repair target — though the direction of modulation matters: activation may preserve intact myelin, while inhibition may enhance remyelination after injury.

Proteostasis & ER Stress Risk

Misfolded or intracellularly accumulated GPR37 (PAEL-R) can trigger ER stress, unfolded protein responses, and apoptosis. HRD1-mediated ERAD clears this receptor and also degrades APP, reducing Aβ generation — and HRD1 is reported reduced in AD cortex. Any GPR37-targeted therapy must assess risk of misfolded, ER-retained receptor accumulation.

ADAM10 Shedding & Biomarker Complexity

GPR37 undergoes N-terminal ectodomain shedding by ADAM10 and furin. Because ADAM10 is central to non-amyloidogenic APP processing, GPR37 cleavage state may intersect with AD-relevant membrane-protein biology. Current biomarker evidence is mixed: one study found ↑ 52 kDa GPR37 in AD striatum, while another found no change in cortical mRNA or CSF ecto-GPR37.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Treat GPR37 as an exploratory, not validated, AD target. Current evidence establishes mechanistic plausibility but does not demonstrate disease-modifying effects in AD models. Investment should be scoped accordingly.
  2. Prioritize AD-specific validation across cell types and disease stages. Assess GPR37 expression, cleavage products, localization, trafficking, and ligand responsiveness in human AD brain across neurons, microglia, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, and vascular cells at defined disease stages.
  3. Test effects on core AD endpoints. GPR37 modulation should be evaluated for effects on amyloid-β clearance, tau phosphorylation and spread, synaptic loss, neuroinflammation, white-matter damage, neuronal survival, and cognition in AD-relevant models.
  4. Use human disease-relevant cell systems. Because GPR37 pharmacology is highly context-dependent, prioritize human iPSC-derived microglia, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, neurons, organoids, and co-culture systems over heterologous receptor overexpression.
  5. Explore biased or cell-selective GPR37 modulators. The optimal therapeutic concept is pathway-biased modulation that enhances protective signaling (phagocytosis, inflammatory resolution, neuronal survival) while avoiding inflammatory, apoptotic, or proteotoxic branches — not global agonism or antagonism.
  6. Investigate pro-resolving agonist strategies. NPD1/protectin-like GPR37 activation should be tested for AD-relevant benefits including improved microglial phagocytosis, amyloid and debris clearance, reduced cytokine toxicity, and restored inflammatory resolution.
  7. Evaluate PSAP–GPR37 with caution. PSAP augmentation may benefit myelin and connectivity, but may be harmful in contexts involving IL-6 release, PANoptosis, apoptotic propagation, or maladaptive glial signaling. Cell-type selectivity is essential.
  8. Distinguish myelin preservation from remyelination goals. GPR37 activation may support myelin integrity after injury, while GPR37 inhibition may promote remyelination after demyelination. Preclinical and clinical studies must clearly define which biology is being targeted.
  9. Include receptor-processing biomarkers. Assays for GPR37 isoforms, ADAM10/furin cleavage products, and ecto-GPR37 in CSF or brain tissue could clarify whether GPR37 processing is altered in AD subtypes and should be incorporated into any biomarker panel.
  10. Address proteostasis risk early in development. Any GPR37-targeted therapy should assess whether it increases misfolded, ER-retained, or aggregated receptor. Strategies promoting correct folding, trafficking, and surface signaling are likely safer than those that increase total receptor abundance.
  11. Consider rational combination approaches. GPR37 modulation may pair productively with interventions targeting ER stress, HRD1/ERAD, ADAM10 biology, lipid mediator signaling, Wnt/LRP6 signaling, mitochondrial resilience, or myelin repair.
  12. Monitor systemic safety. GPR37 is implicated in macrophage function, inflammatory disease, peripheral tissues, and some cancer-associated pathways. Systemic chronic activation may carry immune or proliferative risks; CNS- or cell-targeted delivery may be preferable.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — GPR37 in Alzheimer's Disease

GPR37 represents a biologically compelling but mechanistically unproven node in AD-relevant neurobiology. Its appeal derives from its status as a CNS-enriched GPCR positioned at the intersection of immune-glial signaling, myelin biology, proteostasis, lipid mediator responses, and neuroprotective endocrine axes — all processes implicated in AD progression. However, direct AD evidence is sparse and mixed: no study in the supplied literature demonstrates that GPR37 modulates amyloid-β, tau, or cognitive outcomes in AD models, and current biomarker data do not establish GPR37 as a robust AD readout.

The most defensible hypothesis is that GPR37 sits at a metabolic–immune–glial nexus where it may influence inflammatory resolution, phagocytic clearance, oligodendrocyte and myelin integrity, mitochondrial resilience, ER proteostasis, and neuroprotective lipid signaling — mechanisms central to AD but requiring direct experimental confirmation. The receptor's pronounced context dependence (protective vs. pathogenic outcomes depending on ligand, cell type, trafficking state, and disease stage) means that therapeutic development must pursue biased, cell-selective modulation rather than global receptor activation or suppression. Advancing GPR37 as an AD target requires a structured validation program anchored in human disease-relevant biology.
