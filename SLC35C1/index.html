Overview

⚠ Caution — Indirect & Unvalidated Target: The supplied literature does not establish SLC35C1 as an Alzheimer's disease mechanism or validated therapeutic target. Its relevance to AD is entirely inferential. Direct inhibition carries documented immune and systemic safety risks from inherited deficiency phenotypes.

SLC35C1 is the primary Golgi GDP-fucose transporter required for cellular fucosylation of glycoproteins and glycoconjugates. By shuttling GDP-fucose into the Golgi lumen, it enables the full repertoire of fucosyltransferases to generate fucosylated glycans on secreted and membrane-bound proteins.

The supplied text does not provide direct evidence linking SLC35C1 to Alzheimer's disease, amyloid-beta, tau pathology, synaptic loss, microglial dysfunction, dementia progression, or cognitive decline in AD models or patients. Its relevance to AD is therefore inferential: fucosylation affects immune-cell adhesion, inflammatory signaling, cell–cell recognition, receptor signaling, intracellular trafficking, Notch signaling, Wnt signaling, EGFR/NF-κB signaling, and glycoprotein homeostasis — processes that overlap with AD-relevant biology — but the causal links remain undemonstrated in AD systems.

Key Insights

Core Golgi Fucosylation Role

SLC35C1 transports GDP-fucose into the Golgi lumen, enabling fucosyltransferases to generate fucosylated glycans on glycoproteins and glycoconjugates. Loss causes global hypofucosylation and is causative for Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type II (LAD-II / CDG-IIc).

Immune Safety Risk from Deficiency

Inherited SLC35C1 deficiency produces recurrent infections, leukocytosis/neutrophilia, impaired wound healing, and failure to recruit leukocytes via fucosylated selectin ligands. These phenotypes mean broad SLC35C1 inhibition in patients carries documented immunological safety risks.

Neurodevelopmental — Not AD — Disease

Human SLC35C1-related disease includes psychomotor delay, intellectual disability, speech/cognitive impairment, autistic features, growth retardation, and altered blood-group antigens. These support nervous-system relevance but are congenital neurodevelopmental, not adult-onset neurodegeneration.

Notch & Wnt Pathway Intersections

Notch receptor EGF-like repeats require fucosylation for optimal ligand response; SLC35C2 may partially compensate for SLC35C1 loss. SLC35C1 also modulates canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling — indirectly relevant because Wnt is implicated in synaptic maintenance and neurodegeneration, but without AD-specific validation.

Therapeutic Signal: L-Fucose Supplementation

Oral or exogenous L-fucose can partially restore fucosylation, selectin ligands, immune function, and some developmental/behavioral features in LAD-II patients or models. Responses are mutation-dependent and may carry adverse immune or blood-antigen effects; this is the strongest therapeutic evidence available.

Biomanufacturing vs. Therapeutic Targeting

SLC35C1-deficient CHO-cell systems produce afucosylated therapeutic antibodies with enhanced Fc effector function — a valuable biomanufacturing tool. This is entirely separate from any rationale for targeting SLC35C1 in AD patients and should not be conflated with a therapeutic hypothesis.
