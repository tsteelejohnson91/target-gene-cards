MTSS2 & Alzheimer's Disease

Overview

⚠ Evidence Caveat MTSS2 is not established as a direct Alzheimer's disease gene or validated AD therapeutic target. Current AD-linked evidence is associative (cross-trait GWAS) and no published data demonstrate altered MTSS2 expression, phosphorylation, or activity in AD brain, nor that MTSS2 modulation affects amyloid-β, tau, neuroinflammation, or cognition in AD models. All mechanistic relevance is inferred from MTSS2's general neuronal roles.

MTSS2 (also known as MTSS1L or ABBA) is a brain-enriched I-BAR domain protein that participates in plasma membrane curvature sensing and induction, actin-membrane coupling, protrusion formation, and cytoskeletal remodeling. Its expression is highest in neurons, particularly inhibitory interneurons including parvalbumin-positive cells that are critical for network synchrony.

Its strongest Alzheimer's disease relevance emerges from two converging lines of evidence: (1) genetic association as a shared susceptibility locus identified in a 2022 cross-trait GWAS linking AD and gastrointestinal disorders, and (2) mechanistic involvement in synaptic plasticity, dendritic spine formation, hippocampal circuit remodeling, Rho-family GTPase signaling, and neuronal connectivity — all processes disrupted early in AD pathogenesis.

Human loss-of-function variants in MTSS2, notably the recurrent missense p.Arg671Trp, cause neurodevelopmental disorders with intellectual disability, microcephaly, and seizures, underscoring the protein's importance for brain development and neural circuit integrity.

Key Insights

Genetic Association — AD & GI Disorders

A 2022 cross-trait GWAS identified MTSS2 among shared susceptibility loci for Alzheimer's disease and gastrointestinal tract disorders. This association is purely statistical; MTSS2 has not been established as a causal driver of AD.

Exercise-Induced Synaptic Plasticity

In hippocampal neurons, Mtss1L/Mtss2 is strongly induced by voluntary exercise and is required for exercise-driven increases in dendritic spine density and excitatory postsynaptic currents — directly relevant to the cognitive resilience conferred by exercise in aging and AD risk.

Membrane Curvature & Spine Initiation

MTSS2 clusters at the plasma membrane before new filopodium formation, promoting dendritic spine density via I-BAR domain-mediated membrane curvature induction and actin-membrane coupling — mechanisms central to synaptic maintenance disrupted early in AD.

Parvalbumin Interneuron Expression

MTSS2/ABBA is highly expressed in inhibitory neurons including parvalbumin-positive interneurons, which are critical for network synchrony. Dysfunction of PV interneurons is implicated in the excitatory/inhibitory imbalance observed in AD-related circuit disruption.

Phosphosite Regulatory Nodes

Phosphoproteomic data identify MTSS2 phosphosites S456, S579, S601, and S612, with predicted regulation by MARK2, PAK1/2, and PRKCD — kinase pathways overlapping with cytoskeletal polarity, apoptotic signaling, and neuronal vulnerability mechanisms relevant to AD.

Human Variants & Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Recurrent human MTSS2 variants, especially p.Arg671Trp, cause neurodevelopmental disorders featuring intellectual disability, microcephaly, and seizures, confirming an essential role in brain development — but no direct AD-functional evidence exists from human AD brain data.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — MTSS2 in Alzheimer's Disease

MTSS2 is a biologically compelling but evidence-limited candidate in the Alzheimer's disease landscape. Its mechanistic roles in dendritic spine formation, membrane curvature, actin remodeling, hippocampal plasticity, and Rho GTPase signaling map directly onto synaptic processes that fail early in AD. The discovery that Mtss2 is required for exercise-induced synaptic plasticity in hippocampal neurons offers a potentially tractable link to cognitive resilience mechanisms. Genetic evidence from a cross-trait AD–GI GWAS places MTSS2 at a shared susceptibility locus, but this remains associative. The protein's enrichment in parvalbumin interneurons connects it to network synchrony deficits relevant to AD circuit failure. Human loss-of-function variants confirm brain-critical roles. However, the field currently lacks any direct evidence that MTSS2 is altered in AD brain, or that its modulation influences amyloid-β, tau, neuroinflammation, or cognition in AD models. MTSS2 merits focused, AD-specific experimental investment — particularly in synaptic phenotyping, phosphosite characterization, and exercise-plasticity models — before it can be elevated to a validated therapeutic target.
