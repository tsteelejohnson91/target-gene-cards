Overview

⚠ Caveat — Receptor & Context Dependency: Broad ANXA1 upregulation is not uniformly beneficial. ANXA1–FPR1 or tumor-associated signaling can promote immunosuppression, macrophage remodeling, invasion, or inflammatory pathology. CNS-targeted, receptor-biased approaches are strongly preferred over nonspecific ANXA1 activation.

ANXA1 is an Alzheimer's disease (AD)-relevant molecule supported by two distinct evidence streams: direct AD-associated findings and broader mechanistic evidence from neuroinflammatory, vascular, immune, and tissue-injury models. The direct AD evidence supports ANXA1 mainly as a biomarker and immune-pathway candidate, while the broader literature supports its plausibility as a therapeutic target for AD mechanisms involving chronic neuroinflammation, microglial dysfunction, impaired amyloid/debris clearance, tau-associated signaling, oxidative stress, mitochondrial injury, blood–brain barrier disruption, and immune-resolution failure.

AD-specific findings include strong peripheral ANXA1 upregulation in AD blood, with one study reporting an 8.078-fold increase and diagnostic performance of AUC = 0.866 for ANXA1 alone, and AUC = 0.912 when combined with VCAM1. ANXA1 also appears in urinary extracellular-vesicle signatures, hippocampal hub-gene networks, APOE4-associated CD8 central memory T-cell dysregulation, and peripheral T-cell proteomic changes in APP/PS1 mice.

Mechanistically, ANXA1 is most consistently described as an anti-inflammatory, pro-resolving mediator acting through formyl peptide receptors, especially FPR2/ALX and, in some contexts, FPR1 or FPR3. The most plausible AD therapeutic strategy is selective enhancement of ANXA1's pro-resolution functions, particularly ANXA1–FPR2/ALX signaling or ANXA1-derived peptides such as Ac2-26, while carefully accounting for receptor context.

Key Insights

Direct AD Biomarker Support

Peripheral blood ANXA1 is significantly elevated in AD patients (8.078-fold; AUC = 0.866 alone, AUC = 0.912 combined with VCAM1). ANXA1 additionally appears in AD-associated urinary extracellular vesicles, hippocampal hub-gene networks, APOE4-linked CD8 T-cell signatures, and APP/PS1 peripheral T-cell proteomics.

Inflammation Resolution — Core Therapeutic Rationale

ANXA1 repeatedly suppresses TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, NF-κB, TLR4-linked signaling, and NLRP3 inflammasome activity — pathways central to AD-related glial activation and neurodegeneration. The strongest therapeutic rationale is resolution of inflammation rather than direct anti-amyloid or anti-tau action.

Microglial Clearance & Amyloid / Tau Indirect Links

ANXA1 is linked to efferocytosis, phagocytosis, CD36-mediated clearance, and macrophage/microglial phenotype switching relevant to Aβ removal. In a rat AD model, butyrate restored ANXA1, suppressed ANXA2/GSK-3β/TNF-α, reduced amyloid burden and phosphorylated tau, and improved cognition.

Oxidative Stress, Mitochondria & BBB Protection

ANXA1 or Ac2-26 reduces ROS, preserves GPX4/FTH1/SLC7A11 against ferroptosis, supports PINK1/Parkin mitophagy and SIRT3-related mitochondrial function. ANXA1 also maintains occludin, claudin-5, and ZO-1 tight junctions, protecting blood–brain barrier integrity — relevant to vascular and mixed AD phenotypes.

Receptor Context — Critical Safety Caveat

ANXA1–FPR2/ALX signaling is pro-resolving and protective; ANXA1–FPR1 or FPR1/3 signaling can promote immunosuppression, macrophage remodeling, tumor progression, or inflammatory pathology. Broad ANXA1 upregulation is not uniformly beneficial. Receptor-biased, CNS-targeted, dose-controlled strategies are essential.

AD-Specific Validation Gap

Most mechanistic data originate from non-AD models: stroke, spinal cord injury, Parkinson's disease, traumatic brain injury, sepsis, colitis, cardiovascular injury, and cancer. These support biological plausibility but do not prove AD efficacy. Direct validation in AD models with amyloid, tau, synaptic, and cognitive endpoints remains a priority.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Treat ANXA1 as a priority biomarker candidate. Plasma/serum ANXA1, CSF ANXA1, urinary EV ANXA1-positive subpopulations, brain-derived EV ANXA1, and cell-specific ANXA1 in microglia, astrocytes, endothelial cells, T cells, and monocytes should all be included in AD biomarker matrices.
  2. Validate whether ANXA1 elevation is protective, compensatory, or pathogenic. AD studies should compare ANXA1 levels with amyloid burden, tau pathology, neuroinflammatory markers, BBB integrity, cognitive stage, APOE genotype, age, sex, and vascular comorbidities to resolve directionality.
  3. Prioritize FPR2/ALX-biased approaches over nonspecific ANXA1 activation. The most attractive strategies are Ac2-26-like peptides, FPR2-biased agonists, recombinant ANXA1 delivery, ANXA1-enriched extracellular vesicles, or methods that enhance membrane-accessible ANXA1 specifically in microglia and brain endothelium.
  4. Do not equate total ANXA1 abundance with therapeutic activity. Studies must distinguish intracellular versus extracellular ANXA1, nuclear versus cytoplasmic localization, EV-associated ANXA1, cleaved versus intact forms, post-translational modifications (phosphorylation, SUMOylation, succinylation), and FPR1 versus FPR2 receptor engagement.
  5. Test ANXA1 interventions directly in AD models. Key endpoints must include amyloid-β plaque burden, phagocytosis/efferocytosis, soluble Aβ clearance, tau phosphorylation, GSK-3β activity, microglial and astrocyte states, NLRP3/NF-κB signaling, synaptic markers, BBB integrity, mitochondrial function, ferroptosis markers, EV profiles, and cognition.
  6. Examine microglial clearance carefully alongside anti-inflammation. Excessive anti-inflammatory or M2-like skewing could impair protective immune surveillance or aggregate clearance. Studies should measure both inflammatory suppression and phagocytic/clearance capacity concurrently.
  7. Investigate the ANXA1/ANXA2 reciprocity axis. The butyrate AD model suggests restoring ANXA1 dominance while suppressing ANXA2 may reduce TNF-α, GSK-3β activation, tau phosphorylation, amyloid burden, oxidative imbalance, and cognitive impairment. This axis warrants dedicated study.
  8. Explore vascular and BBB-focused indications. ANXA1-targeted therapies may be especially relevant for AD patients with vascular dysfunction, BBB breakdown, endothelial activation, hypertension, cerebral hypoperfusion, or mixed AD/vascular dementia phenotypes.
  9. Consider gut–brain and metabolic modulation routes. Butyrate and other interventions that restore ANXA1 signaling or correct annexin balance may be relevant where microbiome dysfunction, systemic inflammation, oxidative stress, or metabolic impairment contributes to AD progression.
  10. Build rigorous safety controls around receptor specificity and systemic effects. Chronic or systemic ANXA1 activation carries risks related to tumor immunosuppression, maladaptive macrophage polarization, platelet activation, and excessive immune dampening. CNS-targeted, cell-specific, dose-controlled approaches are mandatory.
  11. Use ANXA1 as a pharmacodynamic marker in immunomodulatory AD trials. Changes in ANXA1-positive EVs, ANXA1/FPR2 pathway activity, cytokine suppression, microglial phagocytic markers, and BBB integrity markers could identify responders and optimize dosing in clinical studies.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — ANXA1 in Alzheimer's Disease

ANXA1 is best understood not as a direct anti-amyloid or anti-tau therapeutic, but as a candidate disease-modifying regulator of AD-amplifying processes: unresolved neuroinflammation, impaired microglial clearance, blood–brain barrier dysfunction, oxidative and mitochondrial injury, ferroptosis, autophagy disturbance, and peripheral immune dysregulation. Its peripheral blood upregulation (~8-fold, AUC ~0.87) provides credible early biomarker utility, and the consistent anti-inflammatory, pro-resolving biology of the ANXA1–FPR2/ALX axis — including efferocytosis enhancement, NLRP3/NF-κB suppression, BBB protection, and mitophagy support — offers a mechanistically coherent therapeutic rationale aligned with multiple AD pathways. However, ANXA1's context-dependent dual nature (protective via FPR2, potentially hazardous via FPR1 in immunosuppressive or tumor contexts) demands receptor-biased, compartment-specific, and stage-aware strategies. The ANXA1/ANXA2 balance and the butyrate-linked AD model provide the most direct molecular mechanistic bridge to AD-relevant amyloid and tau biology identified in this review. ANXA1 is a promising but incompletely validated AD target that warrants dedicated AD-model testing before therapeutic advancement.
