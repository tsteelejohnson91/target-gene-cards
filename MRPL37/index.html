Overview

Safety Caveat: MRPL37 appears to be broadly expressed and potentially essential. Direct systemic manipulation may carry significant safety risks; cell-specific delivery and careful dose control would be required for any therapeutic approach.

MRPL37 is most relevant to Alzheimer's disease (AD) as a mitochondrial ribosomal protein linked to mitochondrial translation, oxidative phosphorylation, and energy metabolism. The available literature includes an AD-focused study in which MRPL37 was incorporated into a five-gene mitochondrial energy metabolism risk model alongside MRPL15, RBP4, ABCA1, and MPV17. In that study, MRPL37 was reported to be downregulated in AD brain regions, and the model distinguished AD from non-demented samples with AUC values greater than 0.815 in internal and external validation.

The current evidence supports MRPL37 more strongly as a biomarker or pathway indicator than as a validated causal driver or direct therapeutic target in AD. Its downregulation suggests impaired mitochondrial ribosomal function, reduced mitochondrial protein synthesis, and downstream oxidative phosphorylation defects — all of which align with established AD mechanisms involving neuronal energy failure and mitochondrial dysfunction.

Key Insights

Mitoribosomal Function

MRPL37 is part of the mitochondrial ribosomal large subunit, supporting mitoribosome structural integrity, mitochondrial protein synthesis, oxidative phosphorylation, and cellular energy production.

Downregulation in AD

MRPL37 expression is reduced in AD brain regions. This downregulation was validated using the AlzData database and qRT-PCR, reinforcing its association with AD-relevant mitochondrial impairment.

Risk Model — Biomarker Role

Incorporation into a LASSO-based five-gene model (with MRPL15, RBP4, ABCA1, MPV17) achieved AUC >0.815. Strong predictive performance in a risk model does not, however, establish MRPL37 causality in AD.

Sex-Specific Genetic Association

Genetic studies outside AD suggest MRPL37 variants may associate with mtDNA levels in a sex-specific manner, particularly in males, raising the possibility that MRPL37 could modify mitochondrial vulnerability in AD in a sex-dependent way.

Mito-Immune Axis

MRPL37-containing mitochondrial energy metabolism signatures were correlated with immune-cell differences in AD, suggesting a potential link between mitochondrial dysfunction and immune dysregulation in disease pathogenesis.

Safety & Essentiality Concern

Broader biological evidence indicates MRPL37 is likely essential and broadly expressed. Direct systemic targeting could carry significant safety risks; direct inhibition is not an appropriate therapeutic strategy based on current evidence.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Biomarker first, target later: Treat MRPL37 as a promising AD mitochondrial biomarker and risk-model component, not yet as a proven or actionable therapeutic target.
  2. Establish functional causality: Prioritize studies testing whether MRPL37 downregulation functionally contributes to AD-relevant phenotypes such as impaired respiration, synaptic failure, tau/amyloid toxicity, microglial activation, or neuronal loss.
  3. Multi-gene stratification panels: Explore MRPL37 as part of multi-gene AD stratification panels for identifying patients with mitochondrial energy metabolism impairment, leveraging its validated performance in the five-gene model.
  4. Pathway-level therapeutics: Therapeutic strategies should focus on restoring or supporting the mitochondrial pathways represented by MRPL37 — mitochondrial translation, oxidative phosphorylation, mitoribosome integrity, and mitochondrial biogenesis — rather than directly inhibiting MRPL37 itself.
  5. Safety-first approach to direct manipulation: Any attempt to manipulate MRPL37 directly would require cell-specific delivery, careful dose control, and thorough safety testing given its apparent broad expression and potential essentiality.
  6. Expand research scope: Future AD research should examine whether MRPL37 expression or genetic variation correlates with disease stage, sex-specific risk, mtDNA copy number, mitochondrial respiratory capacity, immune-cell profiles, and treatment response.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — Evidence Synthesis

MRPL37 represents a biologically plausible and statistically supported mitochondrial biomarker in Alzheimer's disease, with validated downregulation in AD brain tissue and demonstrated contribution to a high-performing multi-gene risk prediction model. Its role as a mitoribosomal large-subunit protein places it squarely within the mitochondrial translation and oxidative phosphorylation axis that is increasingly recognized as central to AD neuronal energy failure. However, the current evidence does not establish MRPL37 as a causal driver of AD, and it has no demonstrated direct connection to amyloid pathology, tau pathology, synaptic degeneration, or neuroinflammation. The additional observations linking MRPL37-containing signatures to immune-cell differences and sex-specific mtDNA variant associations open interesting mechanistic hypotheses but require independent validation. Given its likely essentiality and broad expression, direct therapeutic targeting carries real safety concerns, and the preferred translational path is to use MRPL37 as a stratification biomarker while pursuing pathway-level interventions in mitochondrial biogenesis and respiratory function.
