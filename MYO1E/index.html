MYO1E & Alzheimer's Disease

Overview

⚠ Safety Caveat: MYO1E loss-of-function is strongly linked to steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, podocyte foot-process effacement, proteinuria, and kidney failure. Systemic MYO1E inhibition therefore carries a plausible renal toxicity risk and must be considered in any therapeutic program.

MYO1E is an Alzheimer's disease-relevant candidate primarily through microglial biology, cytoskeletal remodeling, endocytosis/phagocytosis, and disease-associated immune states. The strongest direct AD evidence comes from late-onset Alzheimer's disease (LOAD) single-nucleus multi-omics and AD microglial transcriptomic analyses, where MYO1E appears in LOAD-associated microglial regulatory networks and in anti-inflammatory/phagocytic disease-associated microglia (DAM) programs alongside genes such as APOE, IGF1, and CXCR4.

MYO1E is not yet a validated Alzheimer's therapeutic target. The supplied evidence does not show that direct MYO1E modulation improves amyloid-β, tau pathology, synapse preservation, cognition, or neurodegeneration in AD models. Instead, MYO1E is best interpreted as a mechanistic pathway node that may influence AD through microglial motility, phagocytosis, apoptotic-cell clearance, endocytosis, membrane tension responses, cholesterol trafficking, phosphoinositide signaling, and actin–membrane coupling.

Its therapeutic relevance is plausible but unresolved. Enhancing MYO1E-associated protective microglial programs could theoretically improve amyloid/debris clearance and reduce harmful inflammation, but broad MYO1E inhibition or activation may carry risks because MYO1E is essential in kidney podocytes and broadly involved in immune-cell migration, adhesion, endocytosis, and cytoskeletal organization.

Key Insights

Direct LOAD Evidence

MYO1E is identified in a LOAD-relevant microglial subtype through single-nucleus multi-omics, linked to LOAD-associated cis-regulatory elements, putative functional SNPs, transcriptional networks, and GWAS-overlapping regions. APOE and MYO1E are highlighted together as LOAD differentially expressed genes connected to microglial regulatory elements.

Protective Microglial-State Signal

MYO1E appears alongside APOE and IGF1 in anti-inflammatory/phagocytic disease-associated microglia modules in AD models. In kainic acid epileptogenesis models, MYO1E marks an IGF1-positive microglial phenotype driven by TGFB1/Wnt/β-catenin signaling that suppresses inflammatory cytokines and improves neuronal-like cell survival — suggesting MYO1E may mark protective or repair-associated microglial programs.

Phagocytosis & Trogocytosis Relevance

MYO1E/MYO1F-deficient macrophages show impaired uptake of IgG-coated particles, abnormal phagocytic cup actin architecture, stalled engulfment, disrupted podosomes, and increased trogocytosis. In AD, this is relevant because microglia may either clear pathological material efficiently or contribute to harmful synaptic nibbling and pruning.

Endocytosis Mechanism & AD Relevance

MYO1E is recruited to clathrin-mediated endocytic sites, interacts with dynamin and synaptojanin-1, and promotes actin assembly through WIP/WIRE/N-WASP/Arp2/3 machinery, helping rescue endocytosis under increased membrane tension. These mechanisms are relevant to AD via APP processing, receptor trafficking, synaptic biology, and endolysosomal function.

Cholesterol & Migration Axes

MYO1E contributes to actin-dependent delivery of cholesterol-containing caveolin-coated vesicles to the plasma membrane — mechanistically relevant to AD through lipid rafts, ApoE biology, APP processing, and microglial activation. MYO1E also regulates lamellipodia, focal adhesion turnover, podosome dynamics, cell migration, and membrane ruffling, potentially affecting microglial surveillance and plaque recruitment.

Safety Concern: Renal & Immune Risk

MYO1E loss-of-function is strongly linked to steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, podocyte foot-process effacement, proteinuria, and kidney failure risk. Systemic MYO1E inhibition could plausibly cause renal toxicity and immune dysfunction. MYO1E also influences TLR4 responses, CCL2 secretion, MHC-II surface delivery, and antigen processing.

  3. Resolve directionality before targeting. Determine whether MYO1E activity is protective, harmful, or context-dependent across AD stages and microglial subtypes. Evidence suggests possible protective association with anti-inflammatory/phagocytic and IGF1-positive microglia, but MYO1E may also support inflammatory-cell migration.
  4. Use MYO1E as a biomarker candidate for beneficial microglial reprogramming. It may be useful in signatures including IGF1, APOE, and CXCR4 when evaluating interventions such as LXR agonism or Kv1.3 blockade.
  5. Prioritize cell-specific strategies. Brain- or microglia-selective modulation would be safer than systemic targeting given MYO1E's essential role in kidney podocytes and immune-cell function.
  6. Investigate regulatory mechanisms in LOAD. Follow up on MYO1E-linked cis-regulatory elements, chromatin accessibility changes, transcription-factor networks, and GWAS-overlapping variants in AD microglial subtypes.
  7. Explore pathway-level targeting rather than direct MYO1E inhibition. Potentially safer approaches may involve modulating upstream/downstream pathways shaping MYO1E-associated protective microglial states — such as TGFB1/Wnt/β-catenin/IGF1 signaling, LXR pathways, Kv1.3-linked DAM modulation, or specific MYO1E adaptor-domain interactions.
  8. Include safety screens early. Any MYO1E-directed program should assess podocyte integrity, renal filtration markers, immune-cell migration, antigen presentation, and vascular effects before advancing.
  9. Meet the key validation requirement. Demonstrate that MYO1E modulation changes AD-relevant outcomes — amyloid burden, tau pathology, synapse loss, neuroinflammation, or cognition — in human AD tissue, iPSC-derived microglia/neurons, organoids, or AD animal models.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — MYO1E in Alzheimer's Disease MYO1E is a biologically plausible but pre-validation candidate for AD relevance, supported by its presence in LOAD-associated microglial regulatory networks, anti-inflammatory and phagocytic disease-associated microglia programs, and mechanistic roles in phagocytosis, endocytosis, vesicle trafficking, actin remodeling, cholesterol distribution, and cell migration. Its co-expression with APOE, IGF1, and CXCR4 in protective microglial states is encouraging, but no evidence yet demonstrates that MYO1E modulation directly improves amyloid burden, tau pathology, synapse preservation, or cognition in AD models. Safety is a principal constraint: MYO1E's essential role in kidney podocytes creates substantial renal toxicity risk for any systemic approach. The most defensible near-term strategy is to use MYO1E as an exploratory mechanistic and biomarker tool in microglial functional studies, pursue cell-selective or pathway-level modulation strategies, and rigorously validate AD-relevant phenotypic outcomes before any therapeutic commitment.
