It is an ER-localized and plasma-membrane receptor that binds progesterone with high affinity (Kd ≈ 2.85 nM) and couples to stimulatory G-protein/cAMP signaling. Its Alzheimer's relevance rests on mechanistic overlaps with known neurodegeneration pathways rather than direct genetic or clinical evidence.

The strongest Alzheimer's-relevant links include PAQR9's roles in nonclassical neurosteroid signaling, ER protein quality control via BAG6, insulin and metabolic signaling, lipid and ketone metabolism, MAPK/ERK signaling, immune-cell genetics, and age- or infection-sensitive brain expression. These intersect with proteostasis failure, neuroinflammation, impaired brain energy metabolism, lipid dysregulation, and neuronal stress signaling — all central Alzheimer's mechanisms.

PAQR9 has several features that make it theoretically druggable: it is a membrane receptor, binds progesterone with high affinity, and may respond to neurosteroids. However, direct Alzheimer's efficacy remains entirely unproven, and systemic modulation raises reproductive, hepatic, and developmental safety concerns.

Key Insights

Receptor Identity & Signaling

PAQR9/mPRε binds progesterone with a dissociation constant of ~2.85 nM and couples to stimulatory G-protein signaling, elevating cAMP and GTPγS. This positions PAQR9 at the intersection of nonclassical neurosteroid signaling and pathways relevant to neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity, though direct Alzheimer's validation is absent.

ER Quality Control & BAG6

PAQR9 is an ER-localized protein that interacts with BAG6, a core factor in post-translational quality control of mislocalized membrane proteins. PAQR9 knockdown accelerates their degradation and polyubiquitination. Disrupting the PAQR9–BAG6 interaction under ER stress improved insulin secretion and reduced ER-stress markers, underscoring a role in proteostasis vulnerability relevant to neurodegeneration.

Metabolic Regulation & ERK Dysregulation

PAQR9 stabilizes PPARα to regulate fasting ketogenesis and fatty-acid oxidation. In statin-induced hepatic insulin resistance, elevated PAQR9 promotes STUB1-mediated degradation of PPM1α, activates ERK signaling, and impairs insulin signaling — both insulin resistance and ERK dysregulation being recognized contributors to Alzheimer's risk and progression.

Neurosteroid & Lipid Biology

PAQR9 belongs to a membrane progesterone receptor family implicated in nonclassical neurosteroid signaling. Neurosteroids such as allopregnanolone have reported neuroprotective and anti-apoptotic properties in Alzheimer's biology. Additionally, PAQR9 was identified as a phospholipid GWAS locus alongside APOE, placing it within a lipid-metabolism landscape central to AD risk — though no direct PAQR9–APOE interaction is established.

Brain Expression & Immune Genetics

PAQR9 is expressed in human pituitary gland and hypothalamus, and its expression is age- and infection-sensitive in neuronal and non-neuronal cells. A genetic study linked PAQR9 to a myeloid dendritic-cell CD11c trait, suggesting indirect immune relevance given Alzheimer's innate-immune involvement — though no direct evidence exists for PAQR9 in microglia or Alzheimer's neuroinflammation.

Safety & Therapeutic Tradeoffs

PAQR9 has reproductive and developmental roles raising safety concerns for systemic targeting. Inhibiting PAQR9 may improve diabetic phenotypes — reducing hyperglycemia, hepatic steatosis, and β-cell apoptosis — but could impair adaptive ketone production critical for brain energy metabolism, creating an inherent therapeutic tradeoff that must be evaluated before any clinical claim.
