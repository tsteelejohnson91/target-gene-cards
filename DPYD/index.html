DPYD encodes dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD), the rate-limiting enzyme in the catabolism of pyrimidines and fluoropyrimidines. Key Insights

Established Role: Fluoropyrimidine Catabolism

DPYD encodes DPD, the rate-limiting enzyme controlling clearance of 5-FU, capecitabine, and related fluoropyrimidines. This pharmacogenomic safety role is the dominant and overwhelmingly evidenced function across the supplied text.

Toxicity Profile of Impaired DPD

Reduced or absent DPD activity drives accumulation of 5-FU, causing gastrointestinal toxicity, diarrhea, mucositis, bone marrow suppression, hematologic toxicity, hand-foot syndrome, cardiotoxicity, neurotoxicity, hospitalization, and death.

Clinically Actionable Variants

Four principal variants — DPYD*2A (rs3918290), DPYD*13 (rs55886062), c.2846A>T (rs67376798), and HapB3 — are the focus of pre-treatment genotyping, dose-reduction algorithms, and ancestry-aware expanded panels.

Diagnostic & Dosing Strength

DPYD pre-treatment testing, genotype-guided dose reduction, and DPD phenotyping via uracil or dihydrouracil/uracil ratios are strongly supported as precision medicine strategies to reduce severe fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy toxicity.

Indirect Neuro-Adjacent Biology

Mouse Dpyd influences sleep duration via β-alanine biology; rare developmental/epileptic phenotypes and schizophrenia genetic signals have been noted. Cancer studies implicate STAT3-DPYD signaling, S1PR2/Hippo/TWIST1 transcriptional control, miRNA regulation, and tumor metabolism — all disease-context specific.

No Direct Alzheimer's Disease Evidence
