The SPTB gene encodes beta-spectrin, a membrane-cytoskeletal scaffolding protein best established in erythrocyte biology. The strongest direct evidence positions SPTB as a clinically important gene for hereditary red blood cell membrane disorders — including hereditary spherocytosis, hereditary elliptocytosis, hereditary pyropoikilocytosis, neonatal jaundice, hemolytic anemia, splenomegaly, gallstones, and hydrops fetalis. Across these disorders, SPTB variants impair beta-spectrin function, reduce membrane stability and deformability, alter erythrocyte shape, and promote hemolysis.

The most Alzheimer's-adjacent information is indirect. A single neuroscience record describes beta-I spectrin as brain-expressed, enriched in hippocampal and cortical regions, and involved in postsynaptic organization. Loss of beta-I spectrin in ja/ja mice reduced postsynaptic density size and altered ankyrinR and NCAM levels — a possible role in synaptic architecture, but not a demonstrated link to Alzheimer's disease. Broader spectrin-family literature links neuronal spectrins (SPTAN1, SPTBN1, SPTBN2, SPTBN4) to scaffolding, receptor organization, actin dynamics, calcium/calmodulin signaling, and calpain-linked regulation — processes conceptually overlapping with neurodegeneration, but not established for SPTB specifically.
