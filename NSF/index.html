Key Insights

Synaptic Vesicle Trafficking — Core Plausibility

NSF is an ATPase essential for SNARE-complex disassembly and vesicle recycling, a prerequisite for neurotransmitter release. Because synaptic dysfunction is an early, clinically prominent feature of Alzheimer's disease, altered NSF levels in cortical proteomics may plausibly reflect exercise-associated restoration of synaptic trafficking capacity.

Evidence Source — Single Exercise Proteomics Study

The sole Alzheimer's-relevant signal comes from one proteomics dataset in 6-month-old 3×Tg-AD mice after 12 weeks of treadmill exercise. Directionality of NSF change is not reported, independent validation is absent, and whether NSF modulation was necessary or sufficient for cognitive improvement is unknown.

Synaptic–Mitochondrial Co-Occurrence

NSF co-occurs with DNM1 (dynamin, presynaptic fission), DLG2 (postsynaptic scaffolding), and OPA1 (mitochondrial dynamics). This constellation suggests a coordinated recovery of vesicle cycling, postsynaptic organization, and energy supply — biologically plausible given Alzheimer's disease's dual synaptic and mitochondrial impairments.

Inferential SNARE–APP/Aβ Links

  1. Do not treat NSF as a validated Alzheimer's therapeutic target. The evidence is insufficient to claim that NSF causally contributes to Alzheimer's disease or that NSF modulation would be disease-modifying. Overclaiming based on a single proteomics association would be premature.
  2. Treat NSF as a candidate synaptic-trafficking marker requiring validation. Essential next steps include determining the directionality of NSF change in 3×Tg-AD mice, validating protein levels and ATPase activity independently, and testing whether NSF modulation affects synaptic function, amyloid/tau pathology, or cognition in controlled experiments.

As a broadly required ATPase for SNARE recycling and membrane fusion, global NSF activation or inhibition risks disrupting essential neuronal and systemic trafficking.
