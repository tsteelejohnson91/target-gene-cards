⚠ Evidence Caveat
The supplied evidence contains no direct Alzheimer's disease study of GUCY1A1: no AD patient genetics, no amyloid-β or tau experiments, and no cognitive endpoint data. AD relevance is entirely indirect, derived from mechanistic overlaps with AD pathobiology.

GUCY1A1 encodes the α1 subunit of soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC), a nitric oxide (NO) receptor that converts NO signaling into cyclic GMP (cGMP) production and activates downstream PKG signaling. Dysfunction in this pathway is linked to impaired vasorelaxation, hypertension, microvascular injury, thrombosis, atherosclerosis, stroke susceptibility, and cerebrovascular disease — processes that can drive cognitive decline and contribute to Alzheimer's disease progression.

The strongest AD-relevant rationale is vascular and neurovascular: reduced or dysfunctional GUCY1A1/sGC signaling impairs cerebral perfusion, destabilizes the endothelium, and elevates blood–brain barrier vulnerability. GUCY1A1 also intersects with oxidative and neuroprotective mechanisms — in ischemia-reperfusion and retinal injury models, sGC activation restored cGMP signaling, preserved GPX4, and limited ferroptosis and lipid peroxidation. These findings support a therapeutic hypothesis in which enhancing GUCY1A1/sGC activity may protect the neurovascular unit, though this has not yet been demonstrated in AD-specific models.

Genetic associations link GUCY1A1/GUCY1A3 pathway variants to hypertension, peripheral artery disease, coronary disease, atherosclerotic stroke, and moyamoya-like cerebral angiopathy. Platelet and inflammatory effects — including leukocyte adhesion modulation and angiopoietin-1 release — further position this pathway at the intersection of vascular inflammation and endothelial stability relevant to AD.

Key Insights

Central Mechanism — NO–sGC–cGMP–PKG

GUCY1A1/sGC is the molecular bridge between NO signaling and intracellular cGMP production. PKG, activated by cGMP, governs vascular tone, platelet function, endothelial stability, and cellular stress responses — making this a broad-spectrum regulator of vascular homeostasis.

No Direct AD Evidence

The supplied data contain no GUCY1A1 studies in Alzheimer's patients, no amyloid-β or tau experiments, and no cognitive endpoints. GUCY1A1 is best treated as a biologically plausible, indirect AD-relevant target — not an established AD gene or validated drug target for this disease.

Anti-Ferroptotic & Endothelial Protection

Vericiguat treatment or GUCY1A1 overexpression activated cGMP–PKG signaling, preserved GPX4, and reduced lipid peroxidation, countering endothelial ferroptosis and microvascular dysfunction. Lipid peroxidation and microvascular injury are implicated in neurodegeneration, lending indirect AD relevance.

Vascular Genetic Risk Associations

Rare-variant and exome studies link impaired GUCY1A1/GUCY1A3 sGC signaling to hypertension, peripheral artery disease, coronary disease, atherosclerotic stroke, and moyamoya-like cerebral angiopathy — cerebrovascular conditions that overlap with small-vessel disease mechanisms relevant to AD.

Therapeutic Candidates — sGC Modulators

Runcaciguat demonstrated neuroretinal protection in oxidative injury models by restoring impaired sGC function. Vericiguat showed anti-ferroptotic endothelial effects. Both compounds represent potential therapeutic tools for testing AD-relevant neurovascular hypotheses, though brain-penetrant activity requires investigation.

Important Limitations & Nomenclature Caution

No evidence links GUCY1A1 to amyloid deposition, tau pathology, microglial activation, synaptic plasticity, or cognition. Additionally, literature uses GUCY1A3 and GUCY1A1 inconsistently for the human sGC α1 subunit; conclusions should carefully distinguish gene-specific from pathway-level evidence.
