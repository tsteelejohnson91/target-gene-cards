Overview

⚠ Safety Caveat: Direct inhibition of MRPL50 would likely be harmful. Loss-of-function of MRPL50 is itself pathogenic, causing mitoribosome destabilization and respiratory-chain impairment. MRPL50 is broadly expressed, essential, and has no known redundant compensatory protein.

MRPL50 is a mitochondrial ribosomal large-subunit protein whose primary roles encompass mitochondrial translation and maintenance of oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) capacity — particularly the stability of respiratory chain complex I. The supplied evidence does not directly link MRPL50 to Alzheimer's disease, Alzheimer's patient brain tissue, disease models, or validated Alzheimer's therapeutic targeting. Its relevance to neurodegeneration is mechanistically indirect: MRPL50 supports mitoribosome integrity and respiratory-chain abundance, pathways that overlap substantially with the mitochondrial dysfunction observed in Alzheimer's disease. A homozygous variant (p.Val112Asp) has been documented in patient fibroblasts, leading to loss of MRPL50 protein, large-subunit destabilization, and a mild but significant reduction in complex I abundance. Clinically, MRPL50 mutations are associated with Perrault-spectrum disease — featuring premature ovarian insufficiency, sensorineural hearing loss, kidney dysfunction, and cardiac involvement — rather than primary neurodegenerative disease.

Key Insights

Mitoribosome Selectivity
MRPL50 deficiency destabilizes the mitochondrial ribosomal large subunit while leaving the small subunit intact, producing a selective assembly defect that impairs overall mitochondrial translation capacity.
Clinical Variant Evidence
Patient fibroblasts carrying the homozygous p.Val112Asp variant showed complete loss of MRPL50 protein and a mild but statistically significant reduction in respiratory chain Complex I abundance — the only direct human disease evidence available.
Disease Association: Perrault Spectrum
MRPL50 mutations are linked to Perrault-spectrum disease — premature ovarian insufficiency, sensorineural hearing loss, kidney dysfunction, and heart dysfunction — not to Alzheimer's disease. No direct AD evidence exists in the supplied data.
Mechanistic Plausibility for AD
Alzheimer's relevance is biologically plausible because mitochondrial dysfunction, OXPHOS defects, Complex I impairment, and proteostatic stress are established themes in neurodegeneration — but no supplied evidence demonstrates MRPL50 involvement in AD tissue or models.
Therapeutic Direction: Restoration
If MRPL50 impairment is confirmed in AD contexts, therapeutic strategies should focus on restoring or stabilizing MRPL50 function, improving mitoribosome assembly, or supporting downstream OXPHOS — not on inhibiting MRPL50.
Safety: No Inhibition Strategy
Direct MRPL50 inhibition would likely be harmful. MRPL50 is essential, broadly expressed across tissues, and largely nonredundant; its loss is itself the pathogenic event underlying human disease.
