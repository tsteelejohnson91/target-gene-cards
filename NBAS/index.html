NBAS (Neuroblastoma Amplified Sequence) is a multifunctional protein involved in ER–Golgi retrograde trafficking, nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, autophagy, ER stress responses, oxidative stress, and apoptosis. In the supplied literature, NBAS dysfunction is primarily associated with rare autosomal recessive multisystem disorders — most notably infantile liver failure syndrome type 2 (ILFS2) and fever-triggered recurrent acute liver failure — as well as SOPH syndrome, short stature, optic atrophy, Pelger-Huët anomaly, immune defects, and broader developmental disease.

No supplied study evaluates NBAS in Alzheimer's patients, AD animal or cellular models, amyloid-β biology, tau pathology, synaptic degeneration, microglial activation, or dementia outcomes. The mechanistic pathways disrupted by NBAS deficiency — proteostasis, ER stress, reactive oxygen species accumulation, vesicular trafficking, RNA quality control — broadly overlap with processes implicated in neurodegeneration, making NBAS an indirect, hypothesis-generating candidate for future exploratory study in AD contexts, but nothing more at present.

No supplied study tests NBAS in Alzheimer's patients or models. There is no evidence linking NBAS to amyloid-β processing, tau phosphorylation or aggregation, synaptic failure, neuronal loss in AD, microglial activation, complement signaling, or AD-associated cognition.

Established Disease Context

Biallelic or compound heterozygous NBAS pathogenic variants cause fever-triggered recurrent acute liver failure, ILFS2, and SOPH syndrome — a multisystem disorder encompassing short stature, optic atrophy, Pelger-Huët anomaly, immune defects, cytopenias, hypogammaglobulinemia, skeletal abnormalities, and developmental delay.

ER Stress–ROS–Apoptosis Axis

Thermal or febrile stress in NBAS-deficient fibroblasts triggers ER stress, reactive oxygen species accumulation, and apoptotic tissue injury. This mirrors broad neurodegenerative stress biology, but evidence comes exclusively from liver-failure and fibroblast contexts — not AD neurons or AD models.

Therapeutic Signals (NBAS Disease)

N-acetylcysteine reduced oxidative stress and apoptosis in NBAS-deficient fibroblasts under thermal stress and showed a trend toward reduced hepatic-crisis severity. Spermidine improved autophagic flux in patient fibroblasts. Neither intervention has Alzheimer's-specific NBAS evidence.

NBAS participates in ER–Golgi retrograde transport, syntaxin-18/NRZ tethering, NMD-based RNA surveillance, autophagy, and oxidative stress response — all pathways broadly relevant to neurodegeneration. This overlap is hypothesis-generating only; no mechanistic link to AD pathology is established.
