Overview

Interpretive Caveat: Current evidence does not establish whether elevated LRRC25 in Alzheimer's disease is pathogenic, protective, compensatory, or simply a marker of altered immune/autophagy status. Therapeutic direction must be treated with caution until functional AD-specific studies are completed.

LRRC25 is an emerging Alzheimer's disease–relevant gene most strongly linked to AD through microglial and myeloid immune regulation and autophagy-related protein turnover. Human microglia regulome genetics prioritized LRRC25 as a candidate AD risk gene, and expression studies report increased LRRC25 protein in AD mouse cortex and hippocampus, human AD brain, and AD patient iPSC-derived neurons. Additional genetic work places LRRC25 in an early-onset AD/lipid-overlap locus, though not as a confirmed causal gene.

Mechanistically, LRRC25 is best supported as an immune regulatory molecule rather than a direct amyloid-β or tau gene. In non-AD systems it has been shown to restrain NF-κB signaling by promoting autophagic degradation of p65/RelA through p62/SQSTM1, and to suppress RIG-I/type I interferon signaling by targeting ISG15-associated RIG-I for selective autophagy. These pathways are directly relevant to AD because chronic NF-κB activation, interferon signaling, defective autophagy, microglial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inflammasome activity are central features of neurodegeneration.

Therapeutic potential is credible but unvalidated. LRRC25 could plausibly be targeted to modulate neuroinflammation, microglial activation state, and autophagy-linked clearance pathways. However, no specific AD molecular partners or causal effects of LRRC25 have been established.

Key Insights

Genetic Prioritization in Microglia

Human microglia regulome genetics independently identify LRRC25 as a candidate AD risk gene, placing it in the disease-relevant cell type central to amyloid- and tau-associated neuroimmune responses.

Consistent Upregulation in AD Brain

LRRC25 protein is repeatedly elevated across APP/PS1 mouse cortex and hippocampus, 3xTg mouse cortex, human AD brain, and AD iPSC-derived neurons — yet whether this elevation is pathogenic, protective, or compensatory remains unresolved.

Dual Anti-Inflammatory Mechanism

In non-AD systems, LRRC25 acts as a negative regulator of both NF-κB signaling (via p62/SQSTM1-mediated p65/RelA degradation) and RIG-I/type I interferon signaling (via ISG15-RIG-I selective autophagy) — two pathways chronically dysregulated in neurodegeneration.

Myeloid & Inflammasome Connections

Cancer and immune studies link LRRC25 to myeloid/macrophage activation, ROS production, NLRP3 inflammasome activity, IL-1β signaling, and immune-cell recruitment — all directly relevant to AD microglial biology.

Rab10 Neuroresilience Indirect Link

Rab10 neuroresilience work found Lrrc25 downregulated in mice with reduced neuroinflammatory and aging pathway activation, indirectly supporting the interpretation that lower LRRC25 associates with more resilient brain states.

Genetic Locus: Suggestive but Unconfirmed

An early-onset AD/lipid-overlap locus harbors LRRC25, suggesting possible shared genetic architecture with lipid biology. However, later prioritization analyses did not nominate LRRC25 as a confirmed causal gene within that locus.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Treat LRRC25 as hypothesis-generating: Current evidence supports it as a credible but unvalidated AD target. Do not advance therapeutic programs without functional validation in AD-relevant systems.
  2. Prioritize cell-type-specific functional studies: Conduct experiments in microglia, neurons, and astrocytes to determine definitively whether LRRC25 elevation is pathogenic, protective, compensatory, or a passive disease-state marker.
  3. Test loss- and gain-of-function in AD models: LRRC25 knockdown, knockout, and overexpression should be evaluated for effects on amyloid burden, tau phosphorylation, synaptic loss, neurodegeneration, cognition, and microglial activation states.
  4. Map expression in human single-cell and spatial datasets: Focus especially on disease-associated microglia and vulnerable regions (cortex, hippocampus) using human AD single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics.
  5. Investigate key signaling interactions: Determine whether LRRC25 modulates NF-κB, type I interferon/RIG-I, IFN-γ/ISG15, ROS/NLRP3/IL-1β, and TGF-β-related pathways specifically in AD-relevant cell types.
  6. Clarify autophagy machinery interactions: Determine whether LRRC25 interacts with AD-relevant autophagy components including p62/SQSTM1, and whether it affects degradation of Aβ- or tau-linked proteins.
  7. Evaluate therapeutic direction bidirectionally: Inhibiting LRRC25 might enhance beneficial immune clearance in some contexts; enhancing it might suppress damaging NF-κB or interferon-driven inflammation in others. Both directions require careful evaluation.
  8. Consider biomarker potential: LRRC25 elevation may serve as a reproducible biomarker of myeloid/neuroimmune activation or autophagy dysfunction if validated in brain, CSF, or blood-derived immune cells.
  9. Aim for tuning, not blunt suppression: Any therapeutic strategy should seek to modulate LRRC25-dependent immune/autophagy signaling selectively rather than broadly suppress or activate the inflammatory cascade.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — LRRC25 in Alzheimer's Disease

LRRC25 represents a biologically plausible but pre-validated candidate gene in Alzheimer's disease, with its primary relevance rooted in microglial immune regulation and autophagy-dependent protein turnover. Genetic prioritization from human microglia regulome studies, combined with consistent protein upregulation across multiple AD mouse models, human AD brain tissue, and iPSC-derived neurons, establishes a reproducible disease-association signal. Mechanistically, LRRC25 functions as a dual negative regulator of NF-κB and RIG-I/type I interferon signaling through selective autophagic degradation — pathways chronically dysregulated in neurodegeneration. However, the critical interpretive gap remains: it is not yet known whether elevated LRRC25 in AD drives pathology, constitutes a protective response, or simply reflects altered immune and autophagy status. Broader myeloid biology literature linking LRRC25 to ROS production, NLRP3 inflammasome activity, IL-1β signaling, and immune-cell recruitment further underscores its relevance while highlighting the complexity of its role. LRRC25 merits systematic functional investigation in AD-relevant cell types before any therapeutic direction is committed to, and should simultaneously be evaluated as a potential biomarker of neuroimmune and autophagy dysfunction.
