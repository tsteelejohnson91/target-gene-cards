Overview

⚠ Therapeutic Targeting Caveat: Current evidence does not support direct inhibition, knockdown, or modification of NEFL/NfL as a treatment for Alzheimer's disease. Because NfL is a normal structural axonal protein, direct targeting carries biological risk unless a causal pathogenic mechanism is demonstrated. Therapeutic development should focus on upstream drivers of NfL elevation.

NEFL encodes neurofilament light chain (NfL), a structural axonal cytoskeletal protein that is released into cerebrospinal fluid and blood when axons are injured. In the reviewed literature, NEFL/NfL is consistently characterised as a sensitive but non-specific biomarker of neuroaxonal injury and neurodegeneration in Alzheimer's disease (AD) — not as a proven causal driver or validated therapeutic target.

Across AD and dementia-related studies, elevated plasma, serum, or CSF NfL is associated with cognitive decline, MCI-to-dementia progression, brain atrophy, hypometabolism, neuropsychiatric symptoms, vascular injury, inflammatory states, aging biology, and mixed neurodegenerative pathology. Its strongest current value lies in prognosis, staging, risk stratification, clinical-trial enrichment, and pharmacodynamic monitoring.

Mechanistically, NfL appears downstream of multiple AD-relevant processes — amyloid pathology, tau pathology, astroglial activation, inflammation, vascular/metabolic injury, blood–brain barrier dysfunction, sleep-related stress, traumatic brain injury, and biological aging. It is less AD-specific than p-tau217, p-tau181, Aβ42/40, or GFAP, and rises across many non-AD neurological conditions.

Key Insights

NfL as Axonal Injury Readout

Elevated NfL reflects neuroaxonal damage rather than amyloid plaques, tau tangles, or AD-specific molecular pathology. It is best interpreted as a downstream integrator of multiple injury mechanisms.

Tracks AD Progression & Cognitive Decline

Multiple cohorts link higher baseline NfL or rising NfL over time with worsening cognition, MCI-to-dementia progression, poorer MMSE/CDR outcomes, language and executive decline, and reduced likelihood of MCI reversion.

Often Downstream of Tau & Glial Changes

p-tau217/p-tau181 and GFAP may become abnormal earlier or show stronger AD-specific associations. NfL becomes more informative once neuronal injury is already underway, positioning it as a later-stage marker.

Adds Value in Multimarker Panels

NfL's best use is alongside p-tau217/p-tau181, Aβ42/40, GFAP, MRI/PET measures, APOE status, and vascular or inflammatory markers, contributing the neurodegeneration/axonal-injury dimension.

Not AD-Specific — Broad Confounding

NfL is elevated in ALS, FTD, MS, stroke, TBI, Parkinsonian disorders, neuropathies, delirium, epilepsy, HIV-related CNS injury, and others. This broad responsiveness severely limits its value as a standalone AD diagnostic marker.

Vascular & Systemic Confounders Critical

Age, kidney function (eGFR), BMI, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, small vessel disease, arterial stiffness, TBI, systemic inflammation, and comorbidities can elevate or modify blood NfL levels independently of AD pathology.

Strongest Role: Pharmacodynamic Biomarker

NfL can serve as a pharmacodynamic, prognostic, enrichment, or safety biomarker in AD trials. A therapy that genuinely reduces neurodegeneration may stabilise or lower NfL — but the therapeutic target lies upstream.

Direct Therapeutic Targeting Not Supported

Evidence does not support lowering, inhibiting, or editing NEFL/NfL to treat AD. As a normal structural axonal protein, direct targeting could be biologically harmful unless a causal pathogenic mechanism is proven.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Use NfL as a neurodegeneration marker, not an AD-specific diagnostic. Elevated NfL indicates active or cumulative axonal injury but should never be interpreted alone as evidence of AD pathology.
  2. Combine NfL with AD-specific biomarkers. For AD diagnosis and staging, pair NfL with p-tau217/p-tau181, Aβ42/40, GFAP, amyloid/tau imaging, MRI atrophy measures, and cognitive assessments.
  3. Apply NfL most powerfully in prognosis and monitoring. NfL is most useful for identifying patients with active neuroaxonal injury, estimating progression risk, monitoring MCI-to-dementia transition, and tracking neurodegenerative burden over time.
  4. Employ NfL in therapeutic trials as a response or safety readout. Stabilisation or reduction of NfL may indicate reduced downstream axonal injury after anti-amyloid, anti-tau, anti-inflammatory, vascular, metabolic, lifestyle, or neuroprotective interventions.
  5. Do not prioritise NEFL as a direct AD drug target. Evidence supports targeting the upstream drivers of NfL elevation — amyloid, tau, inflammatory, vascular, and metabolic mechanisms — rather than NEFL/NfL itself.
  6. Adjust interpretation rigorously for confounders. Age, renal function/eGFR, BMI, diabetes, vascular disease, systemic inflammation, TBI, peripheral neuropathy, assay platform, sample matrix, and pre-analytical handling must all be considered before attributing NfL elevation to AD.
  7. Consider NfL for trial enrichment. Patients with elevated or rising NfL may represent a subgroup with active neurodegeneration and higher likelihood of near-term decline, making them valuable candidates for neuroprotective or disease-modifying trial designs.
  8. Prioritise longitudinal change over single values. Rising NfL over time better reflects active neurodegeneration than a single baseline measurement, especially when adjusted for age and systemic factors.
  9. Treat NfL as a downstream integrative biomarker. Its value lies in capturing cumulative axonal damage produced by AD pathology interacting with inflammatory, vascular, metabolic, aging, traumatic, and comorbid injury pathways.
  10. Focus therapeutic development upstream of NfL. The most plausible AD strategies reduce amyloid/tau toxicity, glial and inflammatory injury, vascular/metabolic dysfunction, sleep or hypoxia-related stress, and synaptic degeneration — using NfL as evidence that neuroaxonal injury is being reduced.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — NEFL / NfL in Alzheimer's Disease NEFL/NfL stands as one of the most clinically informative neuroaxonal injury biomarkers available for Alzheimer's disease research, yet its utility is fundamentally that of a sensitive but non-specific readout rather than a disease-specific or therapeutically actionable target. It sits downstream of multiple converging pathologies — amyloid, tau, glial, vascular, inflammatory, metabolic, and aging processes — and its elevations faithfully signal that axonal injury is underway without pinpointing its cause. Its greatest proven value is in prognosis, disease monitoring, multimarker panel construction, and pharmacodynamic assessment in clinical trials. Direct therapeutic targeting of NEFL/NfL is not supported by the current evidence base and carries inherent biological risk. Investigators should deploy NfL as a downstream integrative biomarker while directing drug development upstream, toward the primary drivers of neurodegeneration, and should consistently adjust NfL interpretation for age, renal function, vascular burden, and comorbid conditions.
