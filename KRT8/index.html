Key Insights

🚫 No Direct AD Evidence

No supplied study demonstrates KRT8 expression changes in AD brain, genetic association with AD, interaction with amyloid-β or tau, effects on AD-relevant glia, or therapeutic benefit in any AD model. All AD relevance is inferred by biological analogy.

🔬 Stressed Epithelial Transitional States

Multiple lung injury and fibrosis studies describe KRT8/Krt8-high alveolar epithelial transitional cells arising after injury that persist during failed repair, fibrosis, senescence-like states, and EMT-like remodelling — making KRT8 a marker of cells that have exited homeostasis.

⚖ Context-Dependent Roles

KRT8 can be protective (stress tolerance, mitophagy, autophagy, apoptosis resistance) or pathogenic (fibrosis, chemoresistance, tumour migration, maladaptive remodelling) depending on tissue and disease context. Simple global activation or inhibition is not supported.

🧬 Proteostasis & Autophagy Links

KRT8 supports autophagosome–lysosome fusion, interacts with plectin/actin during autophagy, facilitates mitochondrial fission-mediated mitophagy under oxidative stress, and is regulated by ubiquitination, phosphorylation, methylation, and acetylation — all central to AD biology.

🔗 Inflammation Pathway Bridge

KRT8 is linked in non-AD systems to NF-κB, TRAF6/TLR signalling, IL-6, IL-1β/TNF contexts, p38/JNK/ERK MAPK, and STAT3. These pathways are core to AD neuroinflammation, but the supplied text does not show KRT8 regulating CNS inflammation in AD.

🏗 Vascular & Barrier Relevance (Unproven)

KRT8 is implicated in Moyamoya disease vascular remodelling and non-CNS barrier/tight-junction programmes. One epithelial repair study identified a KRT8/KRT18/Claudin-1/APP-containing network. These findings suggest possible but unconfirmed relevance to AD vascular and choroid plexus compartments.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — KRT8 in Alzheimer's Disease

Based exclusively on the supplied literature, KRT8 should not be treated as an Alzheimer's disease target. The evidence establishes KRT8 as a biologically rich epithelial intermediate-filament protein with documented roles in cytoskeletal integrity, autophagy, mitophagy, inflammatory signalling, barrier maintenance, and maladaptive repair — but exclusively in non-neurological contexts. The conceptual overlaps with AD-relevant mechanisms (proteostasis failure, neuroinflammation, vascular and barrier dysfunction) are real but indirect. The strongest AD-adjacent signals — amyloid-like aggregation, APP-network co-occurrence, and proteostasis regulation — are suggestive but unconfirmed in any AD model or human AD tissue. KRT8 is best positioned as a hypothesis-generating candidate warranting targeted exploratory investigation in AD-relevant datasets, particularly choroid plexus epithelium, vasculature, and barrier compartments, before any therapeutic prioritisation is considered. Its context-dependent, bidirectional biology makes directional drug development especially challenging without AD-specific functional data.
