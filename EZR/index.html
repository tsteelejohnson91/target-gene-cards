⚠ Interpretive Caution: EZR's role in Alzheimer's disease remains hypothesis-generating. Ezrin (EZR) is a membrane–actin cytoskeletal linker and signaling scaffold with broad roles in cytoskeletal organization, membrane signaling, adhesion, migration, immune regulation, extracellular vesicle biology, epithelial and barrier integrity, calcium signaling, and apoptosis. Its canonical signaling network spans EGFR/PI3K-AKT, PI3K-AKT-mTOR, Wnt/β-catenin, STAT3, NF-κB, MAPK/ERK, Ras/Rho GTPase, and focal adhesion pathways.

In Alzheimer's disease, EZR has been identified within computational biomarker or feature-gene sets in bioinformatics studies, linked to short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) metabolism, tryptophan metabolism, metabolism-defined AD subtypes, immune infiltration, and AD progression. These studies support EZR as a candidate AD-associated biomarker rather than a mechanistically validated causal driver. The supplied text does not establish that EZR directly regulates amyloid-β, tau, synaptic degeneration, cognition, or other validated AD pathological endpoints.

Across the broader literature, non-AD studies provide biological plausibility because EZR-regulated processes—neuroinflammation, gut–brain-axis signaling, cytoskeletal remodeling, glial activation, immune-cell trafficking, vesicle and endosomal biology, oxidative stress, cellular survival signaling, and barrier function—substantially overlap with major AD themes. However, this overlap is conceptual, not mechanistically demonstrated in AD-specific experimental systems.

Key Insights

Computational Biomarker, Not Causal Driver

AD-specific studies identify EZR within bioinformatics feature-gene sets linked to SCFA metabolism, tryptophan metabolism, and AD progression. One study nominated EZR alongside SNCA, GFAP, NFKBIA, and SST as five potential biomarkers with good diagnostic performance. EZR has not been mechanistically validated as a causal AD driver.

Metabolic–Immune Interface

AD studies linked EZR-containing gene sets to SCFA biology, tryptophan metabolism, immune infiltration signatures including M1 macrophages, activated dendritic cells, plasma cells, and resting NK cells, and metabolism-defined AD subtypes with altered immune profiles — placing EZR at a metabolic–immune interface potentially relevant to AD neuroinflammation.

Biologically Plausible for AD-Adjacent Processes

Ezrin's role as a membrane–actin scaffold and signaling organizer is compatible with AD-relevant cellular processes including synaptic structure, neurite remodeling, glial morphology, microglial motility, immune-cell trafficking, and membrane receptor organization. Non-AD studies found increased ezrin-labeled astrocytes in neural injury/seizure models and EZR involvement in distal axonal growth-regulatory networks.

Vesicle, Lysosomal & Phagocytic Biology

EZR was identified in extracellular vesicle contexts, as a V-ATPase-associated protein, and linked to LAMP-1/phagocytic vacuole biology in retinal pigment epithelium. These mechanisms overlap conceptually with AD endosomal-lysosomal dysfunction and microglial phagocytosis, though AD-specific validation is absent.

Oncology Is the Strongest Therapeutic Context

EZR knockdown or inhibition reduced proliferation, survival, migration, invasion, metastasis, colony formation, chemotherapy resistance, and tumor growth in multiple cancer models. NSC305787 is a pharmacological EZR inhibitor with effects on PI3K/AKT/mTOR. EZR also appears as a ROS1 fusion partner in targetable cancers — but this fusion biology does not imply native EZR is an AD target.

Therapeutic Directionality Uncertain; Inhibition Risks

In some pathological contexts, EZR suppression reduces harmful invasion or survival signaling. In others, EZR supports immune competence, epithelial integrity, membrane stability, neuritogenesis, and barrier-like functions. Broad systemic EZR inhibition could be harmful. Any AD-targeting strategy would require cell-type-specific, partial, or CNS-targeted approaches.
