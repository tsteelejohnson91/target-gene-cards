HCLS1 (also known as HS1) is a cortactin-related hematopoietic and immune-cell signaling adaptor involved in leukocyte activation, Src/Syk/Lyn-family kinase signaling, phosphorylation-dependent receptor responses, actin cytoskeletal remodeling, immune-cell migration, adhesion, chemotaxis, phagocytosis-related programs, macrophage/myeloid biology, inflammatory signaling, MAPK/ERK pathways, NF-κB-linked pathways, and apoptosis/survival regulation.

Its relevance to AD is indirect and conceptual. The strongest link is neuroinflammation: AD involves microglial activation, myeloid remodeling, cytokine signaling, complement/Fc receptor pathways, phagocytosis, synaptic pruning, vascular inflammation, blood-brain barrier dysfunction, and peripheral immune contributions. HCLS1's repeated association with immune-cell activation and cytoskeletal control makes it a plausible candidate for investigation in AD-related neuroimmune modules. However, the supplied evidence does not show that HCLS1 modifies amyloid-β deposition, tau phosphorylation or spread, synaptic degeneration, neuronal loss, microglial phenotype in AD models, or cognition.

The direct AD-focused evidence in the corpus concerns HAX1, a distinct HCLS1-associated protein (HCLS1-associated protein X-1), which appears in a cerebrospinal fluid proteomic panel for amyloid/tau PET stage stratification. HAX1 findings should not be attributed to HCLS1 itself.

Vascular & Stroke Associations May Be AD-Relevant

HCLS1 features in peripheral vascular atherosclerosis and ischemic stroke immune-response biomarker studies. Therapeutic Direction Unclear — Nomenclature Hazards

Higher HCLS1 can associate with immune activation, inflammatory infiltration, better immune competence, cancer prognosis, or disease activity depending on context — making it unclear whether inhibiting or enhancing it would benefit AD. Critically, HCLS1 ≠ HAX1 ≠ hCLS1: HAX1 carries direct AD CSF biomarker evidence; hCLS1 is a mitochondrial lipid enzyme. Conflating these would misrepresent the evidence substantially.
