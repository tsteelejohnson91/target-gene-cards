PLEK (pleckstrin) is not established by the available evidence as a validated Alzheimer's disease therapeutic target. Most cited studies do not directly examine PLEK in AD, amyloid-β, tau pathology, AD microglia, synaptic loss, or dementia. The Alzheimer's relevance is therefore largely indirect, based on PLEK's repeated appearance in immune, inflammatory, myeloid/macrophage, platelet/coagulation, vascular, cytoskeletal, and neuroinflammatory contexts that overlap with known AD mechanisms.

One AD-specific finding is described: in an APP/PS1 mouse model and Aβ1–42-induced neuronal pathology, miR-409-5p was downregulated early, Plek was validated as a miR-409-5p target, and Plek overexpression partially rescued neurite outgrowth impaired by miR-409-5p overexpression. This suggests a possible protective role for Plek in neurite/cytoskeletal resilience under AD-relevant stress, but it remains preliminary.

The strongest interpretation is that PLEK may be a candidate marker or modulator of immune-cell activation and cytoskeletal/vascular responses relevant to AD pathobiology, rather than a proven AD disease driver or drug target.

Mechanistic Overview

Key Insights

🔬 Direct AD Evidence — Limited but Notable

In APP/PS1 mice and Aβ1–42-exposed neurons, miR-409-5p is downregulated early. PLEK is a validated miR-409-5p target, and Plek overexpression partially rescues impaired neurite outgrowth — suggesting a possible compensatory, protective mechanism under AD-relevant stress.

🧬 Immune & Inflammatory Pathway Overlap

PLEK repeatedly appears in innate immune and inflammatory modules involving TNF, NF-κB, chemokines, NOD/inflammasome, IL-17, TLR, MAPK, PI3K-Akt, and cytokine signaling — all of which strongly overlap with AD neuroinflammation mechanisms.

🧫 Myeloid / Macrophage Co-occurrence

PLEK frequently co-occurs with AD-relevant immune genes including TYROBP, ITGAM, ITGB2, LCP2, CD68, CD14, CSF1R, TLR2, CD33, CR1, C3AR1, FCER1G, and PTPRC — supporting investigation in microglia and infiltrating monocyte/macrophage states in AD brain.

🩸 Vascular, Platelet & Coagulation Links

PLEK is associated with vascular inflammation, atherosclerotic plaque instability, venous thromboembolism, platelet activation, coagulation, and actin cytoskeleton regulation — processes that may contribute to vascular cognitive impairment and mixed AD/vascular dementia.

🧠 CNS Relevance Beyond AD

PLEK appears in cuprizone-induced demyelination/cognitive impairment, multiple sclerosis genetics/proteomics, frontotemporal dementia, stroke aging responses, and microglia-related striatal changes after cocaine exposure — supporting CNS immune relevance but not AD causality.

⚖ Directionality Is Unresolved

In some contexts elevated PLEK may reflect harmful inflammation; in others it appears associated with protective immune function or neurite support. Simple inhibition or activation of PLEK in AD cannot be justified from the current evidence base.

  2. Prioritize expression validation in AD datasets. Assess PLEK across AD brain tissue, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, microglia, astrocytes, neurons, endothelial cells, blood, CSF, plasma, saliva, and platelets to establish a robust expression map.
  3. Correlate PLEK with AD-relevant outcomes. Test whether PLEK expression associates with amyloid burden, tau pathology, synaptic loss, neuroinflammation markers, vascular pathology, BBB dysfunction, cognitive decline, and inflammatory comorbidities such as periodontitis, sleep apnea, diabetes, and atherosclerosis.
  4. Functionally characterize the miR-409-5p–Plek axis in AD models. Determine whether increasing Plek improves neurite integrity, synaptic function, neuronal survival, and cognition under Aβ/tau stress — this is the most mechanistically tractable AD-specific hypothesis available.
  5. Investigate PLEK in microglia/macrophage systems. Determine whether PLEK activity promotes protective phagocytosis and tissue repair or maladaptive cytokine production and neurotoxicity, using primary AD-relevant immune cell models.
  6. Consider PLEK as a biomarker candidate first. Evaluate PLEK as a marker of AD inflammatory subtypes, vascular-inflammatory burden, platelet activation, or peripheral immune dysregulation before pursuing it as a direct drug target.
  7. Resolve therapeutic direction and safety risks before development. Determine whether PLEK should be enhanced, inhibited, or contextually modulated, and formally evaluate safety risks related to immune competence, platelet function, coagulation, and vascular inflammation.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment —

PLEK/pleckstrin is an exploratory, not validated, Alzheimer's disease candidate. Its strongest evidence base lies in immune, inflammatory, myeloid, vascular, and cytoskeletal biology that overlaps indirectly with known AD mechanisms. The single direct AD finding — a miR-409-5p–Plek axis in which Plek overexpression partially rescues Aβ-impaired neurite outgrowth in APP/PS1 and Aβ1–42 models — is preliminary but mechanistically tractable and warrants follow-up. PLEK's co-occurrence with a broad panel of AD-relevant myeloid genes (TYROBP, CSF1R, CD33, CR1 and others) positions it as a potential modulator of microglial and peripheral immune states in AD. Before any therapeutic development, the fundamental question of whether PLEK activity should be elevated or suppressed in AD must be resolved, and safety implications for platelet function, coagulation, and systemic immune competence must be systematically evaluated. In the near term, PLEK is best pursued as a pharmacodynamic or inflammatory-subtype biomarker rather than as an immediate drug target.
