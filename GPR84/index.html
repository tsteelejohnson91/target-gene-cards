Overview

⚠ Therapeutic Caution: GPR84's effects are context-dependent. Inhibition may reduce maladaptive neuroinflammation, but broad chronic antagonism risks impairing protective microglial plaque containment and debris clearance. Neither pure inhibition nor activation constitutes a validated strategy.

GPR84 is a medium-chain fatty-acid/metabolite-sensing Class A GPCR enriched in innate immune cells — particularly microglia, macrophages, monocytes, and neutrophils. Its relevance to Alzheimer's disease centers on neuroinflammation, myeloid-cell activation, lipid and metabolite sensing, microglial recruitment and phagocytosis, oxidative stress, inflammasome signaling, and gut–immune–brain communication.

The strongest AD-specific evidence is mixed but important. In 5xFAD mice, GPR84 PET imaging with [¹⁸F]MGX-110S detected activated microglia/macrophages in AD-relevant brain regions and outperformed TSPO imaging. In APP/PS1 mice, GPR84 deficiency worsened cognitive decline and dendritic degeneration while reducing plaque-associated microglia, suggesting GPR84 supports protective microglial recruitment around amyloid plaques. In tauopathy models, Gpr84 was upregulated with fibrillar P301L tau as part of a microglial inflammatory activation signature. A 2024 AD multi-omics study nominated GPR84 as a potential gut microbial metabolite–GPCR drug target, identifying triacanthine as a GPR84 activator.

Overall, GPR84 should be viewed as a plausible AD neuroimmune biomarker and therapeutic target, but not yet a clinically validated disease-modifying target. Its biology is context-dependent: inhibition may reduce damaging inflammation, while activation or biased modulation may preserve beneficial microglial recruitment, phagocytosis, or tissue-protective functions.

Key Insights

Myeloid-Cell Marker & AD Neuroinflammation

GPR84 is repeatedly upregulated in inflammatory states and marks activated microglia and macrophages, making it directly relevant to the neuroinflammatory component of Alzheimer's disease. Its enrichment in innate immune cell populations underlies its emerging role as both a biomarker and drug target.

PET Imaging: Most Advanced AD Application

[¹⁸F]MGX-110S detected neuroinflammation across cortex, hippocampus, and thalamus in 5xFAD mice and outperformed TSPO tracers in myeloid-cell specificity, positioning GPR84 PET as a promising tool for disease staging, patient stratification, and treatment monitoring.

Protective Role in Amyloid Pathology

In APP/PS1 mice, GPR84 loss reduced plaque-associated microglia and worsened dendritic degeneration and cognition without altering plaque load, implying GPR84 supports protective microglial containment of amyloid rather than amyloid clearance per se.

Pro-inflammatory Amplifier & NLRP3 Activation

GPR84 can amplify TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, NF-κB/p65, ERK/JNK/p38 MAPK, STAT3, NLRP3 inflammasome, cGAS-STING-linked pyroptosis, ROS generation, and DAMP release — pathways that directly overlap with and may accelerate AD-relevant neurodegeneration.

Gut–Microbiome–Brain Axis

Aging-associated MCFA-producing bacteria (Parabacteroides goldsteinii) drove peripheral myeloid inflammation via GPR84, impaired vagal signaling, reduced hippocampal activation, and caused memory deficits in aged mice. GPR84 inhibition improved memory in this gut–brain context.

Therapeutic Direction Remains Unresolved

Antagonism may suppress maladaptive inflammation, inflammasome activity, and oxidative injury, while agonism or biased modulation may enhance beneficial microglial recruitment and phagocytosis. The most promising AD strategy may be biased ligands that selectively engage protective over cytotoxic pathways.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Prioritize GPR84 as an AD neuroinflammation biomarker. Advance GPR84 PET tracers such as [¹⁸F]MGX-110S for studies of microglial/macrophage activation, disease staging, patient stratification, and treatment monitoring.
  2. Validate GPR84 expression in human AD brain. Use single-cell, spatial transcriptomic, proteomic, and imaging approaches to determine whether GPR84 localizes to plaque-associated microglia, tau-associated microglia, or infiltrating myeloid cells, and whether it tracks amyloid burden, tau stage, or cognition.
  3. Test both activation and inhibition in AD models. Because APP/PS1 data suggest a protective GPR84 function while inflammatory models support antagonism, directly compare GPR84 agonists, antagonists, inverse agonists, and biased modulators in both amyloid and tau preclinical models.
  4. Measure functional outcomes beyond inflammation. Studies should assess cognition, dendritic integrity, synapse preservation, plaque-associated microglial coverage, amyloid/tau pathology, cytokines, inflammasome activation, ROS, phagocytosis, and neuronal survival simultaneously.
  5. Develop brain-penetrant and microglia-relevant modulators. Therapeutic candidates must demonstrate CNS exposure, receptor engagement, selectivity over related fatty-acid receptors, and clear signaling profiles in human microglia-like cells.
  6. Explore biased signaling as a preferred strategy. The most attractive AD approach may be ligands that enhance beneficial microglial recruitment or phagocytosis while avoiding excessive TNF/IL-1β/NLRP3/ROS-driven neurotoxicity.
  7. Investigate gut–GPR84–brain mechanisms in AD. Test whether microbiome-derived medium-chain fatty acids or other GPR84 ligands drive peripheral myeloid inflammation, vagal dysfunction, hippocampal impairment, and AD vulnerability in aging-relevant models.
  8. Use caution with simple therapeutic assumptions. GPR84 is not uniformly harmful or protective; effects vary by ligand, cell type, disease stage, inflammatory context, and signaling bias. Broad chronic inhibition could reduce harmful inflammation but might impair protective microglial plaque containment or debris clearance.
  9. Treat GPR84 as promising but exploratory for AD. Current evidence supports strong biomarker potential and a biologically plausible therapeutic rationale, but direct human AD efficacy and disease-modifying effects remain unproven.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — GPR84 in Alzheimer's Disease GPR84 is a plausible and biologically compelling AD neuroimmune target whose case rests on its enrichment in activated myeloid cells, responsiveness to lipid metabolites including gut-microbiome-derived MCFAs, and its documented roles in microglial recruitment, phagocytosis, inflammasome activation, oxidative signaling, and plaque-associated microglial containment in preclinical AD models. Its strongest near-term translational asset is GPR84 PET imaging, which has already demonstrated superior myeloid-cell specificity over TSPO tracers in 5xFAD mice. However, the therapeutic direction remains genuinely unresolved: APP/PS1 model data suggest GPR84 loss is harmful, supporting agonism or neutral modulation, while inflammatory and gut–brain models support antagonism. This duality means that biased signaling — selectively engaging protective microglial functions while limiting cytokine and inflammasome-driven neurotoxicity — is the most scientifically defensible therapeutic hypothesis. GPR84 should be advanced as a high-priority biomarker target and a cautiously explored therapeutic target, with the explicit recognition that its context-dependence demands mechanistically rigorous, function-first studies before clinical translation.
