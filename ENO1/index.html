Overview

⚠ Therapeutic Safety Caveat
Because ENO1 is a core glycolytic enzyme broadly expressed in metabolically vulnerable brain cells, broad systemic inhibition risks worsening AD-related energy failure. Any therapeutic approach must selectively modulate disease-relevant ENO1 functions rather than employing indiscriminate catalytic blockade.

ENO1/α-enolase is a multifunctional glycolytic and "moonlighting" protein with emerging relevance to Alzheimer's disease (AD), operating across brain energy metabolism, amyloid-β (Aβ) handling, mitochondrial dysfunction, proteostasis stress, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, lactate/lactylation signaling, vascular dysfunction, and biomarker biology.

The strongest AD/CAA-specific evidence identifies ENO1 as upregulated in AD brain proteomics and demonstrates that ENO1 can directly interact with Aβ. In vitro, ENO1 inhibited Aβ fibril formation, disrupted preformed fibrils, reduced fibril cytotoxicity in cerebrovascular smooth muscle cells, and promoted proteolytic Aβ degradation. In APP23 AD/cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) mice, intracerebral ENO1 infusion reduced cerebrovascular Aβ deposits and improved cognitive impairment, positioning ENO1 as a candidate therapeutic molecule for AD-related CAA.

Network-level transcriptomic analysis of multi-region AD brain tissue identified ENO1 as one of four "elite" therapeutic target nodes — alongside COX5B, HSP90AB1, and SDHB — linking glycolysis to mitochondrial bioenergetics and protein homeostasis. Docking analyses suggested favorable interactions of genistein and resveratrol with ENO1 and these related nodes. Additionally, an ENO1-MCUR1 chimeric RNA was detected in CSF from AD cases and absent from healthy samples, while ENO1 appeared in neuronal-enriched extracellular vesicle protein signatures overlapping with Long COVID neurological complaints, suggesting biofluid biomarker potential pending further validation.

Overall, ENO1 is best interpreted as an AD-relevant convergence-node candidate rather than a fully validated drug target, with its strongest direct therapeutic rationale rooted in AD/CAA via anti-Aβ fibril and Aβ-degrading activity.

Key Insights

Direct Anti-Aβ Activity in CAA

ENO1 binds Aβ, inhibits fibril formation, disrupts existing fibrils, reduces fibril toxicity, promotes Aβ degradation, lowers cerebrovascular Aβ burden in APP23 mice, and improves cognition after brain infusion — the strongest direct therapeutic rationale for ENO1 in AD/CAA.

Enzymatic Activity Is Required

Enzymatically inactive ENO1 failed to inhibit Aβ fibrillization or disrupt preformed fibrils, establishing that active ENO1 function — not passive Aβ binding alone — underlies anti-amyloid effects. This has critical implications for therapeutic design.

AD Mitochondrial-Proteostasis Network Node

Multi-region AD brain transcriptomics co-prioritized ENO1 with COX5B, SDHB, and HSP90AB1, linking glycolysis to mitochondrial bioenergetics and protein homeostasis — matching core AD mechanisms of hypometabolism, mitochondrial failure, oxidative stress, and protein aggregation.

Biofluid Biomarker Candidate

An ENO1-MCUR1 chimeric RNA was detected in CSF from AD cases but absent from healthy samples. ENO1 protein also appeared in AD-associated neuronal-enriched extracellular vesicles, though specificity and causality remain unproven and require independent validation.

Lactate / Lactylation Epigenetic Link

ENO1 influences lactate generation, histone lactylation, transcriptional reprogramming, mitochondrial injury, ROS production, and ferroptosis-like damage — mechanisms directly overlapping with AD neurodegeneration and connecting glycolytic flux to inflammatory and epigenetic regulation.

Safety Risk: Broad Inhibition Contraindicated

Because ENO1 is a core glycolytic enzyme broadly expressed in energy-vulnerable brain cells, systemic inhibition risks worsening AD-related energy failure. Therapeutic strategies must selectively modulate disease-relevant ENO1 functions — enhancing Aβ-clearing activity or targeting pathological surface interactions — rather than suppressing glycolytic catalysis globally.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Prioritize CAA/AD validation of anti-Aβ activity. Replicate whether ENO1 reduces soluble Aβ oligomers, fibrils, plaques, and vascular amyloid — and downstream neurovascular injury — across multiple AD and CAA preclinical models.
  2. Define cell-type-specific ENO1 roles. Measure ENO1 expression, localization, enzymatic activity, and post-translational modifications in neurons, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocytes, vascular smooth muscle cells, endothelial cells, and pericytes across AD stages and brain regions.
  3. Disentangle protective from harmful ENO1 functions. Test whether ENO1 supports neuronal ATP buffering and Aβ clearance while potentially promoting maladaptive glycolysis, lactate/lactylation, or inflammatory activation in glia or vascular immune compartments.
  4. Validate ENO1 as a biofluid biomarker. Assess CSF ENO1-MCUR1 chimeric RNA, neuronal-enriched EV ENO1 protein, and post-translational modification signatures in independent AD, CAA, MCI, vascular dementia, and control cohorts for specificity and diagnostic utility.
  5. Explore therapeutic strategies beyond broad inhibition. Candidate approaches include enhancing ENO1's Aβ-degrading/anti-fibril function, mimicking its Aβ-interacting activity, targeting pathological surface ENO1 interactions, modulating ENO1-linked lactylation, or employing multi-target compounds such as genistein/resveratrol-like modulators.
  6. Assess safety rigorously. Any ENO1-directed therapy must avoid impairing essential neuronal and astrocytic glycolysis. Brain-region-, cell-type-, compartment-specific, or partial-pathway modulation is likely safer than systemic catalytic blockade.
  7. Test against major AD endpoints. Future studies should determine whether ENO1 modulation affects amyloid burden, tau phosphorylation/aggregation, synaptic function, neuronal survival, glial activation, BBB integrity, mitochondrial function, oxidative stress, ferroptosis-like injury, cognition, and CAA-related hemorrhagic risk.
  8. Treat ENO1 as a convergence-node target, not a single-axis driver. The most defensible therapeutic rationale is that ENO1 intersects with multiple AD-relevant disease axes — Aβ aggregation/clearance, glucose hypometabolism, mitochondrial-proteostasis dysfunction, neuroinflammation, lactate/lactylation signaling, vascular amyloid injury, and extracellular protease biology — rather than causing AD through any one pathway alone.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — ENO1 / α-Enolase in Alzheimer's Disease

ENO1 emerges from the current evidence as a multifunctional, AD-relevant convergence node rather than a fully validated single-target drug candidate. Its most compelling direct therapeutic rationale resides in AD-related cerebral amyloid angiopathy, where ENO1 demonstrably binds Aβ, inhibits fibril formation, disrupts existing aggregates, promotes proteolytic Aβ degradation, reduces cerebrovascular amyloid burden in APP23 mice, and restores cognitive function — effects requiring intact enzymatic activity. Beyond CAA, ENO1 is embedded in a mitochondrial bioenergetics and proteostasis network co-disrupted in AD, connects glycolytic flux to histone lactylation and epigenetic-inflammatory reprogramming, and shows early signal as a biofluid biomarker via CSF chimeric RNA and neuronal extracellular vesicles. However, because ENO1 is a core glycolytic enzyme indispensable for brain energy metabolism, broad inhibitory approaches carry a significant safety risk of exacerbating the very metabolic vulnerability that defines AD. Therapeutic development must therefore target the disease-specific gain-of-function or loss-of-clearance aspects of ENO1 biology — most plausibly by enhancing its Aβ-clearing or anti-fibril activity, leveraging multi-target polyphenolic modulators, or precisely correcting pathological surface-ENO1 interactions — while carefully preserving essential neuronal and astrocytic glycolysis.
