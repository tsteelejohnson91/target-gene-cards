Overview

⚠ Safety / Speculative Target Caveat ANKRD31's established biology centres on meiotic DNA-break regulation and reproductive integrity. Systemic modulation could pose fertility and genome-stability risks. The AD association is genetic and associative only — causality has not been demonstrated.

ANKRD31 has limited direct evidence linking it to Alzheimer's disease (AD). The primary AD-relevant finding is the association of variant ANKRD31-rs4704171 as a novel genetic signal in the GR@ACE genome-wide association study, identified through meta-analysis of dementia/AD cohorts stratified by clinical endophenotypes and vascular burden. This positions ANKRD31 as a possible AD susceptibility locus related to vascular-endophenotype biology, but does not establish it as a causal AD gene.

Most functional evidence describes ANKRD31 as a meiotic DNA double-strand break (DSB) and recombination regulator. It acts as a scaffold or chromosome-axis-associated factor, interacting with REC114 and other DSB-promoting proteins to control the number, timing, and chromosomal location of meiotic breaks. These findings support roles in germline genome integrity, chromosome segregation, fertility, and reproductive development — not established AD neurobiology.

A separate study noted ANKRD31 solely as a locus annotated to a HIF-2α chromatin-binding region in BEAS-2B bronchial epithelial cells, where reporter activity depended on the HIF-2α N-terminal activation domain. This is indirect, non-neurological evidence and does not address AD, neurodegeneration, amyloid-β, tau, or neuroinflammation.

Key Insights

Genetic Association — Not Causality

ANKRD31-rs4704171 was identified as a novel AD signal in the GR@ACE GWAS through meta-analysis of cohorts stratified by vascular burden and clinical endophenotypes. GWAS association alone does not establish ANKRD31 as a causal AD gene, a disease-mechanism actor, or a druggable target.

Established Meiotic Biology

ANKRD31 is best supported as a meiotic recombination and genome-integrity factor. It interacts directly with REC114, supports clustering of DSB machinery on chromosome axes, and is critical for pseudoautosomal-region (PAR) recombination in male meiosis. This is its primary, well-evidenced function.

No Established AD Mechanism Link

The supplied evidence does not demonstrate that ANKRD31 affects amyloid precursor protein processing, amyloid-β accumulation, tau phosphorylation or aggregation, synaptic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, blood–brain barrier integrity, or cognitive decline.

Reproductive & Genome-Stability Risk

Loss or disruption of ANKRD31 causes delayed recombination initiation, unrepaired DNA breaks, meiotic arrest, infertility, reduced oocyte reserves, premature ovarian insufficiency, and embryo aneuploidy. Systemic therapeutic modulation therefore carries plausible fertility and genome-stability liabilities.

Indirect HIF-2α Regulatory Evidence

One study reported an ANKRD31-annotated regulatory locus responsive to non-canonical HIF-2α transcriptional activity in BEAS-2B bronchial epithelial cells. This is indirect and non-neurological; it provides no evidence of AD relevance and was not conducted in brain or vascular cells relevant to neurodegeneration.

Vascular Endophenotype as Clue

The AD association emerged in cohorts stratified by vascular burden, suggesting that if ANKRD31 has any AD-relevant role, it may relate to vascular biology rather than classical amyloid or tau pathology. Fine-mapping and eQTL analyses in vascular and brain cell types are needed to evaluate this hypothesis.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — ANKRD31 in Alzheimer's Disease

ANKRD31 occupies a speculative position as an AD-relevant gene. Its sole AD evidence is a GWAS signal (rs4704171) arising from vascular-endophenotype-stratified meta-analysis — an associative finding that does not confer causal status, identify a disease mechanism, or support druggability. The gene's established and well-evidenced biology lies entirely in meiotic DNA double-strand break regulation and reproductive integrity, through its scaffold interactions with REC114 and its control of DSB timing, number, and chromosomal distribution. No published evidence connects ANKRD31 to the core molecular hallmarks of AD — amyloid-β, tau, neuroinflammation, or synaptic loss. Before ANKRD31 can be considered a legitimate AD target, the field requires fine-mapping of its GWAS locus, eQTL and chromatin-interaction analysis in disease-relevant cell types, independent replication of the association, and experimental demonstration that ANKRD31 modulation influences AD-related phenotypes. Any therapeutic exploration must also confront the significant safety liability posed by its reproductive and genome-stability functions. At present, ANKRD31 warrants careful hypothesis-generating investigation, not therapeutic prioritization.
