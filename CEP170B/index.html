Overview

⚠ Epistemic Caveat
CEP170B has not been directly validated as an Alzheimer's disease gene or therapeutic target. Its relevance to Alzheimer's pathology is inferential, resting entirely on mechanistic overlap with known AD processes rather than disease-specific experimental evidence.

CEP170B is a microtubule minus-end regulator that localizes to cortical microtubule-stabilizing complexes through its interactions with liprin-α1 and PP2A. It antagonizes CAMSAP-mediated microtubule stabilization and cooperates with KIF2A to promote microtubule minus-end depolymerization. These functions are mechanistically adjacent to Alzheimer's disease biology — a disease characterized by tau dysfunction, microtubule destabilization, impaired axonal and dendritic transport, and synaptic failure.

CEP170B has appeared in epilepsy and glioma-related epilepsy transcriptomic signatures, within gene sets enriched for synaptic signaling and calcium ion pathways. In temporal lobe epilepsy, it is inferred as a hub gene in a miR-484-associated mRNA network linked to neurotransmitter synapse pathways. However, no study has demonstrated CEP170B alteration in Alzheimer's brains, interaction with tau, effects on amyloid-β processing, neurodegeneration, or cognition.
