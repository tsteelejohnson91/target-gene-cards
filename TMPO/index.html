Nuclear Lamina: Strongest Mechanistic Bridge

AD and brain aging involve heterochromatin disruption, transcriptional dysregulation, DNA damage accumulation, and impaired neuronal stress responses. TMPO/LAP2's role in lamin A/C-associated nuclear organization makes it a plausible modifier of neuronal vulnerability — but this is unproven.

Cell-Cycle Dysregulation: Recurring Theme

TMPO and TMPO-AS1 are repeatedly linked to E2F/Rb signaling, mitotic genes, and cell-cycle progression in cancer systems. Aberrant neuronal cell-cycle re-entry is proposed in AD, but no supplied evidence demonstrates TMPO/TMPO-AS1 driving this in neurons.

TMPO-AS1 Ferroptosis & Redox Link

TMPO-AS1 has been linked to ferroptosis resistance, lipid ROS, intracellular iron, and malondialdehyde in cancer contexts. Because AD involves iron dyshomeostasis and lipid peroxidation, this represents a credible but entirely unexplored research direction in neurons.

TMPO-AS1 Signaling Pathway Overlap

Reported TMPO-AS1-linked pathways — PI3K/Akt/mTOR, Wnt/β-catenin, TGF-β/Smad, HIF-1α, E2F, ferroptosis, and inflammatory signaling — are all relevant to AD. However, the evidence comes from cancer, wound healing, periodontitis, and ischemic injury, not AD contexts.

Therapeutic Directionality Is Unknown

In cancer, inhibiting TMPO-AS1 or TMPO suppresses tumor growth. In AD, broad inhibition could be harmful if TMPO/LAP2 supports neuronal nuclear integrity, chromatin stability, DNA repair, or stress survival. Cell-type- and disease-stage-specific validation is essential before any therapeutic claim.
