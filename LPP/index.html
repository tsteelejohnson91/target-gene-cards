Across all supplied records, there is no definitive experimental proof that modulating any "LPP" entity treats Alzheimer's disease, reduces amyloid-β or tau pathology, prevents neurodegeneration, or improves cognition in Alzheimer's patients.

🧬 LPP as a Genetic Modifier (TREM2 Rescue)

Lipoma Preferred Partner and ZNF510 were identified as candidate synthetic-rescue genes for TREM2, with interactions associated with delayed Alzheimer's onset and better cognitive outcomes — suggesting a cognitive resilience mechanism linked to glial lipid/sterol metabolism. LPP remains a statistical candidate, not a validated target.

🔬 Lateral Perforant Path & Synaptic Plasticity

The lateral perforant path is Alzheimer's-adjacent as a central component of entorhinal-hippocampal episodic memory circuitry. Studies reveal LPP-dentate gyrus plasticity, cholinergic theta modulation, microglial endocannabinoid (2-AG) signalling, and rescue of LTP by MGL inhibition — overlapping with memory failure, microglial dysfunction, and synaptic loss themes in AD.

🧫 Lysosomal pH-mod LPPs — Plausible But Unproven

Acidic liposome-based particles restore lysosomal pH and improve proteolytic clearance of protein aggregates. This is mechanistically relevant to Alzheimer's proteinopathy (impaired Aβ/tau clearance), but no Alzheimer's animal-model or clinical efficacy data are provided in the supplied evidence.

📡 Late Positive Potential — Biomarker, Not Target

The LPP (Late Positive Potential) reflects sustained attention, emotional salience, evaluative and memory-related processing. It is a candidate noninvasive EEG/ERP readout of cognitive-affective processing in Alzheimer's research, but it is not a molecular therapeutic target.
