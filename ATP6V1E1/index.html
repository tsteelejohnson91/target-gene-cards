Overview

⚠ Key Caveat
ATP6V1E1 is a core, ubiquitous subunit of the V-ATPase proton pump essential for broad cellular physiology. Nonspecific activation or inhibition could disrupt lysosomal function, vesicular trafficking, pH homeostasis, glycosylation, metabolism, immune function, and organ physiology system-wide. Therapeutic strategies must target deficient cell types specifically.

ATP6V1E1 encodes the E1 subunit of the V1 cytoplasmic domain of the vacuolar H⁺-ATPase (V-ATPase), a proton pump required for acidification of lysosomes, endosomes, autophagosomes/autolysosomes, secretory vesicles, and related intracellular compartments. In Alzheimer's disease (AD), this function is highly relevant because lysosomal acidification and autophagic clearance are central to degradation of amyloid-β (Aβ), tau species, damaged mitochondria, protein aggregates, and inflammatory cargo.

Across the supplied literature, ATP6V1E1 repeatedly appears as a lysosome/autophagy-related and mitochondrial-lysosomal dysfunction marker. The most consistent AD-associated pattern is reduced ATP6V1E1 expression in AD-relevant tissues and models — including hippocampus, brain tissue, blood, and Aβ-stressed neuronal cells. Several studies also identify ATP6V1E1 as a potential diagnostic biomarker in both brain and blood datasets, with reported AUC values generally above 0.7 and up to 0.869 in external validation.

Mechanistically, reduced ATP6V1E1 may impair V-ATPase assembly or activity, causing insufficient lysosomal acidification. This could reduce Aβ and tau degradation, disrupt autophagic flux, worsen mitochondrial stress, impair mitophagy, promote proteotoxic accumulation, and amplify neuroinflammation. Single-cell and immune-infiltration analyses further connect ATP6V1E1 to microglia and T-cell-related immune signatures, suggesting relevance beyond neurons to AD-associated immune remodeling.

As a therapeutic target, ATP6V1E1 is promising but not yet validated. Current evidence is strongest at the biomarker and pathway-inference level. No supplied AD study directly demonstrates that therapeutic modulation of ATP6V1E1 improves amyloid, tau, synaptic, inflammatory, or cognitive outcomes. ATP6V1E1 should therefore be treated as a candidate therapeutic node within the broader V-ATPase/lysosome/autophagy/mitochondrial axis.

Key Insights

Consistent Downregulation in AD

ATP6V1E1 is repeatedly downregulated across AD-relevant contexts — hippocampus, brain tissue, blood, AD mouse models, and Aβ25-35-treated SH-SY5Y neuronal cells — establishing a robust expression signature across both central and peripheral compartments.

Diagnostic Biomarker Potential

Multiple transcriptomic studies identify ATP6V1E1 as a biomarker candidate integrating brain and blood datasets. Reported AUC values are generally above 0.7, with one autophagy-focused early AD study reporting external-validation AUCs of 0.869, 0.817, and 0.714.

Mitochondrial-Lysosomal Axis

ATP6V1E1 and NDUFB5 were co-identified as oxidative phosphorylation-related AD biomarkers, both downregulated in the AD hippocampus. This places ATP6V1E1 within a coordinated mitochondrial-lysosomal dysfunction framework rather than as an isolated lysosomal gene.

Microglial & Immune Enrichment

Single-cell RNA-seq shows ATP6V1E1 is predominantly expressed in microglia. Reduced microglial ATP6V1E1 may impair Aβ and debris clearance, shift microglia toward maladaptive inflammatory states, and correlate with altered effector memory CD4⁺ T-cell and NKT-cell signatures.

PTM as Mechanistic Lever

A 2026 tuberculosis study demonstrated that phosphorylation at ATP6V1E1 Tyr56/57 directly suppresses V-ATPase assembly and lysosomal acidification. Though not AD-specific, this supports the concept that restoring ATP6V1E1 function via PTM modulation could rescue lysosomal pH in disease.

Safety & Target Specificity Risk

As a ubiquitous core V-ATPase subunit, broad systemic manipulation of ATP6V1E1 risks disrupting lysosomal function, vesicular trafficking, pH homeostasis, glycosylation, metabolism, and immune cell function across all tissues. Cell-type-specific and deficiency-restoring strategies are essential.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Prioritize as a biomarker candidate: ATP6V1E1 should be advanced for validation in combined brain-blood diagnostic panels and early-stage AD cohorts, given consistent downregulation and AUC values reaching 0.869.
  2. Test causality in functional models: Manipulate ATP6V1E1 in AD-relevant neuronal and microglial models and measure lysosomal pH, autophagic flux, Aβ clearance, tau turnover, mitochondrial function, inflammatory state, and cell viability to determine whether downregulation is causal or merely correlative.
  3. Investigate microglia-specific function: Microglial lysosomal acidification is critical for Aβ and debris clearance; single-cell data indicate ATP6V1E1 enrichment in microglia, making this cell type the primary focus for mechanistic and therapeutic experiments.
  4. Evaluate within the mitochondrial-lysosomal axis: Assess ATP6V1E1 jointly with oxidative phosphorylation defects such as NDUFB5 downregulation and complex I dysfunction to understand coordinated bioenergetic and autophagic failure.
  5. Examine PTM-based suppression in AD: Investigate whether inhibitory phosphorylation events analogous to Tyr56/57 regulation occur in AD brain tissue or models, and evaluate whether blocking such phosphorylation restores V-ATPase assembly and lysosomal acidification.
  6. Explore ceRNA network modulation: Consider upstream regulatory strategies targeting the proposed lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA ceRNA network involving XIST, NEAT1, KCNQ1OT1, HCG18, hsa-let-7c-5p, hsa-miR-107, hsa-miR-129-2-3p, and hsa-miR-214-3p to restore ATP6V1E1 expression early in disease.
  7. Avoid broad systemic V-ATPase manipulation: Any therapeutic strategy must aim for context-specific restoration of deficient lysosomal acidification in vulnerable cell types — microglia and neurons — to prevent adverse disruption of essential physiology across other organs and tissues.
  8. Require direct AD-model validation as next step: No current AD study demonstrates that ATP6V1E1 modulation improves amyloid burden, tau pathology, synaptic function, neuroinflammation, neuronal survival, or cognition. This is the critical gap that must be addressed before advancing to translational development.

Overall Assessment

Target Enabling Package · Synthesis

Overall Assessment — ATP6V1E1 represents a biologically plausible and consistently observed candidate within the lysosomal-autophagic-mitochondrial axis of Alzheimer's disease. Its repeated downregulation across hippocampus, blood, mouse models, and Aβ-stressed neurons, together with diagnostic AUC values reaching 0.869 and strong mechanistic rationale connecting V-ATPase dysfunction to Aβ/tau accumulation, autophagic failure, and microglial immune dysregulation, makes it a credible priority for further investigation. However, the evidence base remains at the level of transcriptomic association and pathway inference. No AD study has yet demonstrated that restoring ATP6V1E1 function improves core pathological or cognitive endpoints. The ubiquitous nature of V-ATPase biology imposes genuine therapeutic specificity constraints. The most productive near-term strategy is to use ATP6V1E1 as a validated biomarker and mechanistic readout within the broader lysosomal biogenesis, autophagy, mitochondrial quality-control, and neuroinflammation programs, while pursuing microglia-focused functional studies and PTM-targeted restoration strategies to build the causal evidence base required for drug development.
