Overview

⚠ Clinical Caveat Pharmacogenomic evidence links SLC22A23 variation to quetiapine-induced QTc prolongation, indicating a potential adverse-event risk when antipsychotics are used in dementia patients. This is unrelated to disease modification but has direct implications for medication safety monitoring.

SLC22A23 is best characterized as a candidate Alzheimer's disease risk modifier, not an established causal gene or validated therapeutic target. The strongest AD-specific evidence derives from a 2025 multi-ancestry genome-wide interaction study reporting an African-ancestry-specific SNP-by-smoking interaction in or near SLC22A23, suggesting that this gene's variation may influence AD risk specifically in the context of cigarette smoking, rather than acting as a broad AD-risk locus across populations.

Biologically, SLC22A23 is an orphan member of the SLC22 solute carrier / organic ion transporter family. Related transporters move drugs, xenobiotics, toxins, endogenous metabolites, nutrients, antioxidants, signaling molecules, and microbiome-derived products — but SLC22A23's own substrate and transport function remain unknown. Animal studies demonstrate broad expression including brain tissue, brain capillaries, and cultured neurons.

Its relevance to AD mechanisms is therefore indirect but biologically plausible: smoking-related toxic exposure, neuroinflammation, vascular and metabolic stress, altered metabolite transport, blood–brain barrier handling, and hippocampal vulnerability could all intersect with SLC22A23 biology. Current evidence, however, is insufficient to support direct therapeutic inhibition or activation.

Smoking Interaction Signal, Not Classic AD Risk
The principal AD-specific finding is an African-ancestry SNP-by-smoking interaction identified in a 2025 multi-ancestry GWIS — not a conventional genome-wide AD risk association. Smoking appears to be an effect modifier that unmasks genetic risk otherwise invisible in unstratified analyses.
Ancestry-Specific Signal Requiring Diverse Replication
The SLC22A23 signal emerged exclusively in African-ancestry participants, emphasizing the critical need for ancestry-aware, diversity-inclusive validation. Key details — the exact SNP, effect size, risk direction, and causal variant — were not provided in available source text.
Orphan Transporter with Brain-Relevant Expression
SLC22A23 is a functionally uncharacterized member of the SLC22 family. Rat studies demonstrate expression in brain, liver, brain capillaries, and neurons, supporting plausible roles in brain exposure and neurovascular metabolite or xenobiotic handling, but the endogenous substrate remains unknown.
Knockout Model Links Gene to Hippocampus & Cognition
A Slc22a23 knockout rat exhibited reduced hippocampal volume and impaired novel object recognition, directly connecting altered gene dosage to brain structure and cognition. The model also showed leanness, increased locomotion, and improved endurance — pointing to a metabolic-neurocognitive axis.
Broad Systemic Associations Inform AD Biology
Non-AD studies link SLC22A23 to inflammatory bowel disease, ulcerative colitis, endometriosis-related phenotypes, tissue differentiation, aging, feeding behavior, and liver methylation. These overlap with neuroinflammation, systemic metabolism, and vascular dysfunction — all central to AD pathophysiology.
Pharmacogenomic Safety — QTc Prolongation Risk
SLC22A23 variation has been linked to quetiapine-induced QTc prolongation, raising a clinically relevant medication safety concern in dementia care settings where antipsychotics are commonly prescribed. This is a pharmacogenomic rather than a disease-modification signal.

Overall Assessment — SLC22A23 in Alzheimer's Disease

SLC22A23 currently occupies the role of a hypothesis-generating, context-dependent AD risk modifier rather than a validated causal gene or druggable target. Its most compelling evidence — a 2025 SNP-by-smoking interaction in African-ancestry individuals — positions it as a gene whose disease relevance is conditional on environmental exposure, making ancestry-aware and exposure-stratified follow-up studies essential before any therapeutic inference is warranted. The orphan transporter status (unknown substrate, unknown precise function) is the central scientific gap: without knowing what SLC22A23 moves, mechanistic hypotheses about AD biology remain speculative, however biologically plausible they may be. The Slc22a23 knockout rat model, which shows reduced hippocampal volume and impaired cognition alongside a metabolic phenotype, offers the most direct functional anchor and should guide experimental prioritization. Near-term clinical relevance may be greatest not in disease modification but in pharmacogenomic safety monitoring — specifically antipsychotic-related QTc risk in dementia patients. In sum, SLC22A23 warrants continued mechanistic investment, particularly substrate identification and ancestry-diverse genetic validation, but should not be elevated to a primary therapeutic priority until its biology is substantially better defined.
