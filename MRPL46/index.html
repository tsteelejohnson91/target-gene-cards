⚠ Evidence Caveat

MRPL46 has not been directly studied in Alzheimer's disease cohorts, amyloid-β or tau models, AD GWAS, or AD-focused therapeutic programmes. All relevance described here is mechanistic and hypothesis-generating only.

MRPL46 is a mitochondrial ribosomal protein whose primary established role lies in mitoribosome structure and function, mitochondrial protein synthesis, and—by extension—oxidative phosphorylation, respiratory electron transport, and ATP production. Its indirect relevance to Alzheimer's disease arises from the substantial overlap between these mitochondrial processes and core AD pathophysiology: impaired energy metabolism, respiratory-chain dysfunction, elevated reactive oxygen species burden, and neuronal metabolic vulnerability.

The strongest disease-specific evidence comes from an ischemic stroke study in which MRPL46 was selected by machine learning as one of four mitochondrial dysfunction feature genes. Neurologically adjacent signals appear in schizophrenia-associated cognitive impairment (immune-metabolic correlation) and major depressive disorder (epigenetic methylation association). None of these findings constitute direct AD evidence, but collectively they position MRPL46 as a plausible exploratory candidate in vascular cognitive impairment and mixed dementia contexts where mitochondrial and hypometabolic mechanisms intersect with Alzheimer's pathology.

Key Insights

Mitochondrial Biology, Not AD-Specific

MRPL46's strongest disease-relevant link is mitochondrial dysfunction broadly. It has not been directly studied in Alzheimer's cohorts, amyloid-β or tau models, or AD GWAS — all AD relevance is mechanistic inference.

Mitoribosome Structure & OXPHOS

As a mitochondrial ribosomal protein, MRPL46 is essential for mitoribosome integrity. Mitoribosomes translate the OXPHOS subunits encoded by the mitochondrial genome, so MRPL46 disruption could theoretically impair respiratory-chain function.

Ischemic Stroke ML Signal

MRPL46 was selected as one of four mitochondrial dysfunction feature genes in a machine-learning ischemic stroke study, alongside MCL1, MTX3, and RNASEH1. Its associated gene set was enriched for ATP synthesis coupled electron transport, ROS signalling, and OXPHOS.

Neurological Adjacency — Not AD Proof

MRPL46 appears in schizophrenia-associated cognitive impairment (negative correlation with NK cell infiltration) and major depressive disorder (methylation at cg00200755 associated with disease risk). These are correlative, disease-specific findings, not AD evidence.

Therapeutic Risk: Essential & Nonredundant

Mitochondrial ribosomal proteins are described as essential and nonredundant. Direct MRPL46 inhibition would likely be broadly harmful. No current evidence supports an MRPL46-directed Alzheimer's therapy.

Pathway Overlap as Entry Point

AD brains commonly show impaired OXPHOS, reduced ATP, and elevated ROS — exactly the pathways linked to MRPL46 biology. This positions MRPL46 as an exploratory candidate in vascular cognitive impairment and mixed dementia, where hypometabolic and mitochondrial mechanisms intersect.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — MRPL46 in Alzheimer's Disease

MRPL46 is a biologically plausible but evidentially immature candidate for Alzheimer's disease research. Its core function in mitoribosome assembly and mitochondrial protein synthesis places it upstream of oxidative phosphorylation, ATP production, and reactive oxygen species signalling — all processes disrupted in AD brain. However, the current evidence base contains no direct Alzheimer's data: no AD cohort studies, no amyloid or tau model findings, and no GWAS signal. The most substantive disease evidence comes from an ischemic stroke machine-learning study, with additional neurological adjacency from schizophrenia cognition and MDD methylation research. As a therapeutic target, MRPL46 presents a clear safety concern: its essential, nonredundant nature makes direct inhibition inadvisable. The appropriate near-term posture is exploratory biomarker investigation, particularly within AD subgroups defined by mitochondrial dysfunction, vascular injury, or hypometabolism, with therapeutic ambitions directed toward the broader pathway rather than MRPL46 itself.
