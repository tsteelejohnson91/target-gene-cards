FARSA encodes the alpha catalytic subunit of cytosolic phenylalanyl-tRNA synthetase (PheRS), an essential enzyme complex that charges tRNAPhe with phenylalanine for protein synthesis. Its primary role is canonical aminoacylation, but evidence from model organisms points to additional non-canonical, moonlighting functions in cell growth and proliferation that are independent of the beta subunit and of detectable translation increases.

Pathogenic FARSA/FARS1 variants reduce PheRS aminoacylation activity or destabilize the complex, causing multisystem pediatric disease: brain abnormalities, calcifications/cysts, hypotonia, growth delay, and developmental phenotypes. This is the best-supported disease mechanism in the literature.

⚠ Inflammatory Pathway Engagement

Pediatric FARSA deficiency was associated with autoimmunity, systemic inflammation, and elevated interferon scores. JAK inhibitor treatment improved lung disease in one patient, demonstrating that FARSA dysfunction can engage IFN/JAK-associated inflammatory pathways — relevant to neurodegeneration in principle.
