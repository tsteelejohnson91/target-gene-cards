Overview

Caution — Limited Direct Evidence: CDC42SE2 has not been directly validated as an AD causal gene or established therapeutic target. Its AD relevance is inferred from miRNA–mRNA enrichment and biomarker prioritization rather than direct experiments demonstrating effects on amyloid-β, tau, synaptic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, neuronal loss, or cognition.

CDC42SE2 is presented as a limited but potentially relevant Alzheimer's disease (AD) signal, principally through extracellular-vesicle (EV) miRNA biology and circulating biomarker discovery. The strongest AD-specific evidence derives from Vogrinc et al., 2021, where the hsa-miR-107–CDC42SE2 interaction was one of four miRNA–target pairs shared between EV-associated miRNAs and AD-associated miRNAs.

Beyond the core AD context, non-AD evidence situates Cdc42se2 within miRNA-regulated neural injury-response networks (including a peripheral nerve injury study involving miR-551b) and epigenetic regulation contexts (asthma-related DNA methylation studies). Cancer studies report CDC42SE2 as a fusion partner or susceptibility-region gene, while drug-response and stem-cell studies place it in broader treatment-response RNA contexts — none of which establish direct relevance to native CDC42SE2 function in AD.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — CDC42SE2 in Alzheimer's Disease

CDC42SE2 represents a weak but not dismissible AD signal, anchored by a single miRNA–target enrichment study (Vogrinc et al., 2021) identifying the hsa-miR-107–CDC42SE2 interaction as shared between EV-associated and AD-associated miRNA networks. The additional co-targeting of IL6 by miR-107 and non-AD evidence from neural injury contexts modestly support a biological plausibility for CDC42SE2 in neurological stress responses. However, the evidence base is entirely inferential — no direct experiments link CDC42SE2 to amyloid-β, tau, synaptic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, or cognition, and neither its expression direction nor its protein function in AD have been characterised. CDC42SE2 is therefore best positioned as a biomarker-network candidate suitable for EV-based diagnostic exploration and patient stratification, with therapeutic development firmly contingent on functional validation yet to be performed.
