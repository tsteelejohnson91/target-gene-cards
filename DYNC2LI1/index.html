DYNC2LI1 encodes a dynein-2 light-intermediate-chain subunit that is essential for retrograde intraflagellar transport (IFT) within primary cilia. It operates as part of the dynein-2 motor complex through direct interactions with DYNC2H1 and functional coupling with WDR60, IFT54, and IFT57.

Pathogenic DYNC2LI1 variants impair dynein-2/IFT function, disrupt ciliary trafficking, alter the ciliary handling of Hedgehog-related GPCRs such as Smoothened and GPR161, disturb GLI3 processing and signaling balance, and are causally linked to skeletal ciliopathies including short-rib thoracic dysplasia 15, Jeune-like disease, and Ellis–van Creveld syndrome.

As it pertains to Alzheimer's disease, DYNC2LI1 should be viewed only as an indirect, hypothesis-generating candidate through broader mechanisms involving primary cilia biology, ciliary GPCR trafficking, intracellular transport, and signaling pathways that may intersect with neuronal or glial homeostasis.
