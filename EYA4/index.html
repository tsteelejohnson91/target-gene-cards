EYA4 & Alzheimer's Disease

Target Gene Card — AI-Assisted Evidence Summary

Overview

Safety Caution — EYA4 loss or variation is repeatedly linked to progressive sensorineural hearing loss (DFNA10), age-related hearing loss, dilated cardiomyopathy, and cardiac conduction abnormalities. Nonspecific systemic modulation of EYA4 carries plausible auditory and cardiac risks that must be evaluated before therapeutic development proceeds.

EYA4 is not established in the supplied evidence as a causal Alzheimer's disease gene or validated AD therapeutic target. Its most direct AD-relevant evidence comes from two distinct lines: association with a resilient excitatory layer 4 neuronal population in neocortex, and a genome-wide significant association of an EYA4-region variant with higher tau PET burden.

In the neuronal-resilience study, EYA4 is part of a RORB/CUX2/EYA4 molecular signature marking layer 4 excitatory neurons in primary visual cortex — a region affected relatively late in AD. These neurons appear relatively resistant to AD-associated neurodegeneration. EYA4 is best interpreted in that dataset as a marker of a resilient neuronal subtype, not as an experimentally validated driver of that resilience.

In the tau-imaging genetics study, variant rs150532 in the EYA4 locus was associated with higher brain tau deposition measured by 18F-flortaucipir PET in non-demented European individuals. Minor allele carriers showed higher tau PET signal, with concordant directions in plasma and CSF phosphorylated-tau measures. The causal gene and mechanism remain unresolved.

Broader EYA4 biology — spanning transcriptional co-regulation, atypical phosphatase activity, DNA damage repair, innate immune signaling, Wnt/β-catenin and GPCR signaling, cellular senescence, sensory-neural maintenance, hearing loss, cardiomyopathy, and cancer pathways — provides plausible but indirect conceptual overlap with AD mechanisms. Most supporting data derive from cancer, hearing-loss, immune, cardiac, or developmental study systems rather than AD models or AD brain tissue.

Key Insights

Neuronal Resilience Marker

EYA4 is part of the RORB/CUX2/EYA4 signature of layer 4 excitatory neurons in primary visual cortex — a neocortical region spared relatively late in AD. These neurons appear resistant to AD-associated neurodegeneration, but EYA4's role as driver versus marker of that resilience is unproven.

Tau-PET Genetic Association

Variant rs150532 in the EYA4 locus associates genome-wide significantly with higher 18F-flortaucipir tau PET burden, with concordant effects on plasma and CSF phospho-tau. The causal gene — EYA4, VNN2, or a linked regulatory element — and the underlying mechanism remain unresolved.

Phosphatase Biology & Tau Plausibility

EYA4's atypical phosphatase activity makes mechanistic involvement in tau phosphorylation conceptually plausible, since abnormal phosphorylation is central to tau pathology. However, no direct dephosphorylation of tau, regulation of MAPT, or effect of EYA4 manipulation on tau aggregation is documented in the source text.

Senescence & Aging Intersection

EYA4 expression increases during aging, and EYA4 knockdown delayed both replicative and stress-induced cellular senescence. EYA4 interacts with SIX2 to enhance p21 transcription via a p53-dependent pathway that is independent of EYA4 phosphatase activity, suggesting a non-enzymatic transcriptional role in cellular aging.

Hearing Loss & Cardiac Safety Risks

EYA4 is repeatedly implicated in autosomal dominant sensorineural hearing loss (DFNA10), age-related hearing loss, dilated cardiomyopathy, and cardiac conduction abnormalities. These associations represent a major safety concern for any systemic EYA4 modulation strategy in AD drug development.

KCNIP4 Clarification & Probe Caveat

KCNIP4, not EYA4, was the gene functionally tested in the resilience study; KCNIP4 overexpression reduced Arc and c-Fos in an AD mouse model, suggesting anti-hyperexcitability compensation. Separately, the "EYA-4" label in the text refers to a chemical amyloid imaging probe — not EYA4 gene or protein function — and must not be conflated with EYA4 biology.

Functional experiments should use human iPSC-derived neurons, astrocytes, microglia, organoids, and AD mouse models, with readouts including tau phosphorylation/aggregation, synaptic integrity, neuronal survival, calcium dynamics, excitability, γH2AX/DNA damage markers, inflammatory cytokines, and cognitive or circuit phenotypes.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — EYA4 in Alzheimer's Disease

EYA4's strongest AD relevance is twofold: it is part of a neuronal resilience signature (RORB/CUX2/EYA4) marking layer 4 excitatory neurons that appear relatively spared in AD, and it maps to a tau-associated genetic locus (rs150532) linked to elevated tau PET burden and concordant phospho-tau biomarkers. Neither line of evidence establishes EYA4 as a causal AD gene or validated therapeutic target. Broader EYA4 biology — encompassing atypical phosphatase activity, DNA damage repair, the SIX2–p21–p53 senescence axis, and MAVS/STING innate immune signaling — offers plausible mechanistic overlap with AD pathophysiology, but this evidence derives predominantly from cancer, hearing-loss, cardiac, and developmental contexts rather than AD brain. Critical uncertainties include whether EYA4 drives or merely marks neuronal resilience, whether EYA4 itself regulates tau, and whether its therapeutic direction should favor activation or inhibition in AD-relevant cell types. Safety concerns are substantial: EYA4 variation is repeatedly linked to progressive sensorineural hearing loss and dilated cardiomyopathy, making nonspecific systemic modulation inadvisable without dedicated safety evaluation. EYA4 merits further investigation as a hypothesis-generating candidate, but causal validation in AD-relevant models is an essential prerequisite before any therapeutic intervention strategy is considered.
