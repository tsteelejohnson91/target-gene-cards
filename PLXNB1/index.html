PLXNB1/Plexin-B1 is a semaphorin guidance receptor, most often linked to SEMA4D/CD100, with increasingly direct relevance to Alzheimer's disease (AD). Across the literature, PLXNB1 is implicated in AD through plaque-associated glial architecture, pathological astrocyte-state regulation, amyloid and tau pathology, synaptic and neuritic degeneration, neuroinflammation, neurovascular dysfunction, and cytoskeletal signaling.

The strongest AD-specific evidence identifies PLXNB1 as a plaque-associated astrocyte-upregulated and late-onset AD network hub gene that regulates peri-plaque "cell distancing" in glial nets. In an AD mouse model, Plexin-B1 deletion altered glial organization around amyloid plaques, increased plaque coverage by glial processes, reduced neuroinflammatory transcriptional programs, lowered plaque burden, promoted compact dense-core plaque morphology, and reduced neuritic dystrophy.

A second major line of evidence identifies PLXNB1 as a top receptor regulating the disease-associated astrocyte state Ast10, linked to aging-related cognitive decline and synaptic loss. Genetic ablation in murine and human iPSC-derived astrocytes reduced the Ast10 signature, supporting causality. Human cortical proteomics additionally links higher PLXNB1 protein abundance to greater β-amyloid load and higher PHFtau tangle density.

Overall, PLXNB1 functions in AD-relevant contexts as a regulator of glial positioning, astrocyte state transitions, inflammatory signaling, plaque compaction, synaptic integrity, neuritic injury, and possibly vascular/immune crosstalk. Its therapeutic potential is compelling but early-stage and context-dependent.

Key Insights

Peri-Plaque Glial Architecture Regulator

Reactive astrocytes use PLXNB1-dependent signaling to control spacing among glial cells around amyloid plaques — a "cell distancing" mechanism that appears to restrict glial and microglial engagement with plaques and is disrupted upon Plexin-B1 deletion.

Beneficial Phenotype Upon PLXNB1 Deletion in AD Models

Plexin-B1 knockout in AD mice produced reduced reactive astrocytes/microglia in peri-plaque nets, increased glial process coverage of plaques, lower plaque burden, more compact dense-core plaques, reduced neuroinflammatory signatures, and decreased neuritic dystrophy.

Driver of the Ast10 Pathological Astrocyte State

Single-nucleus RNA-seq meta-analysis identified PLXNB1 as a top receptor regulating Ast10 — a disease-associated astrocyte subset linked to aging-related cognitive decline and synaptic loss. Functional ablation in both mouse and human iPSC-derived astrocytes reduced the Ast10 signature, indicating causality over correlation.

Human Proteomics: Linked to Core AD Pathological Axis

Higher cortical PLXNB1 protein abundance associates with greater β-amyloid load and higher PHFtau tangle density in human brain. Tau pathology additionally induces glial Plxnb1/Plxnb2, worsened by SORLA deletion and suppressed by SORLA upregulation, placing PLXNB1 near the core AD pathological cascade.

Broad Signaling Intersection with AD Mechanisms

SEMA4D–PLXNB1 activates RhoA/ROCK, R-Ras/PI3K/Akt, GSK-3β, CRMP2, MAPK/ERK, MET/ErbB crosstalk, and integrin regulation — pathways directly overlapping with AD mechanisms of neurite retraction, dendritic spine loss, tau-related cytoskeletal disruption, synaptic failure, and glial motility.

Context-Dependent Protective Role — Therapeutic Caution

While PLXNB1 loss is beneficial in AD plaque and astrocyte models, astrocyte Plexin-B1 supports glial border formation, inflammation containment, axon regeneration, and functional recovery in spinal cord injury. This context-dependence means broad systemic blockade carries real risk; cell-type- and disease-stage-specific modulation is essential.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — PLXNB1 / Plexin-B1 in Alzheimer's Disease

PLXNB1 has emerged as a multifunctional glial regulator deeply embedded in the AD pathological cascade, operating well beyond its classical role as a semaphorin axon-guidance receptor. The convergence of evidence — from AD mouse model genetics, human single-nucleus transcriptomics, cortical proteomics, and tau/SORLA biology — positions PLXNB1 as a regulator of peri-plaque glial architecture, a driver of the Ast10 pathological astrocyte state, and an amplifier of neuroinflammatory and cytoskeletal signaling programs linked to synaptic loss and cognitive decline. Its druggability is supported by multiple modality classes including antagonistic antibodies, allosteric peptides, and downstream pathway inhibitors. However, the critical caveat is context-dependence: PLXNB1 is protective in spinal cord injury and potentially in other CNS repair contexts, meaning any therapeutic strategy must achieve cell-type- and disease-stage-selective modulation rather than broad systemic blockade. Overall, PLXNB1 merits prioritization as a mechanistically plausible, causally supported, and druggable glial target for AD, with the next essential step being demonstration that selective inhibition improves both pathology and cognition in human-relevant models without impairing protective glial biology.
