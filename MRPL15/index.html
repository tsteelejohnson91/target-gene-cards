Overview

Caution — Bidirectional Biology: MRPL15 shows disease-context-dependent directionality: it is downregulated in AD brain regions but upregulated and pathogenic in fluoride neurotoxicity models, and often upregulated in cancer. This argues strongly against simple activation or inhibition strategies and requires careful experimental design.

MRPL15 encodes mitochondrial ribosomal protein L15, a component of the mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit. Because mitochondrial ribosomes synthesize mitochondrially encoded oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) proteins, MRPL15 dysregulation plausibly affects mitochondrial translation, respiratory-chain activity, ATP production, oxidative stress, and neuronal vulnerability — making it a mechanistically relevant candidate in neurodegeneration.

MRPL15's relevance to Alzheimer's disease (AD) is primarily established through bioinformatics studies. A 2025 Journal of Molecular Neuroscience study incorporated MRPL15 into a LASSO-based AD risk prediction model alongside RBP4, ABCA1, MPV17, and MRPL37, achieving AUC >0.815 in validation cohorts. A 2021 Aging study linked MRPL15 to AD diagnosis, methylation-related regulation, and immune inflammation. A 2022 Scientific Reports study identified MRPL15 as one of five overlapping hub genes between musculoskeletal aging and AD, suggesting a broader systemic mitochondrial aging signature. MRPL15 is not a classical amyloid-β or tau gene; its AD relevance is strongest as a mechanistic biomarker and pathway node.

Key Insights

Core Mechanistic Role

MRPL15 is mainly implicated in AD through mitochondrial dysfunction. Its downregulation in AD brain regions suggests impaired mitochondrial translation and oxidative phosphorylation, rather than direct involvement in amyloid or tau pathways.

Biomarker Candidate — Risk Model

The 2025 AD risk-model study (MRPL15 + RBP4 + ABCA1 + MPV17 + MRPL37 + clinical factors) achieved AUC >0.815 in validation cohorts, supporting MRPL15 as a predictive biomarker within an integrated gene signature.

Immune–Mitochondrial Interface

The 2025 study found altered immune cell proportions — including gamma delta T cells and activated CD4 memory T cells — correlating with mitochondrial energy metabolism genes, placing MRPL15 at a neuro-immune–metabolic interface in AD.

Epigenetic & Inflammatory Links

The 2021 Aging study connects MRPL15 to methylation-related regulation and immune inflammation (upregulated macrophages and neutrophils in AD), suggesting its expression may be controlled by disease-associated epigenetic mechanisms.

Systemic Aging Signature

MRPL15 is one of five overlapping hub genes between musculoskeletal aging and AD (2022 Scientific Reports), indicating a possible muscle–brain systemic mitochondrial aging connection beyond CNS-intrinsic disease.

Context-Dependent Directionality — Key Caveat

MRPL15 is downregulated in AD but upregulated and pathogenic in fluoride neurotoxicity (activating NLRP3, pyroptosis) and often upregulated in cancer. Simple activation or inhibition strategies are not appropriate; normalization of mitoribosomal function is the rational goal.

Actionable Takeaways

Key questions include whether MRPL15 expression changes in specific brain regions or cell types, whether it correlates with amyloid/tau burden, synaptic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, mitochondrial respiration, or cognitive decline, and whether restoring MRPL15-associated mitochondrial function improves outcomes.

  6. Investigate epigenetic regulation of MRPL15 in AD. Methylation-related findings suggest that MRPL15 expression may be controlled by disease-associated epigenetic mechanisms that could be measurable or pharmacologically modifiable.
  7. Consider peripheral aging contexts. MRPL15's overlap between musculoskeletal aging and AD suggests utility in studying systemic mitochondrial aging and possible muscle–brain interactions in neurodegeneration research.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — MRPL15 in Alzheimer's Disease

MRPL15 is best characterized as a promising AD-relevant mitochondrial ribosomal biomarker and mechanistic hypothesis-generating node. Its downregulation in AD brain regions, consistent placement in bioinformatics-derived risk models, and connections to mitochondrial translation, oxidative phosphorylation, immune-inflammatory dysregulation, epigenetic control, and systemic aging collectively build a plausible mechanistic framework. The multi-gene risk model achieving AUC >0.815 provides the most concrete evidence for clinical biomarker utility. However, direct experimental evidence that manipulating MRPL15 modifies amyloid pathology, tau pathology, synaptic loss, or cognitive decline in AD models is absent. Its therapeutic potential lies in restoring or preserving mitochondrial translation fidelity and immune–metabolic homeostasis, but MRPL15 remains an unvalidated direct Alzheimer's disease drug target requiring substantial further experimental work before any therapeutic program can be initiated.
