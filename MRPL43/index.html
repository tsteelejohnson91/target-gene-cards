Overview

Important Caveat: MRPL43 is a candidate susceptibility gene for Alzheimer's disease, not an established therapeutic target. All AD-relevant evidence is genetic and associative. No study has demonstrated that MRPL43 manipulation alters amyloid-β, tau, synaptic function, neuronal survival, or cognition in any AD model.

MRPL43 encodes a mitochondrial ribosomal protein with a role in mitochondrial translation and mitoribosome integrity. Its strongest link to Alzheimer's disease (AD) comes from a 2023 splicing transcriptome-wide association study (spTWAS) identifying it among 15 novel genes harboring likely causal splicing introns associated with AD risk. Its relevance to AD mechanisms is therefore primarily indirect, centering on genetically regulated RNA splicing, mitochondrial ribosome biology, and complex post-transcriptional control.

Because mitochondrial dysfunction is a hallmark of neurodegeneration and AD, altered MRPL43 splicing or protein abundance could plausibly impair mitochondrial protein synthesis, respiratory chain function, energy metabolism, or cellular stress responses in vulnerable brain cells. However, the cited AD evidence establishes genetic association only — not direct proof of causality.

Key Insights

Splicing-Based AD Association

The most direct AD link is genetic: MRPL43 was identified in a large AD spTWAS as one of 15 novel genes with likely causal splicing introns associated with AD susceptibility — a specific, splice-level signal rather than a bulk expression change.

Mitochondrial Biological Plausibility

As a mitochondrial ribosomal protein, MRPL43 supports a possible connection to impaired mitochondrial translation and energy metabolism in AD, given that mitochondrial dysfunction is a major and well-established feature of neurodegeneration.

Discordant cis-eQTL Regulation

A cis-eQTL signal affected mRNA expression, ribosome occupancy, and protein abundance in opposite directions, demonstrating that transcript levels alone do not reflect functional MRPL43 protein levels — a critical caveat for any expression-based study.

Complex Multi-Level Regulatory Control

Variants near MRPL43 may influence transcription factor binding, splice donor usage, and microRNA binding, suggesting disease risk could operate through altered isoform usage, RNA processing, translation efficiency, or protein stability — not a single mechanism.

TWINKLE Genomic Neighborhood

MRPL43 is genomically linked to TWINKLE (a mitochondrial DNA helicase) via a head-to-head gene arrangement and shared bidirectional promoter. This places MRPL43 within a mitochondrial regulatory neighborhood relevant to AD but complicates causal attribution.

No Functional AD Validation

No supplied study demonstrates that MRPL43 manipulation changes amyloid-β, tau, synaptic function, neuronal survival, microglial activation, mitochondrial respiration, or cognition in any AD model. Non-AD mechanistic findings (inflammation, apoptosis) should not be directly extrapolated to AD.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — MRPL43 in Alzheimer's Disease

MRPL43 is best characterized as a candidate AD susceptibility gene at the intersection of splicing biology and mitochondrial function, not an established therapeutic target. Its strongest evidence — a spTWAS-derived causal splicing intron — is genetically compelling but requires functional corroboration. The discordant cis-eQTL architecture (mRNA up, protein down) and multi-level regulatory complexity make simple expression-based inference unreliable. Its genomic proximity to TWINKLE deepens biological plausibility but also complicates causal interpretation. Broader neurodegenerative relevance is supported by a rare DLB variant association, and non-AD studies hint at roles in inflammation, apoptosis, and microRNA regulation — but none of these findings have been validated in AD-specific models. The immediate research priority is functional validation: confirming the splicing signal in human AD brain, measuring protein-level consequences, and testing mitochondrial phenotypes in neural cell models, before any therapeutic development is considered.
