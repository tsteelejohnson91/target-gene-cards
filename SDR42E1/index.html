SDR42E1 is a short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase-family enzyme involved in vitamin D, sterol, cholesterol, and steroid metabolism. It has not been directly studied in Alzheimer's disease. Its mechanistic relevance rests on the biological overlap between its enzymatic substrates — vitamin D precursors and sterols — and processes repeatedly implicated in Alzheimer's pathophysiology, including neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, calcium regulation, blood–brain barrier integrity, amyloid processing, APOE-linked lipid metabolism, synaptic function, and neurosteroid signaling.

Functional experiments in HaCaT keratinocytes showed that SDR42E1 depletion disrupted steroid biosynthesis, caused 7-dehydrocholesterol accumulation, and reduced vitamin D3 production. SDR42E1 deficiency in HCT116 colorectal cells dysregulated sterol absorption and reduced cell viability, with overexpression rescuing viability and restoring ABCC2 expression. As a therapeutic target in Alzheimer's disease, SDR42E1 remains entirely preliminary.

Computational studies predicted SDR42E1 binding to vitamin D3, 8-dehydrocholesterol, 7-dehydrocholesterol, and 25-hydroxyvitamin D. SDR42E1 depletion in HaCaT keratinocytes reduced vitamin D3 production and accumulated 7-DHC, supporting a mechanistic role in vitamin D biosynthesis.

🧫 Sterol Biosynthesis Dysregulation

✅ Rescue by Overexpression

Transient SDR42E1 overexpression in deficient HCT116 cells rescued the viability defect and restored ABCC2 expression, suggesting that at least some SDR42E1-deficiency phenotypes are pharmacologically reversible. This provides a proof-of-concept for function restoration, though AD-specific rescue is unproven.

⚠ Human Mutation — Cholesterol & Endocrine

A homozygous human SDR42E1 missense variant (p.Arg154Gln) was associated with low cholesterol and endocrine/connective-tissue abnormalities, confirming an in vivo role in cholesterol and steroid hormone regulation.
