NKAIN2 is a neurobiologically plausible but unvalidated Alzheimer's disease (AD) candidate. The supplied evidence links NKAIN2 most directly to AD through suggestive genetic associations with AD-related cognitive and neurodegeneration endophenotypes, rather than through demonstrated causal mechanisms. The strongest AD-relevant signal derives from an ADNI-based GWAS in which NKAIN2 was reported as a suggestive locus associated with ADAS13, an Alzheimer's cognitive outcome. A second ADNI study listed SNPs within NKAIN2 among candidate variants for rate of ventricular enlargement in nondemented elders — an imaging marker relevant to neurodegenerative progression.

Mechanistically, NKAIN2 encodes Na⁺/K⁺ Transporting ATPase Interacting 2, suggesting a relationship with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase biology — a system supporting neuronal ion gradients, membrane potential, synaptic transmission, excitability, and brain energy use. Additional indirect support comes from associations with neurodevelopmental disorders, psychiatric traits, ADHD treatment-response modeling, schizophrenia symptom dimensions, alcohol dependence, neuroticism, insulin sensitivity, BMI, adiposity, lipid traits, and cancer-related cell survival pathways.

Its current AD relevance is best considered hypothesis-generating. No supplied study demonstrates that NKAIN2 drives amyloid-β accumulation, tau pathology, neuroinflammation, synaptic degeneration, neuronal death, or clinical AD risk.
