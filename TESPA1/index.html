TESPA1 is not directly established as an Alzheimer's disease (AD) gene or therapeutic target in the available evidence. No study has reported TESPA1 expression, genetic association, methylation status, protein function, or therapeutic modulation specifically in AD patients, AD models, amyloid-β biology, tau pathology, neurons, microglia, astrocytes, synaptic dysfunction, or cognitive decline.

Its relevance to AD is therefore indirect and hypothesis-generating. TESPA1 is primarily described as an immune and calcium-signaling regulator — functioning in T-cell receptor (TCR) signaling, thymocyte development, B-cell activation, mast-cell responses, IP3 receptor-mediated calcium flux, and ER–mitochondria/MAM calcium transfer. These processes overlap conceptually with AD-relevant mechanisms such as immune aging, neuroinflammation, ER stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, calcium dyshomeostasis, and hippocampal vulnerability.

The strongest AD-adjacent signals identified are: an association of a TESPA1 variant (rs4374022) with shorter DNA methylation-derived telomere length and lower hippocampal volume, and hippocampal TESPA1 mRNA expression data, together with repeated mechanistic links to IP3R/MAM calcium signaling. None of these findings establish a causal role in AD.

No Direct AD Evidence

No study has demonstrated that TESPA1 affects amyloid-β production or clearance, tau phosphorylation or aggregation, synaptic loss, neuronal survival, microglial activation, astrocyte responses, blood-brain barrier function, or any AD clinical outcome.

Immune Signaling Hub

TESPA1 is most consistently linked to TCR-driven thymocyte development and positive selection, LAT signalosome function, and downstream Ca²⁺/NFAT and MAPK/Erk–AP-1 pathway activation — placing it centrally in adaptive immune regulation.

Context-Dependent & Opposing Roles

TESPA1 deficiency can impair T-cell and B-cell responses and reduce antitumor CD8⁺ activity, yet also attenuate autoimmune arthritis while worsening mast-cell/type-2 inflammation in asthma or allergic models. This bidirectionality raises significant safety concerns for therapeutic targeting.

IP3R / MAM Calcium Signaling

TESPA1 interacts with IP3 receptors to support receptor-induced calcium flux and ER calcium release. Its proposed role as a MAM-associated component affecting mitochondrial Ca²⁺ uptake is biologically plausible given that calcium dyshomeostasis and ER–mitochondria coupling are implicated in AD, though this bridge remains unvalidated in neuronal contexts.

Hippocampal & Telomere Associations

The strongest neuro-relevant finding is that the TESPA1 variant rs4374022 is associated with shorter DNA methylation-derived telomere length, lower hippocampal volume, and reduced hippocampal TESPA1 mRNA expression — all AD-relevant phenomena, but derived from aging/epigenetic studies, not AD-specific investigations.

Broader Disease Context

TESPA1 appears in inflammatory, autoimmune, cancer, and epigenetic disease signatures. These findings reinforce its role as an immune-state regulator with pleiotropic influence, but none establish AD-specific relevance. All links to Alzheimer's disease remain indirect and hypothesis-generating.
