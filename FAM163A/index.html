Key Insights

Mitochondrial & Neuronal Activity

Fam163a knockdown in mouse hypothalamic AgRP neurons altered food intake, leptin levels, Pomc expression, neuronal firing, and genes related to mitochondrial biogenesis, mitophagy, fusion, and oxidative stress — suggesting FAM163A influences neuronal activity via mitochondrial and redox regulation. Critically, this evidence comes from hypothalamic feeding circuits, not AD-vulnerable hippocampal or cortical neurons.

Mitochondrial Localization (Proteomics)

FAM163A was identified in mitochondrial-enriched fractions as a low-abundance "missing protein" in proteomics data, supporting possible mitochondrial or mitochondria-associated localization. Functional relevance to neurodegeneration remains entirely undefined in current literature.

Hippocampal Stress Response

Fam163a was among genes affected by prolonged corticosterone treatment in mouse hippocampus. Because chronic stress and glucocorticoid dysregulation are linked to hippocampal vulnerability and cognitive decline, FAM163A may merit investigation in stress-related AD risk pathways. No connection to amyloid, tau, synaptic degeneration, or AD diagnosis was demonstrated.

ERK/MAPK Signaling

Cancer studies show FAM163A/NDSP can promote ERK phosphorylation, p90RSK activation, cyclin D1 expression, and survival signaling. ERK signaling is relevant to AD via synaptic plasticity, tau phosphorylation, and cell survival/death pathways — but the FAM163A–ERK evidence derives entirely from cancer cells, not AD-relevant neurons or glia.

Secreted Protein & Biomarker Potential

FAM163A/NDSP has been described as a secreted protein, which could in principle make it more accessible for biomarker or therapeutic strategies. There is currently no evidence that FAM163A is present or altered in CSF, AD brain interstitial fluid, plaques, neurons, glia, or AD patient samples.
