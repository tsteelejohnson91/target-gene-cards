Overview

IQGAP1 is a multifunctional scaffold protein with plausible — but unproven — relevance to Alzheimer's disease (AD). The majority of supplied evidence derives from cancer, vascular, immune, infection, fibrosis, neurotoxicity, stroke, Parkinson's disease, and basic cell-biology studies. Only a small subset of the literature provides AD-specific computational evidence, placing IQGAP1 within diagnostic or disease-associated gene signatures.

The strongest AD-specific signals are biomarker-level rather than mechanistic. IQGAP1 appears in disulfidptosis-related and ferroptosis-related AD gene signatures with moderate-to-strong diagnostic performance (AUC 0.8847 in a discovery dataset; AUC >0.7 in two validation datasets). It co-appears with cytoskeletal genes such as MYH9, ACTN4, ACTB, MYL6, DSTN, and GYS1, and with ferroptosis-related genes RAF1, NFKBIA, MOV10L1, and FOXO1. These findings suggest IQGAP1 may mark AD-associated cytoskeletal, oxidative-stress, immune, or regulated-cell-death states, but do not prove causality.

Mechanistically, IQGAP1 converges on pathways central to AD biology: actin/microtubule remodeling, Rac1/Cdc42 GTPase signaling, ERK/MAPK and PI3K/Akt signaling, Wnt/β-catenin signaling, NF-κB/p38 and NLRP3 inflammasome biology, cellular senescence/SASP, autophagy and endosomal trafficking, mitochondrial/oxidative stress, endothelial barrier integrity, ApoE receptor/insulin transport, and synaptic plasticity. This makes it a plausible AD-relevant pathway node, particularly for neuroinflammation, synaptic/cytoskeletal dysfunction, impaired proteostasis, vascular dysfunction, and metabolic signaling.

As a therapeutic target, IQGAP1 is attractive because scaffold proteins can coordinate multiple disease-relevant pathways simultaneously. However, its bidirectionality demands selective, cell-type-specific, or interaction-specific modulation of pathological IQGAP1 complexes rather than nonspecific blockade.

Key Insights

Direct AD Evidence Is Limited

IQGAP1's AD relevance is primarily inferred from pathway overlap and computational biomarker signatures. The supplied text largely lacks direct experimental evidence involving amyloid-β, tau, AD animal models, human AD brain pathology, or cognitive decline in AD models.

AD-Associated Regulated-Cell-Death Signatures

IQGAP1 appears in disulfidptosis- and ferroptosis-related AD transcriptomic analyses. A disulfidptosis model achieved AUC 0.8847 in discovery; a ferroptosis model paired IQGAP1 with RAF1, NFKBIA, MOV10L1, and FOXO1. These link IQGAP1 to cytoskeletal collapse, redox imbalance, and immune-infiltrated AD subtypes — but functional validation is absent.

Major Cytoskeletal Scaffold with AD-Relevant Functions

IQGAP1 regulates actin filament dynamics, microtubule coordination, focal adhesions, membrane protrusions, cell polarity, and endosomal actin networks — functions directly relevant to dendritic spine loss, tau-associated microtubule disruption, synaptic instability, microglial motility, and blood–brain barrier remodeling in AD.

Hippocampal Plasticity & Cognitive Relevance

Non-AD brain studies link IQGAP1 to dendritic spine formation, GluN2A/NMDA signaling, ERK activation, histone H3 phosphorylation/acetylation, c-Fos expression, and memory formation. IQGAP1 deficiency impaired fear memory in mice; miR-124 regulation of IQGAP1 and a 3′UTR SNP were linked to cognitive performance in humans.

PI3K/Akt, Insulin Signaling & ApoE Trafficking

IQGAP1 directly associates with the insulin receptor and IRS-1, supports Akt and ERK phosphorylation, and is required for ApoE3/ApoER2-dependent insulin transcytosis and exocyst recruitment in endothelial cells. These mechanisms are highly relevant to brain insulin resistance, ApoE biology, vascular insulin transport, and tau/GSK3β regulation in AD.

Bidirectionality Demands Targeted Modulation

In inflammatory, vascular, and injury contexts IQGAP1 promotes pathological signaling (SASP, NLRP3, ROS, cGAS-STING, endothelial apoptosis). In other contexts it supports memory, dendritic spines, insulin signaling, autophagosome closure, endothelial repair, and mitochondrial complex I integrity. Global inhibition could worsen AD. Selective, cell-type-specific, interaction-specific modulation is required.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — IQGAP1 in Alzheimer's Disease

IQGAP1 is a biologically plausible but unvalidated AD target. Its convergence on virtually every pathway implicated in AD — cytoskeletal integrity, Rac1/Cdc42 GTPase regulation, ERK/MAPK and PI3K/Akt cascades, neuroinflammation and NLRP3/IL-1β inflammasome biology, ApoE receptor–mediated insulin transcytosis, autophagy and proteostasis, endosomal trafficking, cellular senescence/SASP, synaptic plasticity, and blood–brain barrier maintenance — makes it a high-interest scaffold protein for AD pathway biology. Computational biomarker data place it within AD-associated disulfidptosis and ferroptosis gene signatures with moderate-to-strong diagnostic performance. However, direct experimental evidence in AD models is essentially absent. The most critical barrier is its profound bidirectionality: IQGAP1 can be both protective and pathological depending on cell type, disease stage, and interaction partner. The most rational therapeutic hypothesis is selective normalization of pathological IQGAP1 scaffolding through domain-, complex-, and cell-type-specific interventions — targeting inflammatory/SASP, inflammasome, autophagy, PI3K/Akt, ERK/MAPK, ApoE receptor trafficking, or BBB-related IQGAP1 modules — rather than global IQGAP1 elimination.
