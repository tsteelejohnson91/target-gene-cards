MYO1F & Alzheimer's Disease

Target Gene Card — AI-Assisted Evidence Summary

Overview

⚠ Therapeutic Direction Unresolved: MYO1F may support beneficial microglial phagocytic clearance and contribute to harmful chronic neuroinflammation. Both enhancement and inhibition are mechanistically plausible; systemic inhibition carries immunosuppressive and infection-related risks.

MYO1F is best supported as an Alzheimer's disease-relevant immune and myeloid cytoskeletal regulator, rather than an established AD drug target. The strongest direct AD evidence comes from an integrative single-nucleus RNA-seq and Mendelian randomization study of dorsolateral prefrontal cortex that identified MYO1F among seven genes with a putative causal relationship to AD risk. MYO1F was associated with increased AD risk and showed increased expression in macrophage cells, which were significantly expanded in AD samples.

A second major AD-relevant link is mechanistic: MYO1F is selectively expressed in immune cells and is upregulated in microglia associated with neurodegenerative pathogenesis. In human myeloid cells and microglia, MYO1F interacts with a SH3-domain-dependent adaptor module including CD2AP, ASAP1, SH3BP2, and SH3KBP1. CD2AP is a known AD risk gene, upregulated in AD microglia and implicated in phagocytic responses to amyloid-β. This connects MYO1F to AD through microglial actin remodeling, phagocytic cups, podosomes, and CD2AP-linked amyloid-handling biology.

Across the broader literature, MYO1F repeatedly appears in macrophage/microglial phagocytosis, podosome formation, immune-cell migration and adhesion, inflammatory polarization, cytokine signaling, cGAS/type I interferon activation, MAPK/AKT, MyD88/NF-κB, and STAT-related pathways. These processes are central to AD neuroinflammation, amyloid-β/debris clearance, plaque-associated microglial behavior, synaptic remodeling, and potentially harmful chronic immune activation.

Key Insights

Genetic & Expression Evidence in AD

An AD-focused MR/snRNA-seq analysis identified MYO1F among seven causal AD risk candidates, with increased expression specifically in macrophage cells that were significantly expanded in AD dorsolateral prefrontal cortex samples.

CD2AP Adaptor Axis

MYO1F physically links to a CD2AP-containing adaptor complex (CD2AP, ASAP1, SH3BP2, SH3KBP1) via its SH3 domain. Because CD2AP is an established AD risk gene involved in microglial amyloid-β phagocytic responses, the MYO1F–CD2AP axis is a particularly important therapeutic hypothesis.

Phagocytic Cup & Podosome Function

MYO1F localizes to actin-rich podosomes and phagocytic cups. Its motor activity and PH/SH3 domains are required for phagocytic cup recruitment, organizing membrane–actin remodeling during engulfment of amyloid-β, debris, damaged synapses, and myelin.

Inflammatory Signaling Risk

MYO1F regulates macrophage/microglial activation states, MAPK/AKT, MyD88/NF-κB, cGAS-STING, and type I interferon pathways. MYO1F deficiency reduced IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, and immune-cell recruitment in some models — suggesting a pro-inflammatory role that may drive chronic neuroinflammation.

Context-Dependent Dual Role

MYO1F may support beneficial phagocytic clearance of amyloid-β while simultaneously contributing to damaging chronic neuroinflammation, maladaptive microglial activation, and synaptic pruning. Both MYO1F enhancement and inhibition are mechanistically plausible depending on disease stage.

Myeloid Biology — Not Neuronal

MYO1F is selectively expressed in immune cells and is most plausibly relevant to AD through myeloid and microglial biology — including cell motility, integrin-related adhesion, podosome turnover, and plaque-directed migration — rather than neuron-intrinsic amyloid or tau mechanisms.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Treat MYO1F as a mechanistic candidate, not a validated target. The current evidence positions MYO1F as a promising but unvalidated mechanistic AD candidate; invest in functional studies before committing to therapeutic development.
  2. Prioritize AD-relevant microglial assays. Determine whether MYO1F increases or decreases amyloid-β uptake, plaque compaction, debris clearance, synapse engulfment, tau-related responses, and inflammatory cytokine production in microglial systems.
  3. Test the MYO1F–CD2AP/CASS adaptor axis directly. Expose microglia to amyloid-β, tau aggregates, apoptotic neurons, synaptosomes, or myelin debris and dissect the contribution of the MYO1F–CD2AP–ASAP1 complex to phagocytic outcomes.
  4. Evaluate domain-specific modulation. Assess whether modulating MYO1F motor activity, PH-domain membrane recruitment, or SH3-domain adaptor binding differentially changes phagocytic cup formation and closure versus inflammatory signaling.
  5. Map MYO1F inflammatory signaling routes in AD microglia. Examine whether MYO1F drives harmful AD microglial inflammatory states through MAPK/AKT, MyD88/NF-κB, STAT3/STAT1, cGAS-STING, or type I interferon pathways using microglial-specific knockdown or overexpression.
  6. Avoid assuming systemic MYO1F inhibition is safe. MYO1F supports host defense, leukocyte trafficking, macrophage and neutrophil function, phagocytosis, adhesion, and T-cell responses; systemic blockade could carry immunosuppressive or infection-related risks.
  7. Target brain or myeloid-specific modulation. Consider microglia-targeted delivery strategies rather than systemic MYO1F targeting to minimize peripheral immune compromise.
  8. Stratify by AD disease stage. Early-stage enhancement of phagocytic clearance mechanisms may differ fundamentally from late-stage suppression of chronic inflammatory activation; therapeutic testing should account for this dichotomy.
  9. Use readouts that distinguish beneficial from harmful microglial activity. Include amyloid/tau burden, microglial state markers (DAM, homeostatic), cytokine profiles, complement/synapse loss, neuronal survival, cognition, and infection susceptibility as differentiated endpoints.
  10. Position MYO1F as a neuroimmune biomarker node near-term. The most realistic near-term role is as a mechanistic biomarker and experimental node for AD neuroimmune biology, especially in CD2AP-linked microglial phagocytosis and inflammatory activation studies.

Overall Assessment

Synthesis · Target Evaluation

Overall Assessment — MYO1F occupies a compelling but unresolved position in Alzheimer's disease biology. It carries genetic and expression-level support from Mendelian randomization and single-nucleus RNA-seq identifying it as a putative causal AD risk gene with upregulated macrophage expression in AD brain. Mechanistically, its interaction with the CD2AP-containing adaptor complex — a network centered on an established AD risk gene — provides a direct link to microglial amyloid-β phagocytosis and actin remodeling. Broader literature implicates MYO1F in podosome formation, phagocytic cup dynamics, immune-cell migration, and multiple inflammatory signaling cascades including MAPK/AKT, MyD88/NF-κB, cGAS-STING, and type I interferon pathways. The critical unresolved question is whether MYO1F's net effect in the AD brain is protective — by supporting amyloid clearance — or harmful — by amplifying chronic neuroinflammation. Both outcomes are mechanistically grounded in the existing evidence. Consequently, neither enhancement nor inhibition can yet be recommended as a therapeutic direction without stage-specific, microglia-targeted functional validation. MYO1F is best advanced as a high-priority mechanistic research node within the CD2AP/microglial neuroimmune axis, with therapeutic potential contingent on resolving this fundamental ambiguity.
