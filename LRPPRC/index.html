Overview

⚠ Safety Caveat: Human LRPPRC loss-of-function causes Leigh Syndrome French Canadian type — severe mitochondrial neurodegeneration via respiratory-chain failure. Broad suppression of LRPPRC in neurons carries a real risk of worsening bioenergetic failure and must be avoided until demonstrated safe.

LRPPRC (also known as LRP130) is best characterized as a mitochondrial RNA-binding and gene-expression regulator with strong mechanistic relevance to Alzheimer's disease (AD). Its core functions span mitochondrial mRNA stability, mitochondrial translation, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), complex IV/cytochrome c oxidase function, ATP production, oxidative stress, mitophagy/autophagy, and neuroinflammatory signaling.

Direct AD-specific evidence includes: reduced LRPPRC expression in vulnerable AD cortical tissue alongside reduced mtDNA-encoded OXPHOS genes and reduced PGC-1α; altered Lrpprc in 3×Tg-AD mouse cerebrum proteomics; correlation of Lrpprc mRNA with hippocampal Aβ plaque burden in AD mice; identification as an m6A-related AD risk gene; and preferential interaction with an early-onset AD APP variant in proteomic work. The bulk of mechanistic evidence derives from mitochondrial disease, neurodegeneration models, cancer, metabolic disease, ischemia, and immune/inflammatory contexts.

LRPPRC is not yet a validated AD therapeutic target, but represents a credible mitochondrial resilience node. Its most plausible AD relevance lies in regulating neuronal bioenergetics, mitochondrial RNA homeostasis, respiratory-chain integrity, mitochondrial quality control, and stress survival — rather than acting as an amyloid- or tau-specific factor.

Key Insights

LRPPRC–SLIRP Complex & mRNA Stability

LRPPRC operates primarily through its complex with SLIRP, which stabilizes mitochondrial transcripts, maintains translation-competent RNA structure, supports mRNA delivery to mitoribosomes, and drives respiratory-chain protein synthesis. Disruption of this complex — for example by K224 lactylation — collapses mitochondrial transcript pools and impairs bioenergetics.

OXPHOS Defects & Leigh Syndrome Risk

Reduced or dysfunctional LRPPRC repeatedly causes OXPHOS defects, especially Complex IV/cytochrome c oxidase deficiency, affecting also Complex I, ATP synthase, and respiratory supercomplex organization. In humans, LRPPRC loss-of-function causes Leigh Syndrome French Canadian type, establishing that LRPPRC deficiency can produce severe neurodegeneration. This strongly cautions against broad LRPPRC inhibition as an AD strategy.

Lactylation as a Therapeutic Switch

Hyperglycemia-induced lactylation at mouse K223/human K224 disrupts LRPPRC–SLIRP binding, destabilizes mitochondrial mRNAs, causes hippocampal mitochondrial dysfunction and neuronal apoptosis, and produces cognitive decline in diabetic mice. A peptide blocking this lactylation rescued cognition — making pathological K224 lactylation the strongest cognition-linked actionable mechanism identified in the evidence.

PGC-1α–LRPPRC Mitochondrial Resilience Axis

In AD brain, both PGC-1α and LRPPRC were found reduced together, suggesting an impaired mitochondrial resilience axis. Restoring this co-regulatory relationship could theoretically improve mitochondrial transcript handling and ATP-generating capacity — representing a restorative rather than inhibitory therapeutic direction.

Context-Dependent Mitophagy Roles

LRPPRC has dual roles in mitochondrial quality control: it can restrain Beclin1-dependent autophagy via Bcl-2-related complexes and intersects with Parkin/PINK1 pathways. In AD, where defective mitophagy and accumulation of damaged mitochondria are central concerns, selective modulation of LRPPRC protein interactions may be more useful than global activation or inhibition of the full protein.

AD-Specific Evidence & Biomarker Potential

Direct AD evidence includes reduced LRPPRC in vulnerable AD cortex alongside reduced mtDNA OXPHOS genes and PGC-1α; altered Lrpprc in 3×Tg-AD mouse proteomics; correlation with hippocampal Aβ plaque burden; selection as an m6A-related AD risk gene; and interaction with an early-onset AD APP variant. These observations position LRPPRC as a candidate biomarker of mitochondrial resilience failure in metabolic- and oxidative-stress-driven AD subtypes.

Define key next experiments: LRPPRC rescue or stabilization in AD neuron models; knockdown/overexpression dose-response studies in aged neurons; testing K224 lactylation in AD and diabetic cognitive impairment cohorts; mapping LRPPRC–APP, –SLIRP, –Parkin, and –Beclin1 interactions in AD models; and evaluating whether LRPPRC modulation improves mitochondrial and synaptic phenotypes without oncogenic or mitochondrial-toxicity risk.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — LRPPRC as an AD Mitochondrial Resilience Target LRPPRC is a credible but not yet validated target in Alzheimer's disease, with its greatest relevance lying in neuronal bioenergetics, mitochondrial RNA homeostasis, and respiratory-chain integrity rather than in amyloid or tau biology directly. The mechanistic case is strong: LRPPRC anchors mitochondrial mRNA stability via the LRPPRC–SLIRP complex, drives OXPHOS and Complex IV function, intersects with mitophagy quality control through Parkin/PINK1 and Beclin1 pathways, and is co-reduced with PGC-1α in AD-vulnerable cortex. The strongest actionable signal is pathological K224 lactylation, a hyperglycemia-induced post-translational modification that disrupts the LRPPRC–SLIRP complex, collapses mitochondrial mRNA stability, and produces cognitive decline reversible by a blocking peptide — positioning this as a tractable mechanism for metabolic-risk-associated dementia. The principal constraint is safety: human LRPPRC loss-of-function produces Leigh Syndrome and fatal mitochondrial neurodegeneration, meaning any therapeutic strategy must restore or stabilize LRPPRC function rather than broadly suppress it. With targeted restorative approaches, modification-specific interventions, and cell-type-selective tools, LRPPRC warrants continued investigation as a mitochondrial resilience node in AD.
