⚠ Therapeutic Caution MYO1D is not a validated Alzheimer's disease target. Systemic inhibition risks disrupting fundamental cytoskeletal, receptor-trafficking, barrier-integrity, myelin-repair, and oligodendrocyte-survival pathways. Directionality of any intervention must be established before therapeutic development is pursued.

MYO1D is an unconventional class I, actin-associated motor protein involved in actin–membrane coupling, F-actin interactions, membrane trafficking, endosomal and receptor localization, vesicle transport, tunneling nanotube-mediated cargo transfer, cytoskeletal remodeling, ROS/JNK signaling, caspase regulation, synaptic/spine localization, oligodendrocyte and myelin maintenance, cilia/planar cell polarity, and vascular or barrier-associated biology. It is not directly established in the supplied evidence as an Alzheimer's disease gene, biomarker, or validated therapeutic target.

The relevance to Alzheimer's disease is indirect and mechanistic. No cited evidence directly shows MYO1D alterations in Alzheimer's patient tissue, Alzheimer's models, amyloid-β processing, tau pathology, cognitive decline, or AD-specific neurodegeneration.

The strongest disease-adjacent finding comes from a synucleinopathy-related blood–brain barrier study: MYO1D activation supported α-synuclein preformed fibril transfer from brain endothelial cells to pericytes through tunneling nanotubes, and MYO1D inhibition blocked this transfer. This is not AD-specific, but it is highly relevant by analogy because AD also involves misfolded protein spread, blood–brain barrier dysfunction, pericyte vulnerability, actin-dependent intercellular trafficking, and vascular contributions to neurodegeneration.

Key Insights

No Direct AD Evidence

No supplied evidence demonstrates MYO1D involvement in amyloid-β processing, tau pathology, APP biology, synaptic loss in AD, microglial activation in AD, or Alzheimer's-related cognitive impairment. MYO1D's relevance is entirely indirect and mechanistic.

BBB / Tunneling Nanotube Finding

MYO1D activation drove α-synuclein preformed fibril transfer via tunneling nanotubes from brain endothelial cells to pericytes; MYO1D inhibition blocked this transfer. By analogy, MYO1D may mediate tau, Aβ, or inflammatory cargo spread across vascular–neural interfaces in AD.

Synaptic Enrichment in Hippocampus

MYO1D is enriched in dendritic spines of hippocampal neurons and depends on its TH1 domain for F-actin colocalization — connecting it to dendritic spine loss and actin dysregulation, which are early and cardinal features of Alzheimer's disease.

Myelin & Oligodendrocyte Maintenance

MYO1D is expressed in CNS myelin and mature oligodendrocytes, supports PLP transport and myelin-like membrane integrity, and its knockdown impairs remyelination while increasing inflammatory glial responses and oligodendrocyte apoptosis — suggesting relevance to AD-associated white-matter injury.

Receptor Signaling & Trafficking

MYO1D binds EGFR-family receptors and PDGFRα/β heterodimers, influences receptor anchoring or internalization, and alters ERK signaling — pathways that intersect with vascular integrity, glial signaling, repair responses, and neuroinflammation in AD.

Therapeutic Risk: Directionality Problem

MYO1D participates in opposing biological roles: inhibition could block pathogenic protein transfer, but the same or related activity also sustains myelin integrity, oligodendrocyte survival, and tissue repair. AD intervention must first resolve this directionality conflict before any therapeutic targeting is pursued.
