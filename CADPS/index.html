Overview

⚠ Safety Caveat: CADPS participates in secretion across neurons, neuroendocrine cells, pancreatic islets, adrenal/chromaffin systems, pain pathways, and metabolic tissues. Non-selective modulation carries significant risk of disrupting insulin secretion, catecholamine release, monoamine tone, pain sensitivity, and broad neuropeptide/hormone physiology.

CADPS/CAPS proteins — including CADPS1/CAPS1 and CADPS2/CAPS2 — are calcium-dependent regulators of vesicle priming and regulated secretion. They support synaptic vesicle and dense-core vesicle exocytosis, SNARE-mediated fusion, PI(4,5)P₂/PIP₂-dependent membrane priming, neurotransmitter and neuropeptide release, monoamine/catecholamine handling, BDNF and NT-3 secretion, vesicle acidification, and hippocampal synaptic plasticity.

The supplied literature provides limited direct Alzheimer's disease evidence. One direct statement links CADPS2 genomic variability with Alzheimer's disease in the context of altered neurotransmission, but most evidence is mechanistic and inferential rather than AD-specific. No supplied study demonstrates that CADPS directly regulates amyloid-β production, tau phosphorylation, plaque formation, neurofibrillary tangles, ApoE biology, or cognitive decline in an AD model.

CADPS is nevertheless highly relevant to AD mechanisms because it sits at the intersection of several processes disrupted early in AD: presynaptic failure, impaired vesicle trafficking, calcium dysregulation, phosphoinositide signaling, dense-core vesicle dysfunction, altered neuromodulator release, reduced neurotrophic support, excitation–inhibition imbalance, hippocampal LTP impairment, and memory-circuit vulnerability. Its therapeutic potential is best framed as a candidate synaptic-resilience and neurotrophic-support target rather than a validated disease-modifying AD target.

Key Insights

Core Vesicle Priming Factor

CADPS/CAPS proteins are required for generating or maintaining fusion-competent vesicle pools, especially for Ca²⁺-triggered dense-core vesicle release and fast phasic neurotransmitter release. This positions CADPS as a presynaptic control point whose impairment mirrors early AD synaptic failure.

SNARE & Lipid-Dependent Mechanism

CAPS binds or functionally interacts with syntaxin-1, SNAP-25, VAMP2, and PI(4,5)P₂/PIP₂-rich membrane domains. Altered membrane lipid signaling or calcium homeostasis — both disrupted in AD — could impair CADPS-mediated secretion at this presynaptic control point.

BDNF / Neurotrophic Axis

CADPS/CAPS regulates dense-core vesicle exocytosis, vesicle acidification, neurotransmitter loading, and release of BDNF and NT-3. Because BDNF supports synaptic plasticity and neuronal survival, impaired CADPS-dependent neurotrophin secretion could plausibly worsen AD-related synaptic vulnerability. The CADPS2–BDNF axis is highlighted as a high-value therapeutic candidate.

Hippocampal Circuit & AD Relevance

Conditional Caps1 loss in hippocampal circuits reduces docked vesicles, impairs synaptic transmission, disrupts CA1/CA3 LTP, and causes learning deficits. CADPS2 genomic variability is the only direct AD association in the supplied text, linked to altered neurotransmission. Most other evidence is mechanistic and inferential.

Precision Targeting Opportunities

A-to-I RNA editing of Cadps alters CAPS1 localization and inhibitory short-term plasticity. An exon-16a-containing CAPS1 isoform regulates CaV2.2 coupling in nociceptors and can be modulated by a peptide. These findings demonstrate isoform-, domain-, and cell-type-specific tractability for precision intervention.

High-Risk Global Target

CADPS participates in secretion across neurons, neuroendocrine cells, pancreatic islets, adrenal/chromaffin systems, pain pathways, and metabolic tissues. Non-selective activation or inhibition risks disrupting insulin secretion, catecholamine release, monoamine tone, pain sensitivity, stress responses, and broad neuropeptide/hormone physiology. Safety screening is essential before any therapeutic advance.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Treat CADPS as a mechanistic AD candidate, not a validated target. Current evidence supports prioritization for AD mechanism studies, especially around synaptic vesicle priming, dense-core vesicle secretion, BDNF release, hippocampal plasticity, and calcium/PIP₂-dependent exocytosis — not as a primary amyloid/tau modulator.
  2. Generate direct AD validation data. Key studies should test CADPS1/CADPS2 expression, protein abundance, localization, phosphorylation, RNA editing, alternative splicing, and vesicle association in AD patient brain and AD models — focusing on hippocampus, entorhinal cortex, cortex, basal forebrain, and olfactory-related regions.
  3. Link CADPS status to AD pathology and clinical phenotypes. Future work should assess whether CADPS alterations correlate with synaptic loss, amyloid burden, tau pathology, neuronal hyperactivity or hypoactivity, neuroinflammation, BDNF deficits, cognitive decline, or disease stage.
  4. Test functional rescue in AD models. Experiments should determine whether restoring CADPS-dependent vesicle priming, BDNF secretion, or dense-core vesicle release improves synaptic transmission, LTP, network stability, and memory in amyloid/tau or sporadic AD models.
  5. Prioritize precision modulation over global targeting. The most plausible therapeutic strategies would target CADPS isoforms, splice variants, edited transcripts, phosphorylation states, protein-lipid interactions, SNARE/syntaxin coupling, or cell-type-specific functions rather than broadly increasing or suppressing CADPS activity.
  6. Explore the CADPS2–BDNF axis as a high-value therapeutic priority. Because impaired BDNF signaling is strongly relevant to AD synaptic failure, CADPS2-mediated neurotrophin release may be one of the most attractive mechanisms to investigate within this gene family.
  7. Evaluate inhibitory synapse and E/I effects carefully. CADPS editing affects GABAergic synapses and short-term depression. Any AD therapy that changes CADPS activity could improve or worsen network hyperexcitability depending on disease stage and circuit context.
  8. Assess safety liabilities early in drug development. CADPS-targeted interventions should be screened for effects on endocrine secretion, insulin release, catecholamine handling, monoamine storage, pain pathways, sleep/circadian regulation, and stress responses before advancing toward clinical translation.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — CADPS / CAPS in Alzheimer's Disease

CADPS/CAPS proteins occupy a compelling but inferential position in AD biology. The direct genetic link — CADPS2 genomic variability associated with AD — is real but uncharacterized mechanistically, and no supplied study demonstrates CADPS regulation of amyloid-β, tau, or plaques. Despite this, CADPS is mechanistically highly relevant: it sits at the convergence of presynaptic vesicle priming, calcium homeostasis, PIP₂ lipid signaling, BDNF neurotrophic support, hippocampal LTP, and neuromodulatory tone — all processes that fail early in AD. Its best therapeutic rationale is as a synaptic-resilience and neurotrophic-support target, particularly through the CADPS2–BDNF secretion axis and precision modulation of isoform- or cell-type-specific function. However, its broad expression across endocrine, metabolic, and autonomic tissues means global targeting carries substantial safety risk; precision approaches targeting specific splice variants, edited transcripts, or phosphorylation states represent the only credible therapeutic path forward.
