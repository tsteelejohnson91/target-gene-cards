Overview

⚠ Selectivity Caveat:  Calpain-1 and calpain-2 can have opposing roles in the nervous system. Calpain-1 is often neuroprotective and plasticity-supporting; global pan-calpain inhibition risks impairing beneficial signaling. AD-directed strategies require selective, brain-penetrant, context-aware CAPN2 modulation.

CAPN2 encodes calpain-2, a calcium-activated cysteine protease repeatedly positioned as a downstream effector of calcium dyshomeostasis, proteolytic substrate cleavage, synaptic dysfunction, tau-related pathology, neuronal apoptosis, ER stress, mitochondrial impairment, autophagy/lysosomal disruption, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and tissue remodeling. Across the evidence base, CAPN2 is more often associated with pathological or neurodegenerative outcomes than protective ones — especially when activation is prolonged, excessive, synapse-localized, or mislocalized to the nucleus.

The Alzheimer's disease relevance is strongest where the evidence directly links CAPN2 to AD models, human AD tissue, tau proteolysis, synaptic hyperactivation, and cholinergic dysfunction. CAPN2 is reported to be selectively hyperactivated in synaptosomal fractions from APPswe/PS1ΔE9 mice as early as 1 month of age — before overt pathology or behavioral deficits — and this synaptic hyperactivation persists with age. Human AD frontal neocortical tissue also shows synaptosomal CAPN2 hyperactivation, with activity correlating with cognitive/memory decline and β-amyloid burden.

CAPN2 has direct tau-related relevance: calpain-2, but not calpain-1, cleaves tau to generate an N-terminal tau fragment ending at amino acid 224 (N-224 tau), a major CSF tau fragment increased in AD. CAPN2 knockdown reduced N-224 tau release by 76.4% in SH-SY5Y cells. Additional studies connect CAPN2 to tauopathy mechanisms through the CAPN2–PTPN13–c-Abl pathway, where CAPN2 cleavage of PTPN13 permits c-Abl activation, tau tyrosine phosphorylation, and tau oligomer accumulation.

Therapeutically, selective inhibition or knockdown repeatedly reduces pathological outcomes in neurodegeneration-relevant models. However, CAPN2 also participates in physiological synaptic plasticity, memory, differentiation, and membrane repair. Therefore, AD-directed strategies would need selective, brain-penetrant, context-aware modulation rather than broad pan-calpain blockade. The clinical candidate alicapistat showed short-term tolerability and measurable plasma pharmacokinetics but lacked convincing CNS pharmacodynamic engagement, highlighting the need for better brain-penetrant compounds and direct CNS biomarkers.

Key Insights

Early Synaptic Hyperactivation in AD

In APPswe/PS1ΔE9 mice, synaptosomal CAPN2 activity increases presymptomatically (from 1 month) and remains elevated. Human AD frontal cortex also shows synaptosomal CAPN2 hyperactivation correlating with memory/cognitive decline and β-amyloid deposition, suggesting CAPN2 contributes to early synaptic failure rather than merely reflecting late-stage neuronal death.

Direct Role in Tau Proteolysis

Calpain-2 selectively generates N-224 tau, a CSF tau fragment elevated in AD. CAPN2 knockdown reduced N-224 tau release by 76.4% in SH-SY5Y cells. CAPN2 also drives tau phosphorylation and oligomer accumulation via PTPN13 cleavage and c-Abl activation, placing CAPN2 at the center of tau pathology.

Mechanistic Link to Cholinergic Dysfunction

Human full-length tau accumulation elevates intracellular calcium, activates calpain-2, and promotes degradation of α4 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors in hippocampal neurons. Selective suppression of calpain-2 — but not calpain-1 — attenuates this tau-induced receptor loss, providing a direct mechanistic bridge between tau pathology and the cholinergic deficit central to AD.

p25/Cdk5 Dysregulation

Multiple neuroinjury studies connect CAPN2 activation to cleavage of p35 into the pathological p25 fragment, producing aberrant Cdk5 activation. p25/Cdk5 dysregulation is directly linked to tau phosphorylation, synaptic dysfunction, and neuronal death — making this a key mechanistic node for AD-relevant neurodegeneration.

CAPN2 vs. CAPN1: Opposing Roles

Calpain-1 is often described as neuroprotective or plasticity-supporting, while calpain-2 is associated with neurodegeneration, delayed injury signaling, STEP-mediated death pathways, and restriction of LTP consolidation. This functional divergence strongly supports CAPN2-selective targeting and argues against broad pan-calpain inhibition for AD.

Clinical Translation: Alicapistat Gap

Alicapistat, an orally active calpain-1/2 inhibitor developed for AD, demonstrated short-term tolerability and measurable plasma pharmacokinetics but lacked convincing CNS pharmacodynamic engagement based on REM sleep parameters. This highlights the critical need for brain-penetrant compounds and direct CNS target-engagement biomarkers such as CSF tau-fragment assays or α-spectrin breakdown products.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Prioritize CAPN2 as a mechanistically credible AD target, especially for early synaptic dysfunction, tau proteolysis, calcium-driven neuronal injury, p25/Cdk5 signaling, and tau-associated cholinergic deficits.
  2. Focus on selective CAPN2 modulation rather than pan-calpain inhibition. Therapeutic development should preserve calpain-1 functions that support neuronal survival, LTP, learning, memory, and plasticity.
  3. Validate CAPN2 activity in AD-relevant compartments. Key readouts should include synaptosomal CAPN2 activity, nuclear CAPN2 translocation, N-224 tau fragments, p35/p25 ratios, Cdk5 activity, α-spectrin breakdown products, α4 nAChR cleavage, and CAPN2-generated PTPN13 fragments.
  4. Develop CNS target-engagement biomarkers. Plasma exposure alone is insufficient. CSF, brain-derived extracellular vesicles, FRET-based calpain activity probes, tau-fragment assays, and α-spectrin or PTPN13 breakdown products could confirm CAPN2 inhibition in relevant brain compartments.
  5. Consider targeting pathological localization or substrate interactions — blocking CAPN2 nuclear translocation, synaptic hyperactivation, p35 cleavage, tau-224 cleavage, PTPN13 cleavage, or α4 nAChR degradation may be safer than global catalytic suppression.
  6. Evaluate upstream calcium-stabilizing strategies. Because CAPN2 is often activated downstream of ER Ca²⁺ leak, NMDA receptor activity, oxidative stress, and excitotoxicity, therapies normalizing calcium handling may reduce pathological CAPN2 activation while preserving physiological calpain signaling.
  7. Account for disease stage and dose. Low-level or transient CAPN2 activity may support normal plasticity, whereas prolonged or localized hyperactivation appears pathological. AD therapy should aim for partial, disease-state-dependent inhibition with careful cognitive safety monitoring.
  8. Investigate CAPN2 across multiple AD-relevant cell types. Beyond neurons, CAPN2 may affect microglia, astrocytes, endothelial cells, immune-cell trafficking, and blood-brain barrier integrity, broadening the scope of target-relevant biology.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — CAPN2 as an AD Target

CAPN2 represents a mechanistically credible and convergent target for Alzheimer's disease, sitting at the intersection of calcium dyshomeostasis, tau proteolysis, synaptic dysfunction, cholinergic failure, and neuronal stress cascades. The evidence for presymptomatic synaptic hyperactivation in both transgenic mouse models and human AD tissue, together with the selective generation of the AD-relevant N-224 tau fragment and a direct mechanistic link to p25/Cdk5 dysregulation and cholinergic receptor loss, argues that CAPN2 is not merely a bystander but an early-acting effector of synaptic and neuronal damage. However, the target demands selectivity over calpain-1, context-aware dosing to preserve physiological plasticity, and — critically — the development of CNS-active compounds with robust brain target-engagement biomarkers. The failure of alicapistat to demonstrate CNS pharmacodynamic engagement underscores that peripheral pharmacokinetics are insufficient proof of efficacy. Larger AD-specific studies with direct CNS readouts are needed before CAPN2 inhibition can be considered a validated therapeutic strategy.
