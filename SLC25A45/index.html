No cited study directly examines SLC25A45 in Alzheimer's brain tissue, amyloid biology, tau pathology, neuroinflammation, or Alzheimer's models. Relevance is currently indirect and mechanistic only.

SLC25A45 is a mitochondrial inner-membrane transporter whose strongest evidence positions it as an importer of methylated amino acids — especially trimethyllysine (TML) — enabling de novo carnitine biosynthesis. Carnitine is essential for shuttling long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondrial matrix for β-oxidation, underpinning mitochondrial fuel flexibility. These processes are broadly implicated in Alzheimer's-associated mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired metabolic adaptability.

Beyond the carnitine axis, SLC25A45 is functionally linked to mitochondrial translation, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), methylated amino-acid homeostasis, possible NMN/NAD⁺ regulation, thyroid-hormone-responsive transcription, and human genetic associations with circulating SDMA and resting heart rate. Together, these features constitute a mechanistically coherent but as-yet speculative rationale for SLC25A45 as an exploratory Alzheimer's-related candidate.

Key Insights

Core Mechanism: TML–Carnitine Axis

The best-supported function of SLC25A45 is import of trimethyllysine (TML) into the mitochondrial matrix, directly enabling de novo carnitine biosynthesis. Carnitine is required for long-chain fatty-acid transport and β-oxidation, and thus for mitochondrial fuel flexibility.

Deficiency Phenotype & Metabolic Shift

SLC25A45 deficiency depletes the carnitine pool, impairs mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation, and forces a shift toward carbohydrate metabolism. In mice this manifests as cold intolerance and resistance to lipid mobilization induced by GLP-1 receptor agonists.

Broad Mitochondrial Function

Genome-wide knockout data implicate SLC25A45 in mitochondrial protein synthesis, biogenesis, OXPHOS system integrity, and mitochondrial morphology — all processes critical for neuronal survival and synaptic maintenance, though no Alzheimer's-specific evidence is yet available.

NMN / NAD⁺ Connection

A biosensor study suggests SLC25A45 may influence local mitochondrial NMN/NAD⁺ ratios. Given that NAD⁺ decline and mitochondrial redox imbalance are established aging and neurodegeneration themes, this is intriguing — but direct NMN transport and Alzheimer's relevance remain unproven.

Human Genetic & Endocrine Links

SLC25A45 variation associates with circulating SDMA and resting heart rate in GWAS, indirectly connecting it to vascular, renal, nitric-oxide, and autonomic pathways relevant to dementia risk. Thyroid hormone regulates Slc25a45 transcription, suggesting broader endocrine-metabolic control, though not brain-specific.

Safety & Off-Target Concerns

SLC25A45 activity shapes systemic metabolite signatures and may support metabolic reprogramming in certain cancers. Rare variants associate with creatinine levels and chronic kidney disease traits. Any therapeutic strategy must account for systemic fuel-use effects, renal function, cardiovascular physiology, and cancer-related metabolic risks.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — SLC25A45 in Alzheimer's Disease

SLC25A45 is mechanistically coherent but currently speculative as an Alzheimer's disease target. Its core function — mitochondrial TML import enabling carnitine biosynthesis, fatty-acid oxidation, and metabolic fuel switching — overlaps convincingly with established Alzheimer's themes of mitochondrial energetic failure, impaired lipid metabolism, and reduced metabolic flexibility. Additional roles in OXPHOS, mitochondrial translation, NAD⁺ regulation, and methylated amino-acid homeostasis further enrich its mechanistic plausibility. However, no direct evidence links SLC25A45 to Alzheimer's brain pathology, amyloid or tau biology, neuroinflammation, or cognitive decline, and the human genetic associations identified to date point toward vascular and renal rather than neurodegenerative phenotypes. Safety considerations around renal, cardiovascular, and cancer-related effects add further caution. The appropriate near-term posture is rigorous exploratory investigation — beginning with brain expression profiling and genetic association analyses — before any commitment to therapeutic development.
