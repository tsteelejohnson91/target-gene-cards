Overview

⚠ Evidentiary Caveat: KANK2 is not an established Alzheimer's disease gene or validated AD therapeutic target. Direct AD-specific functional evidence is absent. Its candidacy rests on positional genomics (chr19p13.2), cross-disease tauopathy prioritization (PSP), and plausible mechanistic overlap with AD-relevant biology.

KANK2 is located at chromosome 19p13.2, a region implicated in AD susceptibility and transcriptomic perturbation in AD brain tissue, yet the supplied evidence does not demonstrate that KANK2 itself is altered, causal, or functionally validated in Alzheimer's disease.

The strongest disease-relevant evidence comes from progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP), a primary tauopathy. A cross-species systems biology study — integrating human brain transcriptomics, single-nucleus RNA-seq, mouse tauopathy models, and Drosophila tau-model validation — prioritized KANK2 alongside DDR2 and STOM as a promising PSP therapeutic target. Because AD also involves tau pathology and glial dysfunction, this positions KANK2 as an exploratory AD-relevant candidate, not an AD-proven target.

KANK2's core biology centers on cytoskeletal scaffolding: it acts as an adaptor in focal adhesion and integrin/talin complexes, bridges adhesions to microtubules, and regulates cell migration, force transmission, and mechanotransduction. These mechanisms intersect plausibly with AD-related tau biology, microtubule disruption, axonal transport, glial activation, and vascular dysfunction.

Overall Assessment — KANK2 in Alzheimer's Disease

KANK2 is a biologically interesting but unvalidated AD candidate. Its most robust disease-relevant evidence derives from tauopathy (PSP), where cross-species systems biology has prioritized it as a promising target — a finding that carries indirect but meaningful relevance to AD given the shared tau pathology. The gene's core biology as a cytoskeletal scaffold linking focal adhesions to microtubules, its connections to inflammatory pathways (NF-κB, COX2, TNF, IL-6), mechanotransduction (RHOA/GEF-H1), metabolic regulation (ERRα/PGC-1α), and HSP70-mediated stress responses provide multiple plausible points of intersection with AD mechanisms. The fact that KANK2 operates primarily as an interaction hub rather than an enzyme adds further complexity to therapeutic development. Investment in AD brain transcriptomic/proteomic characterization and targeted perturbation in AD-relevant models represents the most defensible next step before any stronger claims about KANK2 as an AD target can be made.
