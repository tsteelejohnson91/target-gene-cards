Overview

⚠ Therapeutic Caution

Complete or systemic LACTB inhibition carries meaningful risk: LACTB participates in mitochondrial lipid metabolism, cardiolipin remodeling, apoptosis regulation, autophagy/mitophagy, and degradation of D-aspartate-containing damaged proteins. Global suppression could disrupt these essential functions across multiple cell types and tissues.

LACTB is a conserved mitochondrial serine β-lactamase-like protease with Alzheimer's disease (AD)-relevant links centered on mitochondrial metabolism, myeloid/microglial immune function, succinylcarnitine handling, lipid remodeling, inflammatory signaling, proteostasis, autophagy/mitophagy, oxidative stress, ferroptosis, and mitochondrial membrane organization.

The strongest AD-specific evidence comes from Romero-Molina et al. (2026 bioRxiv), identifying a LACTB–succinylcarnitine–myeloid cell axis. Lower LACTB expression in myeloid cells was genetically associated with reduced AD risk, while lower LACTB also correlated with higher succinylcarnitine levels. Succinylcarnitine independently correlated with lower AD risk, and LACTB was identified as the primary enzyme responsible for succinylcarnitine hydrolysis. This supports a model in which reduced myeloid LACTB increases succinylcarnitine, shifting immune-metabolic states toward lower AD susceptibility.

A second AD-relevant finding (Ito & Utsunomiya-Tate, 2025) identifies LACTB as a mammalian D-aspartyl endopeptidase capable of cleaving D-Asp-containing peptides, including an amyloid-β1–10-derived fragment containing D-Asp at position 7. This links LACTB to age-related protein damage and amyloid-relevant proteostasis, but also complicates therapeutic interpretation — complete LACTB inhibition could theoretically impair degradation of abnormal D-Asp-containing proteins.

Non-AD studies further reinforce mechanistic relevance through mitochondrial lipid metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, phospholipid remodeling, apoptosis, autophagy/mitophagy, ferroptosis, p53 signaling, NF-κB-linked inflammation, and respiratory/membrane organization. Overall, LACTB is best viewed as a plausible but context-dependent AD therapeutic target, with the strongest rationale favoring selective modulation or inhibition in myeloid/microglial compartments rather than systemic suppression.

Key Insights

Central AD Mechanism

The primary AD-specific pathway is: lower myeloid LACTB → higher succinylcarnitine → reduced AD risk. LACTB is identified as the principal enzyme hydrolyzing succinylcarnitine, making its activity a direct modulator of this protective metabolite axis.

Microglial Plaque Engagement

In vivo xenotransplantation experiments demonstrated that human LACTB-KO microglia showed enhanced association with amyloid plaques in amyloid-pathology mouse brains, accompanied by enhanced oxidative phosphorylation and altered lipid profiles. Evidence supports increased plaque engagement, though definitive improved clearance remains unconfirmed.

Proteostasis Complexity & Safety Risk

LACTB functions as a mammalian D-aspartyl endopeptidase, cleaving age-damaged D-Asp-containing proteins including an Aβ1–10-derived fragment. Complete inhibition could impair degradation of abnormal age-modified proteins, and systemic suppression risks disrupting cardiolipin remodeling, apoptosis, and autophagy/mitophagy across tissues.

Inflammatory Induction & NF-κB

LACTB expression is upregulated by IFN and TNF stimulation, suggesting chronic inflammatory signaling in AD brains could further induce LACTB in myeloid cells. Reduced LACTB may thus counteract or reshape pathological inflammatory microglial programs, with NF-κB and p53 signaling implicated.

Succinylcarnitine as Translational Biomarker

Succinylcarnitine is the clearest proposed translational biomarker for LACTB target engagement. Post-translational succinylation of LACTB may further regulate its proteolytic activity, mitochondrial respiration, and inflammatory signaling, offering an additional mechanistic regulatory layer to investigate.

Context-Dependent, Non-Linear Directionality

Myeloid genetics favor lower LACTB as protective, while mitochondrial proteostasis data support a role for LACTB in clearing damaged proteins. LACTB's effects depend critically on cell type, disease stage, substrate type, compartment, and degree of inhibition — precluding simple universal therapeutic conclusions. Human cortical proteomics further suggests relevance to dementia vulnerability rather than direct amyloid/tau pathology.

  2. Target selectively, not systemically: Develop selective myeloid/microglial LACTB modulation strategies; systemic inhibition risks disrupting mitochondrial lipid metabolism, cardiolipin remodeling, apoptosis regulation, autophagy/mitophagy, and D-Asp-protein degradation in non-target tissues.
  3. Validate succinylcarnitine as a biomarker: Use succinylcarnitine as a leading target-engagement biomarker, while rigorously determining whether changes in this metabolite correspond to beneficial microglial phenotypes and cognitive outcomes.
  4. Move beyond plaque association: Determine whether LACTB inhibition improves plaque handling beyond mere association — including assays of amyloid uptake, degradation, compaction, neuritic dystrophy, synapse preservation, and inflammatory state transitions.
  5. Clarify neuronal LACTB function: Determine whether LACTB activity in neurons is protective or harmful under AD stressors, including amyloid-β, tau, oxidative stress, iron loading, lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial injury, and impaired autophagy.
  6. Investigate post-translational succinylation: Examine how succinylation modulates LACTB proteolytic activity, mitochondrial respiration, and NF-κB inflammatory signaling, as this represents an understudied regulatory dimension with potential therapeutic relevance.

Evaluate the LACTB–PLA2G6–oxidized phospholipid axis: Given connections to mitochondrial lipid handling, ferroptosis, and neurologically relevant lipid metabolism, this pathway warrants targeted investigation in AD models — particularly given AD's iron dyshomeostasis and lipid peroxidation burden.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — LACTB as a Context-Dependent AD Therapeutic Target

LACTB represents a biologically plausible but not yet validated Alzheimer's disease therapeutic target. The strongest current evidence, anchored in genetic and metabolomic data, supports a myeloid LACTB–succinylcarnitine axis in which selective reduction of LACTB activity in microglial/myeloid compartments may raise succinylcarnitine levels and shift immune-metabolic states toward lower AD susceptibility. Supporting mechanistic evidence from LACTB-KO microglial models — including enhanced oxidative phosphorylation, altered lipid profiles, and increased amyloid plaque association in vivo — reinforces the rationale for this compartment-specific approach. However, LACTB's additional roles as a D-aspartyl endopeptidase involved in clearing age-damaged proteins, alongside its broad participation in mitochondrial lipid remodeling, cardiolipin metabolism, apoptosis, autophagy/mitophagy, ferroptosis, and inflammatory NF-κB signaling, create a complex, context-dependent therapeutic landscape. Complete or systemic inhibition carries meaningful risks. The most defensible near-term strategy is controlled, cell-type-selective modulation of LACTB in myeloid/microglial populations, paired with succinylcarnitine as a translational biomarker and rigorous stratification by cell type, disease stage, and inflammatory context.
