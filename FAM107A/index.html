FAM107A (also known as DRR1/TU3A/downregulated in renal cell carcinoma 1) is a stress-responsive, glucocorticoid/steroid-regulated, actin- and cytoskeleton-associated gene with documented roles in neural development, hippocampal structure and function, astrocyte maturation, neuronal survival, neurite/axon growth, synaptic biology, and tumor suppression. Its direct connection to Alzheimer's disease (AD) is limited: the clearest AD-specific evidence is its qRT-PCR validation as a differentially expressed mRNA within a hippocampal circRNA–miRNA–mRNA ceRNA network identified in cognitively impaired 5×FAD mice.

This positions Fam107a within an altered post-transcriptional regulatory network in an amyloid-driven familial AD mouse model, but not as a proven causal AD gene. The AD-associated network was enriched for pathways central to disease mechanisms: axon guidance, MAPK signaling, neurotrophin signaling, neuron projection development, cell morphogenesis, axons, postsynapses, and neuronal bodies.

Across broader non-AD studies, FAM107A is repeatedly linked to actin dynamics, cytoskeletal regulation, stress-hormone responses, synaptic and neuritic compartments, neuronal survival, astrocyte maturation, NF-κB/AKT-related signaling, and epigenetic or miRNA-mediated regulation — mechanisms that overlap strongly with AD-relevant biology, including synaptic failure, neuritic degeneration, hippocampal vulnerability, neuroinflammation, and glial dysfunction.

Key Insights

Direct AD Evidence — Limited but Validated

Fam107a was one of 13 mRNAs validated by qRT-PCR in the hippocampus of 5×FAD mice within a ceRNA network involving circRNA04655, circRNA00723, circRNA01891, miR-3470b, and miR-6240. This positions it in an amyloid-driven, altered post-transcriptional network — but not as a proven causal AD gene.

Actin / Cytoskeletal Mechanism

FAM107A/DRR1 increases F-actin content, affects actin nucleation and barbed-end capping, and reduces filament treadmilling. Because dendritic spine remodeling and synaptic maintenance depend critically on actin dynamics, FAM107A dysregulation could plausibly influence AD synaptic vulnerability.

Hippocampal Functional Relevance

Fam107a is regulated by mineralocorticoid receptor signaling in forebrain glutamatergic neurons, and hippocampal overexpression rescued an anxiety phenotype in MR-deletion mice. This directly supports a role in hippocampal neural structure and function, even outside an AD context.

Stress- and Steroid-Responsive Expression

Corticosterone, dexamethasone, androgen signaling, and glucocorticoid receptor activation all alter FAM107A/Fam107a expression. This is AD-relevant because chronic stress and glucocorticoid dysregulation are established contributors to hippocampal vulnerability, neurodegeneration, and cognitive decline.

Astrocyte & Glial Maturation

FAM107A was identified among targets associated with astrocyte maturation in neuron/endothelial co-culture systems. Given that AD involves astrocyte reactivity, synaptic dysfunction, and neurovascular changes, FAM107A may be relevant to glial maturation and neuron–glia–vascular interactions — though its specific role in AD astrocytes remains unknown.

Context-Dependent Safety Concern

FAM107A can be associated with pro-apoptotic effects in cancer cells and invasion-related behavior in glioblastoma contexts. Cancer-derived tumor-suppressive roles cannot be assumed to translate directly to AD neurons or glia. Therapeutic direction (activation vs. inhibition) is unresolved and requires disease-stage- and cell-type-specific validation.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — FAM107A in Alzheimer's Disease

FAM107A is a biologically plausible but evidentially immature candidate in the context of Alzheimer's disease. Its strongest AD-specific evidence — qRT-PCR-validated membership in a hippocampal ceRNA network in 5×FAD mice — places it within an amyloid-relevant post-transcriptional regulatory landscape but does not establish causality. The gene's well-documented roles in actin dynamics, cytoskeletal remodeling, hippocampal circuit function, stress-hormone responsiveness, neural development, and glial maturation provide a mechanistically coherent rationale for AD relevance. However, the direction of change in AD, the cell-type specificity of any effect, and whether modulation of FAM107A would be beneficial or harmful in an AD context are all unresolved. Cancer literature adds mechanistic depth but also caution — context-dependent pro-apoptotic and invasion-related behaviors mean that neither activation nor inhibition can be assumed safe. FAM107A warrants prioritized characterization in human AD tissue and cell-type-resolved AD models, with a near-term focus on expression validation, directional clarification, and ceRNA network functional testing, before any therapeutic program is considered.
