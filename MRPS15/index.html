Key Insights

Mitochondrial Translation & Respiratory Function

In cardiac stress models, reduced Mrps15/Mrps21 impaired mitochondrial translation, depolarized membrane potential, reduced respiration, increased mitochondrial ROS, disrupted nuclear-encoded protein import, and weakened antioxidant defenses linked to Glutaredoxin-2. These defects closely mirror the mitochondrial lesions observed in Alzheimer's-vulnerable neurons.

ZHX1–MRPS15/MRPS21 Regulatory Axis

ZHX1 promotes Mrps15/Mrps21 transcription; its activation or overexpression protected against mitochondrial damage in hypertensive-heart-disease models. Silencing Mrps15/Mrps21 reduced this protection, suggesting MRPS15 mediates mitochondrial benefits downstream of ZHX1. This axis has not yet been studied in neurons or Alzheimer's models.

Precision Modulation Required — Translation Balance

In a LONP-1 deficiency model, suppressor mutations in mitochondrial ribosomal proteins including MRPS-15 restored mitochondrial function by apparently slowing translation and improving OXPHOS complex assembly. Excessive or poorly coordinated mitochondrial translation can be harmful; rebalancing — not simply boosting — translation may be the therapeutic goal.

Extra-Mitochondrial Role in Stress Translation

In stressed human cardiomyocytes, MRPS15 appeared in cytosolic polysomes, increased during ER stress, interacted with cytosolic ribosomal proteins, and promoted IRES-dependent translation of UPR-related mRNAs. This is directly relevant to Alzheimer's disease, where ER stress, UPR signaling, and stress-selective translation contribute to neuronal dysfunction.

Cancer Safety Concern

MRPS15 is associated in several cancers with mitochondrial biogenesis, ribosome biogenesis, OXPHOS, metabolic reprogramming, and survival/growth phenotypes. Broad systemic stimulation of MRPS15-linked pathways could promote unwanted cell survival or proliferation, necessitating brain-targeted and physiologically normalizing therapeutic strategies.

Cross-Disease Signature & Essentiality

MRPS15 appears repeatedly in mitochondrial dysfunction signatures across diseases including non-obstructive azoospermia and collagen VI-related dystrophies, reinforcing its role as a hub of mitochondrial disease biology. Broader MRP studies indicate these proteins are widely expressed, essential, and generally nonredundant, ruling out complete inhibition as a therapeutic approach.

Overall Assessment — MRPS15 in Alzheimer's Disease Research

MRPS15 is a core, nonredundant mitochondrial ribosomal protein whose functions in translation, OXPHOS, ROS control, protein import, and stress-adaptive cytosolic translation are mechanistically relevant to the mitochondrial failure that characterizes Alzheimer's disease. However, no direct Alzheimer's evidence exists in the supplied literature: all supporting data derive from cardiac, cancer, and model-organism contexts. The gene's promise lies in its position at the intersection of mitochondrial resilience pathways that are genuinely disrupted in neurodegeneration. Critically, modulation strategy matters profoundly: the LONP-1 suppressor data warn against naive upregulation, and cancer-association data mandate safety-first, brain-targeted approaches. The ZHX1–MRPS15 axis and cytosolic UPR-translation role offer two mechanistically distinct but complementary research directions. MRPS15 warrants structured investigation as a mitochondrial resilience node in AD-vulnerable cells, with priority given to brain-specific expression profiling, functional validation in neuronal stress models, and rigorous safety evaluation before any therapeutic development is contemplated.
