Overview

⚠ Therapeutic Caution: Broad, chronic CTSS inhibition carries risk of impairing beneficial lysosomal clearance, lipid-repair programs, myelin maintenance, and normal immune surveillance. Selective, timed, compartment-aware modulation is recommended over complete inhibition.

CTSS (cathepsin S) is a lysosomal cysteine protease repeatedly linked to Alzheimer's disease-relevant mechanisms, including neuroinflammation, microglial and macrophage activation, antigen presentation, lysosomal function, cytokine signaling, extracellular matrix and barrier remodeling, lipid handling, oxidative stress, apoptosis, and aging-associated immune dysfunction.

Evidence spans direct and indirect AD relevance. Direct findings include elevated CTSS in AD brain regions and hippocampal neurons, association with cognitive decline and AD biomarkers, involvement in APP/PS1 and 5xFAD mouse studies, and increased CatS signal in 5xFAD brain sections using a PET radiotracer scaffold. Indirect evidence from CNS injury, aging, autoimmune, vascular, metabolic, and inflammatory models positions CTSS as a druggable immune-protease hub.

Overall, CTSS is a plausible but context-dependent AD therapeutic target. It may contribute to harmful neuroinflammation and neuronal injury, but may also support beneficial immune surveillance, antigen processing, lysosomal proteostasis, lipid trafficking, debris clearance, and repair. Broad chronic inhibition could therefore be risky.

Key Insights

AD Biomarker & Cognitive Association

Higher baseline plasma CTSS in older adults with memory complaints predicted longitudinal cognitive decline and correlated with worsening MMSE, increased neurofilament light, and increased phosphorylated tau 181 — supporting CTSS as a blood biomarker of AD-related progression.

Neuroinflammation in AD Models

In APP/PS1 mice, neuronal CTSS increased with aging, correlated negatively with recognition ability, promoted CX3CL1–CX3CR1 and JAK2–STAT3 signaling, and drove M1-like microglial activation. The selective inhibitor LY3000328 rescued AD-related pathological features in these mice.

Amyloid-Associated Microglial Program

In 5xFAD mice, interventions improving cognition and reducing Aβ deposition also reduced microgliosis-associated genes including Ctss, Trem2, Spi1, Fcgr1a, Csf1r, CD68, Ccl6, and IL-1β, placing CTSS within a disease-associated microglial/myeloid activation module.

Barrier Integrity & Aging

Macrophage-derived CTSS, upregulated by cellular senescence in aged choroid plexus, cleaved claudin-1 and impaired the blood–CSF barrier. CTSS inhibition or CLDN1 restoration rejuvenated barrier function and improved brain function in aged animals — directly relevant to AD-associated barrier dysfunction.

Beneficial Lipid & Myelin Biology

In a brain injury model, CTSS-high lipid-associated macrophages supported ApoE, ABCA1, ABCG1, Npc1/Npc2, LXR-associated lipid efflux, myelin lipid transfer to OPCs, OPC differentiation, and remyelination. CTSS inhibition impaired these reparative programs — critical given ApoE and cholesterol trafficking's centrality to AD.

Druggability — Tools & Caveats

Inhibitors include LY3000328, RO5459072/RO5461111, ASP1617, RJW-58, activity-based probes, and the PET scaffold [18F]LY3000328. Challenges include compensatory cathepsin induction (CTSB, CTSL, CTSD, CTSK), rebound activity, poor BBB penetration for some imaging tools, and risk of impairing repair functions.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Validate, don't assume: Prioritize CTSS as an AD-relevant neuroimmune target for rigorous validation, not as an already-proven AD therapy.
  2. Measure CTSS comprehensively: Assess CTSS expression and activity in hippocampus, cortex, choroid plexus, CSF, plasma, extracellular vesicles, microglia, border-associated macrophages, and infiltrating myeloid cells.
  3. Stratify rigorously: Stratify analyses by disease stage, APOE genotype, amyloid/tau burden, vascular pathology, white matter injury, cognitive decline rate, and inflammatory biomarker profile.
  4. Distinguish harm from benefit: Track harmful readouts (MHC-II/CD74, IL-1β/TNF-α/IL-6, NF-κB/p38/JAK–STAT, CX3CL1–CX3CR1, apoptosis, barrier disruption) separately from beneficial readouts (lysosomal proteostasis, debris clearance, ApoE/ABCA1/ABCG1 lipid efflux, Npc1/Npc2 cholesterol trafficking, myelin repair, OPC differentiation, immune surveillance).
  5. Use broad outcome panels in AD models: Evaluate inhibitors against amyloid-β burden, tau pathology, synaptic integrity, neuronal loss, microglial states, antigen presentation, lysosomal flux, cholesterol trafficking, myelin integrity, blood–brain and blood–CSF barrier function, cognition, and compensatory cathepsin changes.
  6. Develop CNS-appropriate tools: [18F]LY3000328 shows AD-model CatS binding potential but needs improved BBB penetration. Optimize activity-based probes and selective inhibitors for brain exposure, cell-type specificity, and activity-state measurement rather than total CTSS abundance alone.
  7. Avoid complete inhibition: The most rational therapeutic concept is selective, timed, and compartment-aware modulation of pathological CTSS activity — especially in CTSS-high inflammatory microglial/macrophage or choroid-plexus barrier-disruptive states — while preserving lysosomal clearance, lipid repair, and normal immune functions.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — CTSS/cathepsin S is a plausible, biologically compelling, but context-dependent AD therapeutic target. Direct evidence — elevated CTSS in AD brain and hippocampal neurons, correlation with cognitive decline and AD biomarkers, rescue of pathological features in APP/PS1 mice with LY3000328, and PET signal in 5xFAD sections — justifies continued prioritization. However, the same protease that drives harmful neuroinflammation, MHC-II antigen presentation, barrier disruption, and apoptosis also sustains beneficial lysosomal proteostasis, ApoE-mediated lipid efflux, Npc1/2 cholesterol trafficking, OPC differentiation, and myelin repair. Broad, chronic CTSS inhibition risks undermining these reparative functions while potentially triggering compensatory induction of related cathepsins. The field's most rational path is selective, timed, compartment-aware modulation of pathological CTSS activity — targeting CTSS-high inflammatory microglial/macrophage or choroid-plexus states — paired with careful monitoring of compensatory biology and preservation of beneficial functions.
