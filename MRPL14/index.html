Key Insights

Core Mitochondrial Function

MRPL14's strongest evidence is mitochondrial, not Alzheimer's-specific. It functions within the mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit and is essential for mitochondrial translation and oxidative phosphorylation complex assembly. Depletion disrupts monosome formation and impairs translation of most mtDNA-encoded polypeptides.

Safety Concern: Essential Gene

Developmental expression studies indicate mitochondrial ribosomal proteins including MRPL14 are broadly expressed and likely essential. Direct systemic inhibition would probably be unsafe, with high-energy tissues (neurons, cardiac, skeletal muscle) particularly vulnerable to loss-of-function toxicity.

Cancer & ROS Evidence

In thyroid cancer, MRPL14 knockdown reduced the Complex IV marker MTCO1 and increased ROS; ROS scavenging rescued impaired proliferation and migration. This supports a role in mitochondrial redox balance but is not directly transferable to Alzheimer's without disease-specific validation.

Vascular & Multi-disease Signatures

A Mendelian randomization study in aortic aneurysm identified MRPL14 among mitochondrial proteins associated with vascular disease, with downregulation in aneurysm tissue. This may connect indirectly to Alzheimer's via vascular dysfunction, inflammation, and cerebral hypoperfusion risk.

Splicing & RNA Processing

MRPL14 showed altered splice-site usage after SRPK1 inhibition in cervical cancer cells, suggesting isoform regulation by RNA-processing pathways. This is potentially relevant because Alzheimer's involves splicing abnormalities, but no Alzheimer's-specific MRPL14 splicing data currently exist.

Indirect AD Relevance Only

No cited studies directly test MRPL14 in Alzheimer's models, patient brain tissue, amyloid-β biology, tau pathology, synaptic degeneration, or cognitive decline. MRPL14 is best viewed as a plausible mitochondrial contributor to AD-relevant stress pathways — not a proven causal driver of Alzheimer's disease.

Overall Assessment — MRPL14 in Alzheimer's Disease

MRPL14 is a nuclear-encoded mitochondrial ribosomal protein whose primary evidence base is in mitoribosome biogenesis and oxidative phosphorylation, not Alzheimer's disease. Its relevance to neurodegeneration is mechanistically plausible but entirely indirect: disruption of MRPL14-dependent mitochondrial translation could impair respiratory chain assembly, deplete ATP, elevate ROS, and amplify mitochondrial stress — all processes central to Alzheimer's pathophysiology.
