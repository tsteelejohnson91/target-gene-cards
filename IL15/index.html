IL-15 & Alzheimer's Disease

Target Enabling Package — AI-Assisted Evidence Summary

Overview

Safety Flag — Oncology and cell-therapy data show IL-15 agonists, superagonists, and IL-15/IL-15Rα complexes can drive hyperproliferation, systemic cytokine elevation, inflammatory toxicity, hepatotoxicity, weight loss, and organ damage. Broad IL-15 stimulation in AD carries a plausible risk of worsening chronic CNS inflammation via NK/CD8 cytotoxic activation.

The available evidence on IL-15 in Alzheimer's disease (AD) is mixed and mostly indirect. One AD-specific study reports elevated IL-15 in AD cerebrospinal fluid, increased astrocytic IL-15 colocalization near amyloid-β plaques in human AD cortex, and elevated hippocampal IL-15 in 5xFAD mice. Across the broader literature, IL-15 is a potent immunoregulatory cytokine shaping cytotoxic lymphocyte survival, proliferation, persistence, tissue residence, and activation — especially in NK cells, CD8⁺ T cells, tissue-resident memory T cells, and engineered immune effector cells.

In AD-relevant mechanistic terms, IL-15 sits at the intersection of astrocyte-mediated inflammation, cytokine amplification, neuronal receptor signaling, cytotoxic immune activation, tissue-resident immune niches, JAK/STAT signaling, NF-κB/p38 MAPK inflammatory pathways, systemic inflammaging, and neurovascular/metabolic inflammation. Its role is context-dependent: IL-15 could plausibly contribute to harmful chronic neuroinflammation or, alternatively, support protective immune surveillance and tissue repair.

The strongest AD-specific rationale positions IL-15 as a biomarker and mechanistic node in plaque-associated astrocyte-driven neuroinflammation. IL-15 inhibition may be worth investigating if IL-15 sustains harmful inflammatory circuits; IL-15 agonism is more speculative and potentially hazardous unless highly targeted.

Key Insights

Direct AD Evidence

One study found IL-15 elevated in AD CSF, increased GFAP+ astrocytic IL-15 colocalization near amyloid-β plaques in human frontal/prefrontal cortex, and elevated hippocampal IL-15 in 6–7-month-old 5xFAD mice — establishing a plausible, if limited, AD-specific signal.

Astrocyte–Neuron Axis

Astrocytes (but not neurons) upregulate IL-15 following IFN-γ and TNF-α exposure in vitro, supporting a model in which the plaque inflammatory microenvironment drives astrocytic IL-15 production. Neuronal IL2Rβ/IL2Rγ expression increases with Braak score, suggesting growing neuronal responsiveness to IL-15 as tau pathology advances.

Safety: Cytotoxic Amplification

Across oncology and cell-therapy studies, IL-15 agonists, superagonists, and IL-15/IL-15Rα complexes strongly amplify NK/CD8 cytotoxicity, IFN-γ, granzyme/perforin, and tissue infiltration. Constitutive or excessive IL-15 signaling has caused hyperproliferation, cytokine toxicity, hepatotoxicity, weight loss, and organ damage — a critical concern for any therapeutic use in AD.

CNS Neuroinflammation Links

Anti-IL-15 treatment in an SIV primate model reduced neuroinflammation, brain-infiltrating T cells, and microglial IL-6. A neuropathic pain model showed IL-15/IL-15Rα upregulation in neurons and microglia with downstream NF-κB/p38 MAPK activation, driving TNF-α, IL-1β, CXCL11, and complement C4a — directly relevant to AD neuroinflammatory mechanisms.

Potential Protective Roles

IL-15 consistently enhances NK-cell and CD8⁺ T-cell survival, expansion, tissue residence, and cytotoxicity. In AD, this could theoretically support immune surveillance, clearance of pathological material, or tissue repair — though no direct evidence for beneficial CNS clearance by IL-15 in AD has been established.

Genetic & Systemic Context

A genome-wide significant telomere-length interaction signal between IL15 and INPP4B was reported in a European ancestry AD genetic study, hinting at immune-aging involvement. IL-15 also functions as a myokine in exercise/metabolic signaling and inflammaging, but these links do not yet connect IL-15 to amyloid or tau modification.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Treat IL-15 as an exploratory AD target. Current evidence supports further mechanistic investigation — particularly around plaque-associated astrocyte signaling and neuronal IL-15 receptor expression — but does not justify clinical IL-15 agonism or blockade for AD yet.
  2. Prioritize AD-specific validation. Measure IL-15, IL-15Rα, IL2Rβ/CD122, and IL2Rγ in CSF, plasma, postmortem brain, single-cell datasets, microglia, astrocytes, neurons, endothelial cells, border-associated macrophages, meninges, and choroid plexus.
  3. Correlate IL-15 with AD pathology markers. Test whether IL-15 correlates with amyloid burden, tau Braak stage, plaque-associated astrocytosis, microglial activation, synaptic loss, neurofilament light chain, cognitive decline, APOE genotype, telomere length, vascular disease, and systemic inflammatory status.
  4. Determine whether astrocyte-derived IL-15 is harmful or compensatory. Key experiments should test whether blocking IL-15 or IL-15 receptor signaling reduces inflammatory signaling, synaptic dysfunction, tau pathology, amyloid-associated gliosis, or cognitive impairment in models such as 5xFAD.
  5. Evaluate IL-15 blockade first in inflammatory AD subtypes. The strongest inhibition rationale applies to an AD subgroup with elevated IL-15 plus IFN-γ/TNF-α signatures, cytotoxic CD8/NK activity, microglial IL-6, JAK/STAT activation, complement activation, or tissue-resident memory-like immune niches.
  6. Approach IL-15 agonism with caution. While IL-15 enhancement may theoretically improve immune surveillance or clearance, broad stimulation risks worsening neuroinflammation through NK/CD8 activation, IFN-γ, granzyme/perforin, TNF-α, and inflammatory tissue infiltration.
  7. Favor targeted over systemic IL-15 modulation. Potential strategies include CNS- or cell-targeted delivery, receptor-biased ligands, local modulation of astrocytic IL-15 induction, downstream pathway modulation, or carefully titrated blockade — not broad chronic systemic dosing.
  8. Include a comprehensive companion biomarker panel covering IL-15, IL-15Rα, STAT3/STAT5 activation, JAK1/3 activity, IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, CXCL10/11, CCL5, granzyme B, perforin, CD8⁺ TRM markers, NK-cell activation markers, complement markers (C4a/C1q/C3), and neurodegeneration markers (NfL) in all IL-15-focused AD studies.
  9. Stratify studies by context-dependent variables: age, sex, APOE status, telomere length, vascular/metabolic disease, periodontal/gut inflammatory burden, microbiome profiles, exercise status, and systemic immune activation — as IL-15 biology is highly context-dependent.
  10. The most defensible current conclusion: IL-15 is a plausible mediator or amplifier of AD-relevant neuroinflammation, especially through astrocyte plaque-associated signaling and immune-cell activation. Blocking IL-15 may be appropriate if it sustains damaging neuroimmune activation; enhancing IL-15 would require strong evidence of protective effects and tight safety controls.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — IL-15 as an Exploratory AD Neuroimmune Node

IL-15 occupies a biologically plausible but therapeutically unresolved position in Alzheimer's disease. The most direct AD evidence — elevated CSF IL-15, increased astrocytic IL-15 at amyloid-β plaques, upregulated hippocampal IL-15 in 5xFAD mice, and progressive neuronal IL-15 receptor expression with Braak stage — collectively position IL-15 as a candidate mechanistic amplifier of plaque-associated neuroinflammation rather than a bystander marker. Broader literature robustly documents IL-15's capacity to drive cytotoxic lymphocyte programs, NF-κB/p38 MAPK inflammatory signaling, JAK/STAT activation, and tissue-resident immune niche formation — pathways with clear neuroinflammatory relevance. However, the therapeutic direction remains genuinely uncertain: IL-15 inhibition is the more cautious and mechanistically better-supported intervention hypothesis, applicable if IL-15 sustains damaging astrocyte–neuron–immune circuits, while IL-15 agonism carries significant safety liabilities from cytotoxic immune amplification. Rigorous AD-specific validation — spanning CSF/plasma biomarkers, single-cell brain profiling, animal model interventions, and biomarker-stratified patient substudies — is required before IL-15 modulation can be responsibly advanced toward clinical investigation.
