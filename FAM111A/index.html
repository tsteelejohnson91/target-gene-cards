Key Insights

Genome Maintenance — Strongest AD-Relevant Theme

FAM111A is linked to DNA replication, DNA-protein crosslink repair (topoisomerase- and PARP1-associated complexes), replication fork protection, and genome stability. These processes parallel AD-relevant neuronal aging biology — including oxidative genomic injury, impaired repair, and transcriptional stress — but no direct neuronal evidence exists.

No Direct Alzheimer's Disease Evidence

The supplied literature contains no data showing altered FAM111A expression, mutation, activity, localization, or function in Alzheimer's brain tissue or any AD experimental model. All AD relevance is strictly inferential, based on shared pathway biology.

Dosage-Sensitive — Narrow Therapeutic Window

Gain-of-function or hyperactive FAM111A variants cause Kenny-Caffey syndrome type 2 and osteocraniostenosis, while deficient activity may also be pathogenic. Simple broad inhibition or activation carries significant risk of off-target harm.

Protease Hyperactivity Is Cytotoxic

Excessive FAM111A protease activity can suppress replication and transcription, disrupt chromatin-associated factors, impair nuclear pore/nuclear envelope integrity, and trigger cell death-like phenotypes — mechanisms conceptually overlapping with neurodegenerative stress but unproven in AD.

Antiviral & Innate Immune Roles — Hypothesis Only

FAM111A restricts certain viruses and is antagonized by viral SPI-1. Since innate immune and antiviral-like responses are discussed in AD biology, this connection is hypothesis-generating. No evidence links FAM111A to AD neuroinflammation, interferon signatures, or microglial activation.

Proliferative-Cell Context Limits Neuronal Relevance

Much of FAM111A biology centers on replication forks and proliferative-cell genome maintenance. Because mature neurons are largely post-mitotic, AD relevance would require evidence in neurons, glia, vascular cells, neural progenitors, or aberrant neuronal cell-cycle re-entry — none of which is currently supplied.
