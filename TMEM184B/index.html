Key Insights

Endolysosomal–V-ATPase Axis

TMEM184B localises to endosomal compartments and interacts with V-ATPase. Its loss reduces V0/V1 assembly, lowers endolysosomal pH, and disrupts endosomal flux — directly impinging on APP processing, Aβ handling, and autophagic clearance, all central to AD pathobiology.

Synaptic Gene Network Disruption

Tmem184b-mutant mice show altered hippocampal expression of pre- and post-synaptic genes governing synapse assembly and neural development. Synaptic loss is the strongest correlate of cognitive decline in AD, making this transcriptomic signature mechanistically relevant.

Dual Role — A Critical Caveat

TMEM184B has an inherently dual functional profile: loss delays peripheral axon degeneration after acute injury, yet simultaneously causes progressive synaptic abnormalities, sensorimotor deficits, impaired endolysosomal function, and neuronal degeneration-like phenotypes. Any therapeutic strategy must resolve this dichotomy before advancing.

No Direct AD Causality

Young adult Tmem184b-mutant mice exhibited normal object discrimination, and there are no direct amyloid/tau experiment data. TMEM184B has not been established as an AD-causative gene or validated therapeutic target; its AD relevance is entirely mechanistic and indirect.

Broad Neurobiological Footprint

The evidence base supports rigorous target-validation studies as the clear next step, not prioritization for drug development.
