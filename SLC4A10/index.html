Overview

⚠ Evidence Caveat: The supplied literature provides little to no direct evidence that SLC4A10 causes Alzheimer's disease, modifies amyloid-β or tau pathology, or improves outcomes in Alzheimer's models. Its relevance is indirect and hypothesis-generating only.

SLC4A10 (also known as NCBE/NBCn2) is a brain-enriched sodium-dependent bicarbonate transporter involved in intracellular pH regulation, chloride/bicarbonate handling, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) physiology, choroid plexus epithelial transport, neuronal excitability, inhibitory synaptic transmission, and immune-cell phenotypes.

The strongest Alzheimer's-relevant mechanism runs through the choroid plexus–CSF axis. SLC4A10 is localized to choroid plexus epithelial cells and supports Na⁺/HCO₃⁻-dependent transport required for CSF secretion and intraventricular fluid homeostasis. Reduced CSF production and turnover are proposed contributors to impaired amyloid-β clearance in late-onset Alzheimer's disease. A choroid plexus knockout study reports that Slc4a10 disruption shifts choroid plexus cells from a high-rate secretory phenotype toward a more dormant state, similar to changes observed during aging or Alzheimer's disease.

SLC4A10 also has plausible Alzheimer's relevance through neuronal mechanisms. It is expressed in cortex, hippocampus, principal neurons, interneurons, and inhibitory presynapses. Loss of Slc4a10 impairs intracellular pH recovery, alters neuronal excitability, and compromises GABA release while preserving glutamate release — overlapping with Alzheimer's disease mechanisms involving synaptic dysfunction, hippocampal hyperexcitability, and inhibitory interneuron dysfunction.

Additional indirect links include vascular and immune biology. SLC4A10 marks CD8⁺ T-cell and MAIT-cell subsets in inflammatory, vascular, infectious, and cancer contexts. In carotid atherosclerosis, SLC4A10⁺ CD8⁺ T cells are enriched in unstable plaques and associated with cerebrovascular events, while experimental SLC4A10 overexpression in mice reduces cytotoxic inflammatory mediators and stabilizes plaques.

Key Insights

CNS-Relevant Expression Profile

SLC4A10 is expressed throughout mammalian brain — hippocampus, cortex, principal neurons, inhibitory presynapses, and choroid plexus epithelium — qualifying it as a genuine CNS transporter rather than a peripheral incidental gene.

Minimal Direct Alzheimer's Evidence

The supplied literature does not demonstrate that SLC4A10 expression, variants, or activity are altered in Alzheimer's patients, nor that SLC4A10 modulation changes amyloid, tau, neurodegeneration, cognition, or disease progression.

Strongest Hypothesis: CSF & Aβ Clearance

Late-onset Alzheimer's disease is framed as involving impaired Aβ clearance rather than overproduction. SLC4A10-dependent choroid plexus transport supports CSF secretion, and Slc4a10 knockout shifts epithelial cells toward an aging/Alzheimer's-like dormant phenotype with reduced secretory capacity.

Broad Choroid Plexus Remodelling

Slc4a10 knockout reduces aquaporin-1, Na,K-ATPase abundance, claudin-2, and alters spectrin/cytoskeletal organization — indicating SLC4A10 supports broader epithelial transport architecture beyond simple bicarbonate movement.

Therapeutic Direction is Conflicted

In hydrocephalus models, reducing SLC4A10 lowers CSF production and improves outcomes; however, an Alzheimer's clearance hypothesis would more likely favor preserving or enhancing choroid plexus function — making simple inhibition potentially counterproductive.

Substantial Safety Liabilities

Human biallelic loss-of-function variants are associated with neurodevelopmental impairment, hypotonia, intellectual disability, autistic-spectrum traits, microcephaly, abnormal ventricles, epilepsy, and cognitive impairment. Mouse loss causes altered ventricles, behavioral abnormalities, and impaired GABA release.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — SLC4A10 & Alzheimer's Disease

SLC4A10 is a biologically credible but evidentially immature Alzheimer's candidate. Its strongest claim to relevance rests on the choroid plexus–CSF axis: the transporter is required for high-rate epithelial secretion, and its loss phenocopies the aging- and Alzheimer's-like dormant choroid plexus state proposed to impair Aβ clearance. Neuronal roles in intracellular pH regulation, GABAergic transmission, and hippocampal excitability provide a secondary mechanistic rationale overlapping with synaptic and network dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease. Immune and vascular observations add a third, more speculative layer. However, no direct evidence links SLC4A10 to Alzheimer's pathology, biomarkers, or patient outcomes. The safety profile of SLC4A10 loss — neurodevelopmental impairment, epilepsy, cognitive deficits, and CSF dysregulation in humans and mice — imposes high caution on any inhibitory therapeutic strategy. The appropriate next step is rigorous profiling of SLC4A10 status in Alzheimer's tissue and model systems before committing resources to drug discovery.
