Overview

⚠ Evidence Caveat

CDH23 is not a validated Alzheimer's disease mechanism gene or therapeutic target. Its presence in some AD-relevant genetic/epigenetic datasets is suggestive but falls far below the standard required for therapeutic prioritization. All AD-linked findings should be treated as hypothesis-generating only.

CDH23 encodes Cadherin-23, a calcium-dependent cell-adhesion protein with its primary, best-characterized role in the inner ear. It constitutes the upper strand of stereocilia tip-link structures, partners with PCDH15, and is anchored through a macromolecular complex including harmonin, MYO7A, and SANS. Loss-of-function mutations underlie hereditary hearing loss and Usher syndrome type 1D, which combines profound congenital deafness, vestibular dysfunction, and progressive retinal degeneration. CDH23 variants also modify age-related and noise-induced hearing loss.

In the Alzheimer's disease domain, the available evidence is limited and heterogeneous. CDH23 has appeared among loci showing altered DNA methylation and RNA expression in AD brain (including replicated methylation signals near ANK1, RPL13, and RHBDF2), and as a suggestive — but sub-genome-wide-significant — locus in a CSF BACE activity GWAS. There is no direct evidence that CDH23 regulates amyloid-β, tau, neuroinflammation, or synaptic degeneration.

Therapeutically, the strongest case is for CDH23-associated sensory disease: gene correction and editing of Cdh23 alleles, large-gene AAV delivery strategies, base and RNA editing concepts for Usher syndrome, chemical chaperones, ER-stress inhibitors, and otoprotective agents are among the approaches described. An indirect AD-relevance angle exists because CDH23-related hearing loss may confound cognitive testing and could contribute to dementia risk through sensory deprivation — but the supplied text does not establish that CDH23-mediated hearing loss causes Alzheimer's disease.

Key Insights

AD Evidence is Weak & Pre-Causal

CDH23 does not directly regulate amyloid-β generation or clearance, tau phosphorylation or aggregation, AD neuroinflammation, microglial activation, central synaptic loss, blood–brain barrier dysfunction, or cognitive decline. No therapeutic-grade AD evidence exists in the supplied text.

Strongest Mechanism: Sensory Mechanotransduction

CDH23 forms calcium-dependent tip-link structures with PCDH15 in hair-cell stereocilia, anchored by harmonin, MYO7A, and SANS. This complex enables mechanoelectrical transduction; mutations disrupt hearing, balance, and retinal function — not cortical or hippocampal circuitry.

Therapeutic Target for Sensory Disorders

Reported therapeutic approaches — gene correction and editing of Cdh23 alleles, large-gene AAV strategies, base/RNA editing for Usher syndrome, chemical chaperones, ER-stress inhibitors, and otoprotective interventions — are well-grounded for hearing loss, Usher syndrome type 1D, vestibular disease, and retinal degeneration.

AD-Relevant Association Signals: Suggestive Only

CDH23 appears among loci with altered DNA methylation and RNA expression in AD brain (co-reported with ANK1, RPL13, RHBDF2 methylation signals) and as a sub-genome-wide-significant locus in a CSF BACE activity GWAS. These are hypothesis-generating, not causal.

Research Confounder in AD Mouse Models

The Cdh23ahl variant in C57BL/6-derived backgrounds drives progressive high-frequency hearing loss, which can confound auditory behavior, startle responses, social behavior, and sensory-dependent cognitive assays used in aging and AD models. Genotype should be documented and controlled.

Indirect Dementia-Risk Framework

If CDH23-related hearing loss is confirmed to contribute to dementia risk via sensory deprivation or cognitive load, the most plausible AD-adjacent intervention is early hearing-loss detection and treatment — not direct CDH23 targeting for amyloid/tau disease modification.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — CDH23 Evidence Synthesis

CDH23 is a well-characterized cadherin of the inner ear whose primary, mechanistically rich role is in stereocilia tip-link assembly, mechanoelectrical transduction, and sensory-cell survival. Its disease relevance is strongest for hereditary hearing loss, Usher syndrome type 1D, vestibular dysfunction, and retinal degeneration — disorders for which gene-based and otoprotective therapeutic strategies are actively being developed. In the Alzheimer's disease context, CDH23 currently occupies a much weaker position: epigenetic and expression signals in AD brain and a sub-genome-wide-significant BACE GWAS signal are intriguing but not causal, and no evidence links CDH23 to the canonical AD pathological cascade (amyloid-β, tau, neuroinflammation, synaptic loss). The most scientifically defensible AD-adjacent angle is an indirect one — Cdh23-related hearing impairment may confound cognitive testing in mouse models and possibly contributes to dementia risk in humans through sensory deprivation, though causality is unproven. Researchers should invest in CDH23 as a validated sensory-disease target while treating its AD connections as strictly exploratory, requiring substantial functional validation before any therapeutic consideration in the neurodegeneration space.
