Key Insights

No Direct AD Mechanism Demonstrated

The supplied literature does not show FLNC regulating amyloid-β production, tau phosphorylation or aggregation, synaptic degeneration, microglial activation, or cognitive outcomes. AD involvement remains entirely inferential from shared pathway biology.

Cytoskeletal & Mechanosensitive Scaffold

FLNC is primarily described as an actin-binding scaffold central to Z-disc/sarcomere stability, cytoskeletal organization, cell adhesion, mechanotransduction, and structural repair — predominantly in heart and skeletal muscle.

Proteostasis: Strongest AD-Pathway Overlap

FLNC mutations in myofibrillar myopathy and cardiomyopathy cause misfolding, aggregation, impaired dimerization, lysosomal/protein accumulation, and activation or failure of protein quality-control — mechanisms strongly paralleling AD proteotoxicity, though shown only in muscle/cardiac contexts.

Autophagy & Chaperone Pathway Links

Multiple studies connect FLNC pathology with BAG3-mediated chaperone-assisted selective autophagy (CASA), HSPB7/HspB1/HSP70/HSPB8, LC3, p62/SQSTM1, NBR1, ubiquitinated aggregates, and proteasome/autophagy stress — all overlapping AD proteotoxicity biology.

Biomarker Signal in MCI/AD

A blood leukocyte methylation study in Chinese MCI/AD patients identified FLNC cg20186636 as a differentially methylated CpG. This constitutes hypothesis-generating biomarker evidence only — not causality or therapeutic validation. Replication in larger cohorts is required.

Major Safety Concerns Constrain Targeting

FLNC loss or mutation is repeatedly associated with severe cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, sudden cardiac death, myocardial fibrosis, skeletal myopathy, and respiratory weakness. Systemic FLNC inhibition would be high-risk without brain-specific rationale and cell-type-selective delivery.

Overall Assessment — FLNC in Alzheimer's Disease

FLNC is mechanistically adjacent to AD-relevant pathways but is not directly implicated in Alzheimer's disease by the supplied literature. Its core roles in cytoskeletal organization, proteostasis, autophagy, and mechanosensing overlap meaningfully with neurodegeneration biology, yet no evidence directly links FLNC to amyloid-β, tau, synaptic loss, microglia, or AD outcomes. The sole AD-adjacent signal — a blood methylation association at cg20186636 in MCI/AD patients — is intriguing but far from actionable without replication and mechanistic follow-through.
