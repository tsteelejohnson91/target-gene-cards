PRDX6 & Alzheimer's Disease

Target Gene Card — AI-Assisted Evidence Summary

Overview

⚠ Dual-Role Caution: PRDX6 is not uniformly protective or harmful in AD contexts. Its iPLA2 activity can drive NOX2 activation, neuroinflammation, vascular injury, and pro-apoptotic signaling under certain conditions, while its peroxidase activity is broadly neuroprotective. Indiscriminate activation may cause harm.

PRDX6 is a multifunctional redox- and lipid-metabolism enzyme repeatedly linked to Alzheimer's disease (AD) through oxidative stress control, phospholipid hydroperoxide repair, ferroptosis regulation, neuroinflammation, astrocyte and microglial responses, mitochondrial dysfunction, apoptosis, synaptic impairment, blood–brain barrier/vascular injury, senescence, and biomarker variation.

The strongest direct AD evidence positions PRDX6 as a context-dependent disease node rather than a uniformly protective or harmful protein. In AD models and human datasets, PRDX6 is reported as altered across multiple compartments: reduced in amyloid-β–injured hippocampal/neuronal models in some studies, upregulated in AD entorhinal cortex and astrocyte-enriched signatures in others, elevated in brain-derived extracellular vesicles alongside tau and phosphorylated tau, increased in male AD CSF, and present as a memory-associated plasma protein in clinically unimpaired older adults.

Mechanistically, PRDX6 carries three distinct enzymatic activities — glutathione-dependent peroxidase/phospholipid hydroperoxidase, acidic calcium-independent phospholipase A2 (iPLA2), and lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase — enabling ROS/lipid hydroperoxide detoxification, oxidized membrane repair, phospholipid remodeling, inflammatory lipid signaling, and interaction with the ferroptosis network (GPX4, GSH, SLC7A11, FTH1, selenium metabolism).

Key Insights

🔬 Amyloid-β Stress & m6A Regulation

In an Aβ1–42 oligomer neuronal model, METTL14 stabilised PRDX6 mRNA via m6A modification. PRDX6 then suppressed ROS–ASK1/p38 signalling and ferroptosis, and PRDX6 overexpression rescued neuronal injury caused by METTL14 knockdown, establishing a direct Aβ→epigenetic→PRDX6 protective axis.

🧠 Astrocyte-Mediated Plaque Clearance

In an AD mouse model, higher astrocytic PRDX6 promoted astrocyte recruitment to Aβ plaques, enhanced plaque penetration by astrocytic processes, activated periplaque microglial phagocytosis, reduced plaque seeding, lowered Aβ burden, and lessened neuritic degeneration. Haplodeficiency of PRDX6 had the opposite effect.

🛡️ Core Anti-Ferroptotic Network

Across AD and non-AD systems, PRDX6 enhancement reduces ROS, Fe²⁺, malondialdehyde, lipid peroxidation, GSH depletion, ACSL4 induction, and GPX4 impairment. PRDX6 also supports selenium utilisation and selenoprotein/GPX4 function, placing it within the core lipid-peroxide defence network relevant to AD neurodegeneration.

⚠️ Context-Dependent Pathogenicity

In vascular/cognitive impairment and AD-linked cognitive dysfunction models, elevated PRDX6 correlated with IL-1β and TNF-α, and PRDX6 silencing in SH-SY5Y cells improved synaptic protein expression while reducing inflammatory cytokines and apoptosis — the inverse of its role in amyloid-β neuronal models, underscoring its dual nature.

📊 Multi-Compartment Human Biomarker Evidence

PRDX6 is upregulated in AD entorhinal cortex, enriched in astrocyte-related oxidative-stress signatures, elevated in AD brain-derived extracellular vesicles alongside total tau and phosphorylated tau, increased specifically in male AD CSF (NULISAseq), and associated with memory performance in plasma proteomics of clinically unimpaired older adults.

⚙️ Mitochondrial & Synaptic Links

PRDX6 connects to mitochondrial membrane integrity, oxidative phosphorylation, PINK1/Parkin mitophagy, and mitochondrial fission. Prdx6 knockout mice show impaired spatial memory, altered hippocampal LTP, and ERK1/2 abnormalities. Aged animals with preserved memory maintain PRDX6 levels in dentate gyrus synaptosomal fractions near those of young controls.

Actionable Takeaways

  1. Prioritise cell-type-specific AD validation. Test PRDX6 expression, localisation, oxidation state, enzymatic activities, and secretion in neurons, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocytes, endothelial cells, and pericytes across AD stages, especially in entorhinal cortex and hippocampus.
  2. Separate PRDX6 activities experimentally. Distinguish protective peroxidase/phospholipid hydroperoxidase functions from potentially harmful iPLA2-driven lipid signalling using activity-specific mutants, MJ33-like iPLA2 inhibitors, and peroxidase-selective enhancement strategies.
  3. Evaluate PRDX6 in ferroptosis-focused AD models. Measure whether PRDX6 modulation changes Fe²⁺, lipid hydroperoxides, MDA/4-HNE, ACSL4, GPX4, GSH/GSSG, SLC7A11, FTH1, mitochondrial lipid peroxidation, neuronal survival, synaptic markers, and cognition after amyloid-β or tau stress.
  4. Explore upstream therapeutic axes. Candidate approaches include METTL14-mediated PRDX6 mRNA stabilisation, Nrf2/ARE activation, CREB1 transcriptional activation, selenium/GPX4 support, SESN2/SIRT5-linked K209 modification control, SP1-dependent induction, and interventions that prevent PRDX6 degradation or pathological oxidation.
  5. Consider activity-selective astrocyte-targeted modulation. Astrocytic PRDX6 can improve Aβ proteostasis by enhancing astrocyte plaque engagement and microglial phagocytic activation. However, astrocytic iPLA2 activity may also promote inflammatory or degenerative astrocyte states; astrocyte-directed therapies must therefore be activity-selective.
  6. Avoid indiscriminate systemic PRDX6 activation. PRDX6 can support tumour survival, ferroptosis resistance in cancer, senescent-cell survival, and maladaptive inflammatory signalling. AD strategies should emphasise CNS-targeted delivery, disease-stage selection, and activity-specific modulation.
  7. Investigate extracellular PRDX6 as a distinct therapeutic target. Intracellular PRDX6 may be antioxidant and anti-ferroptotic, while extracellular PRDX6 may act as a DAMP-like signal via RAGE, TLR4/MD2, or other inflammatory pathways. Therapeutic blockade of extracellular PRDX6 signalling may be useful if it drives neuroinflammation.
  8. Develop PRDX6 biomarkers only with strict preanalytical control. Validate separately in CSF, plasma, serum, and brain-derived extracellular vesicles; control for haemolysis and erythrocyte signal; stratify by sex; and compare against amyloid, tau, neurofilament, GFAP, inflammatory markers, and cognitive outcomes.
  9. Use combination endpoints in therapeutic studies. PRDX6-targeted interventions should be assessed against amyloid burden, tau phosphorylation/aggregation, neuritic dystrophy, synaptic proteins, LTP, spatial memory, neuroinflammation, NLRP3 activation, NOX2 activity, mitochondrial function, BBB integrity, and ferroptosis markers.
  10. Frame PRDX6 as a precision target, not a simple on/off switch. The best-supported therapeutic concept is to enhance PRDX6's antioxidant, lipid-peroxide-detoxifying, GPX4-supporting, and mitochondrial-protective functions while limiting iPLA2/NOX2/RAGE/TLR/NF-κB-linked inflammatory or pro-apoptotic signalling in contexts where PRDX6 becomes maladaptive.

Overall Assessment

Overall Assessment — PRDX6 in Alzheimer's Disease

PRDX6 represents a high-complexity, high-interest precision target for Alzheimer's disease rather than a simple drug target amenable to broad activation or inhibition. The evidence base spans human AD tissue, CSF, plasma, extracellular vesicle proteomics, genetic mouse models, and in vitro mechanistic studies, collectively demonstrating that PRDX6's three enzymatic activities can be either neuroprotective or pathogenic depending on cell type, brain region, disease stage, and molecular context. The most compelling AD-relevant functions are its anti-ferroptotic lipid hydroperoxide defence (supporting GPX4/GSH/selenium networks), astrocyte-mediated Aβ plaque clearance, mitochondrial quality control, and suppression of ROS-driven ASK1/p38/NF-κB signalling. Counterbalancing these, its iPLA2-driven NOX2 activation, RAGE/TLR inflammatory signalling, and SASP-linked effects represent genuine risks of maladaptive engagement. The optimal therapeutic strategy is activity-selective, CNS-targeted, and stage-aware modulation — enhancing peroxidase and membrane-repair functions in vulnerable neurons and astrocytes while pharmacologically limiting iPLA2-driven inflammatory cascades and extracellular DAMP signalling. Biomarker development is promising but requires rigorous preanalytical standardisation and sex-stratified analysis. PRDX6 merits continued investment as a mechanistically-anchored node in the oxidative stress, ferroptosis, and neuroinflammation axes of AD pathogenesis.
